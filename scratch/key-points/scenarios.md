# Scenarios

Exercises the reader runs in their own case. Each one is a recall, a question, and a place the answer is supposed to land.

This file holds scenarios and nothing else. **Part 1** is the full text of the ones written out at length (the first two are used in the long book's Preface and Chapter 1; the write-off is proposed). **Part 2** is the inventory of every check in the short book, with how each fared with cold readers. **Part 3** is which thesis each check supports, moved here verbatim from the general book's plan when that book was deleted.

# Part 1: Full text


## Pill-vs-enlightenment

Think of the "bad people" of the world—whoever's responsible for ruining things for the rest of us. Hypocritical CEOs. Greedy bankers. Corrupt politicians, and all who support them. Whoever it is for you.

They seem to lack something fundamental that the rest of us have, right? A moral compass, or the sense to listen to it.

Would some tiny part of you be disappointed to discover they're not *fundamentally* evil—that they're just badly confused but inherently redeemable, like the rest of us? Would that part feel robbed of the right to deliver the righteous vengeance they so richly deserve?

If you had to choose between delivering that vengeance and offering them a pill that would instantly enlighten them—which would you choose? Forget about the "right" answer. Is there a tiny flicker in there that's itching to mash that first button?

There is **nothing** more primally, viscerally satisfying than violently punishing evil.

---

I'd like to see them punished, and if I'm honest, *hurt*—quite independently of any good it might do. I'm not going to act on it, of course. That would be distasteful. But there's nothing wrong with feeling it. In fact, it's something all good people should harbor, to keep evil in check.

---

## Contempt and Integrity

Think back to a moment you felt contemptuously judged by another person. No matter how "right" they may have been; even if you *knew* you had done wrong, you still fought back, right? Something felt *off* about them. You could tell they weren't coming from a good place. How?

Now think of a time when someone set you straight with profound integrity—seemingly getting nothing out of it themselves. Even if it stung your ego; made you lose face, you had no desire to take revenge, did you? Why not? What could you tell they were serving?

Now turn it around.

Think of a time when *you* contemptuously judged someone. Even if you tried to hide your contempt, they still dug in their heels, didn't they? And somewhere inside, that resistance confirmed what you already believed.

And finally, a time when you corrected someone with integrity yourself. Perhaps you had to do it with more ferocity; more violence than in the previous case—and yet something in you knew it would ultimately bring *more* peace. *How* did you know?

---

That time you stood in integrity, and the time the person who corrected you did: even if you knew nothing else about them—their politics, their religion, their creed—didn't it feel like you were somehow serving the same thing? What is that?

And the time you stood in contempt, and were treated with contempt: didn't it feel like *those* were somehow serving the same thing? Not just your personal egos, either.

Integrity feels clean from both the inside and outside.

Contempt feels righteous inside but dark, dirty, insincere from the outside.

There's a reason for that distinction.

---

When we mock or shame our enemies, part of us already knows they're liable to dig in, right? And we know there's a way that *doesn't* cause this. So why do we still do it? Is it just because we can't help it?

Notice: their digging in brings them one step closer to a condition that can only be solved through violent punishment.

But this can't be right. We're not... trying to maneuver our enemies into a position where we'll be forced to unleash our righteous wrath, right?

Right??

---

## The write-off (proposed)

Think of the last time you said something with an edge in it to someone on the other side. A relative at dinner, or a reply under a politician's post. You knew what you were doing, mostly. And they did what they do: called you brainwashed, or deranged, or just laughed.

Now notice what that did to you. Did it sting? Or did something in you light up: *look at that, that's exactly what they're like.* It felt good. More than good. It felt *righteous.* You'd gotten them to show it.

Might that have been the point?

Now hold the other thing you saw in Chapter 1: that what was driving the stranger wasn't him. If you could really see that, right now, looking at this person, could you still want to shove them further in? Not *should* you. *Could* you.

You couldn't. It isn't a discipline. You can't want it while fully looking at them, the same way you couldn't give the stranger that look while fully looking at him.

So the wanting you just found is a reading. It measures how far you are from seeing this person. And that is exactly what you were reading in the first stranger, and the reason you could read it at all: you've been where he is. You're there now, a little.

*Notes.* Chapter 2, "From the delivering end," already runs this loop in the first person, from my side: the target is defiant, so my contempt was justified, so the world is even shittier than I thought. What's missing is the reader's own instance, the invitation to feel how righteous it feels, and the possession move. The Preface plants the flicker with the pill test and Chapter 5 says that part wants them to stay as they are, but nothing closes the loop from the reader's side. The logic of the last step isn't clean and doesn't need to be: it's the same inference Chapter 1 makes (you recognize it because you've been there), run from your own wanting back to his state. See spiral.md for why the circle isn't a problem.

## From the general book (proposed)

Checks that were written out only in the general book, verbatim, with where each came from.

**The shark** (plan.md). "Do you feel contempt for a shark? A tornado? A rabid dog? However much harm they do, the feeling doesn't come. It comes only for someone you take to know better." / "If contempt tracked how broken someone was, it would fall off at the far end, as it does for the shark. It rises." The essay's version: "You wouldn't bother saying that about a rock, or a shark. You say it about someone who could have known, and did." Both author-readers called it "the idea that would make the book matter".

**The bystander** (Chapter 1, Common knowledge). "When someone treats others with contempt in front of you, do you trust them more, or less? Would you tell them something you were ashamed of?" The only check run from the bystander's seat.

**Counted on** (Chapter 1, Common knowledge). "In the opening, when I asked you for a memory of doing wrong, I was counting on one thing: that you couldn't have done whatever you did while looking squarely at what you knew." / "Does it surprise you that a stranger could count on that about you?" Common knowledge shown on the reader, not asserted.

**The feud** (plan.md). "recall a feud in which you later saw that you'd been reading everything they did the worst way. At the time, did it feel like reading, or like seeing?"

**The rabbit hole** (plan.md). "think of someone you've watched go down a rabbit hole. Notice that every piece of counter-evidence became more proof, and that from inside it felt to them like finally seeing clearly."

**The retreat** (Chapter 1, The way out). "Now picture someone going off alone for a month to reflect, and coming back having looked hard at themselves. Can you picture them saying that underneath everything, what they'd found was that they wished they could harm others more?"

**The cashier** (Chapter 1, Limbo). "Picture someone who's done nothing to you, like the last cashier you spoke to, and see their face clearly. Imagine giving them a small kindness first: a real smile, a thank-you. Then a small cruelty: a sneer, a cutting remark. Did either one need a reason before it would go?"

**The write-off and real indifference** (Chapter 1's holding pen). "Think of someone you've written off. Is not caring about him effortless, the way it is with a stranger on the far side of the world? Or does it take work… It takes upkeep, and real indifference doesn't: there's nothing to keep up."

**The return** (Chapter 1's holding pen). "did that person look different? More like someone than they had while you were doing it?"

**Deflections** (Chapter 2). "Did you plan those deflections? I didn't plan mine. Try it now with something small you'd rather not look at, and watch a reason arrive by itself."

**How much of the day** (plan.md, the outline). "the expense rounded up, the email left unanswered, the tone taken with someone who couldn't answer back. 'Count yours for one day.'"

# Part 2: Inventory of every check in the short book (17 Sept 2026)

*Stateless. Every place the short book asks the reader to look at something in their own experience, grouped by the six-part shape proposed for the general-reader book. For each: what the reader is asked, where it lives now, what it's evidence for, how it fared with the cold readers (five rounds, saved in `llms/claude-2026-08/short-book/readers/`, and the general book's at the git tag `general-book-final`), and the question it leaves open. "Worked" means the reader reported finding something without being told what they'd find.*

### 1. Limbo

**1.1 The wrong you couldn't admit.** "Have you ever done something you knew was wrong at the time, but couldn't fully admit it to yourself until later?" Then: "did it feel like new information? Or like something you had always known, but somehow hadn't *let* yourself know?" (Ch 1, Limbo.) Evidence for: knowing and not-knowing at once. Worked on every reader in every round; the most reliable check in the book. One reader (exam cheat) found a third case the prompt doesn't offer: known, weighed, and overridden by wanting.

**1.2 The deflecting.** "Any time someone came close to exposing it… you found a way to deflect. Did that happen passively?" and "can you see how some part of you knew exactly what you were doing, even as you did it?" Evidence for: the hiding is active. Worked.

**1.3 How you knew.** "Did it arrive before or after you'd reasoned through it?… Why did one side have to argue, and the other not? And which side did you listen to?" Evidence for: the part that knows versus the part that proves. Worked; several readers wrote the question down. The lawyer objected that the arguing side is often the right one.

**1.4 Degrees.** "The worse the behavior, the harder we must hide it." Paired in Ch 3 with **1.4b**: "Think of the smallest wrong you've had to admit to yourself, and the biggest. Which took longer? And was that because you knew the big one *less*?" Evidence for: the knowing grows with the harm. 1.4b is new and untested.

**1.5 With the person in view.** "Can you harm or use someone while staring your conscience straight in the face, with no justification at hand? Try it in imagination… picture the person in front of you, fully in view, and see whether it will go." Evidence for: harm requires turning away. Mixed. Three readers could do a small harm in full view (time entry, overpayment, invoice); one couldn't hold dismissiveness while actually looking at his wife. The honest version is whether you can do it *while seeing them as mattering*, and the result is not guaranteed.

**1.5b Caught mid-cruelty** (proposed, untested). "Have you ever been in the middle of being cruel, and something made you actually see them, they said your name, their face changed, a child walked in, and it stopped you?" The seeing comes first and the stopping follows, so it isn't circular.

**1.5c The public record** (not introspective). Obedience in Milgram's experiments fell as the victim got closer; soldiers resist killing at close range and distance makes it easier; executioners hood the condemned; atrocities are preceded by campaigns to make the victims not count. If harm didn't take turning away, none of that machinery would be needed.

**1.6 Limbo knows itself.** "When you were in limbo, did part of you know you were?" One reader: unverifiable from inside; could be confabulation.

**1.7 How you held yourself.** "When you finally admitted it to yourself, how did you hold yourself?" (the self-contempt passage). New, untested. Both the wise reader and the novice asked for this material.

*Open question it leaves:* if I knew, what was doing the hiding, and what was it for?

### 2. Two strangers

**2.1 The two people who caught you.** (Fuller version in Part 1, "Contempt and Integrity," which has all four quadrants: contempt received, integrity received, contempt sent, integrity sent. The short book uses only the first two.) "Think of two times you were caught doing something you shouldn't have… The first person catches you and lets you know what you are. Not what you did; what you are… What did you do?" / "The second person catches you and is, if anything, harsher… And it lands as… a reminder that *you're better than this.*" (Ch 1, The Two Strangers.) Evidence for: the act/verdict distinction; kind is not nice; the difference is carried by something other than the words. Worked on nearly everyone, and both author-readers wanted it as page one. One reader remembered only the first kind and did *not* dig in: ashamed for a year, and stopped.

**2.2 What were they serving?** "The second person may have had different politics, a different religion… What were they serving? Not you, exactly… Could you have defined it?" Evidence for: the light is recognized, not defined, and across creed. Worked as a question; the novice couldn't get from "I recognized it in her" to "I am it."

**2.3 What the first one added.** "He needed you to feel it" (now hedged as a guess). Two readers objected to knowing the stranger's mind.

**2.4 Being shamed when you're already in the wrong.** "Think of how it feels when you've skirted your own conscience and somebody tries to shame you for it… We think it's somehow different when we do it." (Ch 4.) Evidence for: the privilege we grant ourselves. Not singled out by readers.

**2.5 Where it reaches.** "Recall the last time someone's contempt really got under your skin, and feel for *where* it was trying to reach. Not your pride…" (Ch 1.) Evidence for: contempt and love are addressed to the same place. Worked on all of round 4 and both authors; one author felt it as a status injury and wasn't sure.

**2.7 Correcting someone with integrity yourself.** (Part 1.) "Perhaps you had to do it with more ferocity… and yet something in you knew it would ultimately bring *more* peace. *How* did you know?" Not in the short book. The sending end of the second catcher.

**2.8 The two contempts served the same thing.** (Part 1.) "The time you stood in contempt, and were treated with contempt: didn't it feel like *those* were somehow serving the same thing? Not just your personal egos." Not in the short book. The reader's own route to "two turnings-away have nothing to differ by," which readers called wordplay when it was only asserted.

**2.9 Clean inside and out, versus righteous inside and dirty outside.** (Part 1.) Not in the short book.

**2.6 The dehumanizing gaze.** "Doesn't the person feel somehow absent or 'turned away' from themselves?" (Ch 1.) One reader: no, it feels like being seen too much.

*Open question it leaves:* what did the first one add, and have I ever been the first one?

### 3. The delivering end

**3.1 The stranger's eyes.** "Imagine looking into a stranger's eyes long enough that their face… becomes a place somebody is looking out of… *This one is like me. I want them to be all right.*" Worked for anyone who has done something like it; frame-dependent for others.

**3.2 The same eyes after the atrocity.** "Can you still find that same recognition?… It appears to be what they **are**." and "make the case real." Worked once a real person was used.

**3.3 The verdict wants to land.** "Something in you wants this verdict to *land*; to register deep inside. Can you feel that?" The single sentence most readers named as what got them.

**3.4 The monologue, hot and cold.** "Check if you recognize yourself in any of it." The hot version slid off readers whose contempt is polite; the cold paragraph ("a quiet filing-away… a small satisfaction at being done with them") caught every one of them.

**3.5 Relying on them to know.** "You can rely on your target to know it, and for them to know you know it, etc." Readers found two levels, not the tower.

**3.6 The good kind.** "It just convinces me it's the *good kind* of darkness… See if you've had that feeling before, too."

**3.7 The pill.** (Fuller version in Part 1, "Pill-vs-enlightenment," which also asks whether part of you would be *disappointed to discover they're not fundamentally evil*.) "Deliver the vengeance they so richly deserve. Or give them a pill that ends the threat… Is there a flicker…?" Worked on almost everyone; often "relief" for the public enemy and "robbed" for the personal one.

**3.8 Relief or robbed.** "If the harm simply stopped tomorrow, with nobody punished and nobody exposed, would you feel relief, or robbed?" The check readers said they'd keep. Both authors named it as the idea they'd still be carrying.

**3.9 The case you picked.** "Check whether any of that is true in the case you picked." New.

**3.11 The write-off.** (Part 1, proposed.) The edge in a reply to someone on the other side, and what lit up when they reacted: "Might that have been the point?" Not in the short book; it is the distance case both author-readers said the book lacked.

**3.12 The maneuvering question.** (Part 1.) "We're not… trying to maneuver our enemies into a position where we'll be forced to unleash our righteous wrath, right?" Not in the short book.

**3.13 The same thing, turned toward** (proposed by Aditya; untested). "Notice how much you enjoyed it: the verdict landing, the clip, the takedown. Now go back to the stranger's eyes, the way you held them at the start, and imagine doing the same thing to your enemy from there, and enjoying it the same way. See whether the enjoyment will come." It doesn't prove nobody could. It lets the reader feel, from inside, that the enjoying needs the turning away, which is what their contempt shares with what the sadist does. Needs only 3.1 and one of 3.3, 3.7, or 4.3; it does not need the open secret first.

**3.10 Someone you love.** "The sigh… The look on their face that tells us it landed, which we'll pretend not to have seen. Think of a time you did this." For many readers the hardest hit in the book.

*Open question it leaves:* why would I want that, and what is it that wants it?

### 4. Other checks

**4.1 The primal satisfaction.** "Few things are as primally, viscerally satisfying as punishing evil. Pause and check that."
**4.2 The smaller sizes.** Putting someone in their place; the last word; "*I told you so*, and the small wait… to watch it land"; "the takedown you reread." Worked; "would the correction be enough, or does something in you need them to *feel* it?"
**4.3 The clip.** "Someone on your side has humiliated someone on theirs… What happens in you?" Worked on everyone who met it.
**4.4 Who can really say?** Mixed: some found the shrug word for word; two found "yes, and he's earned it."
**4.5 The extra.** "*You lied to me.* … *Of course you lied to me.*" Worked on everyone; readers began hearing it in their own speech.
**4.6 How sure you are.** "Notice how *sure* you are that it's good."
**4.7 The two heats.** "Watch what happens the moment the harm gets a face."
**4.8 The cause without the contempt.** "Give up only the permission to feel contempt for the people on the other side. Would you still want it?" Produced a real hesitation in the fighter and in one author.
**4.9 Why would I want to stop corruption, when I feel so good judging it?**
**4.10 Someone you once despised and came to see differently, without their having changed.** Worked (a father, an old boss). And its reverse: "on a day when you're tired and self-justifying, everyone looks worse."
**4.11 The sister's ex.** Defending someone you love by tearing down the person who wronged them. Worked.
**4.12 The measure.** "*This one is like me.* Whoever you can't say that about…"
**4.13 The ideology test.** "Does it tell you what to do to the person in front of you before you've looked at them?" And the grand-plan test: does it need contempt now for a good that comes later?
**4.14 The story that restored your faith in humanity.** "You felt gratitude, and none of it benefited you directly." Includes the stranger who dove in after someone else's child, the town that took people in, and the person who gives quietly to something that will never come back to them: "something in you thanks them." Worth adding: *even when the person doing it was on the other side from you.*
**4.15 Are they aimed at the light?** With its stated limit: unreliable on anyone you already hold in contempt.
**4.16 The license.** "*I've been hurt, so I've earned the right to let a little of my own darkness out.* If it's there, look at it." And "look at who is relieved when you hold it."
**4.17 Deciding to stop seeing.** "Hold someone's eyes and decide, while holding them, to stop seeing them. See if you can find the moment." Mixed: some can't; the scientist and the wise reader can find a flinch.
**4.18 The worst person with kindness in their eyes.** "Imagine even the worst person doing their worst with what you'd recognize as genuine kindness in their eyes. See if either picture will hold." Partly forms for some; counterexamples raised: the sadist, the charming psychopath, the bodhisattva captain.
**4.19 The cases.** "Check it against the cases you'd already use that word for." Objections: the sadist; the person who never looked.
**4.20 The tree and the arch.** "Notice what you feel, and whether it arrives before any story about who was deprived." Mostly felt; one reader found grief, not violation.
**4.21 The locker room; the scene of someone getting what they had coming.** Read as staged by one reader.
**4.21b Two people, contemptuous together** (proposed by Aditya as the way into collaboration; untested). Remember two people you know tearing into someone who wasn't there. Watch what happened between them: each a little bolder than they'd have been alone, each handing the other permission, and a warmth between them that had nothing to do with the person they were discussing. Now remember a time you were one of the two. What did it feel like when the other person agreed? What would have happened if someone had said, quietly, *I don't think that's fair*? Then widen it: a group chat, a meeting, a comment thread, a rally. Evidence for: the handshake, and that contempt is shared and amplified beneath what anyone says. It replaces the locker room's staging with the reader's own memory, and gives the institutions passage the check readers said it lacked.
**4.22 The hostage.** "Ask what you're looking at when you look at such a person and feel that particular horror." A lens, not a check.
**4.23 Looking at the contempt itself.** "Where is it? What's it made of?" Partial: one reader found fear under it; the novice found it mostly survived.
**4.24 Maximally turned away** (footnote; "for those who dare"). Untested.
**From the appendices, and wanted in the body by both authors:** cutting in line ("the heat is out of all proportion to thirty seconds"); the forgotten birthday against the cutting remark; the prediction that the wise see everything and no verdict forms; the three marks of "fully seeing."

*Open question it leaves:* if it's this common, at every size, what is it?

### 5. The explanation (acceptable to a naturalist)

Not checks but what the checks are evidence for: harm requires turning away; contempt is addressed to the capacity it denies; the rigged test; why the rival explanations (honest correction, ignorance, cruelty, double standard, status and signaling) each leave something unexplained; and the concession both authors asked for early: "What's left to a naturalist is a universal human faculty that can't be outweighed and happens to have an evolutionary history. That differs from my reading only about origin." Supporting research lives in Appendix 2 and Appendix 1's reading list (Tangney; Braithwaite; Bandura; Trivers inverted).

### 6. My understanding

Marked as belief: care is what awareness is; nothing tells two awarenesses apart, which is what would make the open secret exact common knowledge; the parasite, with its stated drawbacks; coordination across minds; the far end and the hostage; contempt for the world; the correspondences in Appendix 3.

# Part 3: Which thesis each check supports

*The working principle: land a thesis as soon as a check can carry it. IDs refer to the inventory in Part 3. "Strong" means it worked on nearly every cold reader without being told what they'd find.*

**The act and the verdict are different, and kind is not nice.** 2.1 (strong), 2.7, 4.5 the extra (strong), 2.4.
**An open secret: you knew what the first one was doing, he knew you knew, and he was relying on that.** Partly lands straight off 2.1, from the receiving end, with no theory: *could you have said what he was doing? could he have denied it? did you both know that?* Then 3.5 from the sending end, and 2.5. Readers reliably found two or three levels, never the tower, which is all the claim needs.
**Something in me wants the verdict to land, and it isn't after justice.** 3.3 (strong), 3.7 the pill (strong), 3.8 relief or robbed (strong; the idea both authors said they'd carry), 4.3 the clip (strong), 4.2, 3.11, 3.12, 4.9.
**It runs hot and cold, and at every size.** 3.4 (the cold paragraph is what caught the polite readers), 3.10 someone you love (strong), 4.2.
**Limbo: knowing and not knowing at once, and the hiding is active.** 1.1 (the strongest check in the book), 1.2, 1.6.
**The part that knows doesn't argue; the part that proves doesn't know.** 1.3 (strong), 4.6, 4.13.
**Harm takes turning away.** 1.5 (mixed: fails for small harms unless the person is held as mattering), 4.18 (partly), 4.19, 4.17 (mixed). The weakest-supported load-bearing thesis. What it needs, so as not to beg the question:
- *Three senses of "seeing," kept apart.* Having them in sight; modeling their mind (the con man and the sadist are good at this); and being turned toward them, where their wellbeing registers as mattering. The thesis is only about the third. Every counterexample readers raised (the sadist, the charming psychopath, "cruelty with the victim in plain view") is about the first two.
- *Marks of the third that can be checked without knowing whether harm followed* (Appendix 3): their objection would land on you, and you'd have to answer it rather than manage it; their hurt registers as bad, the way yours does; what's in them could change what you do next. With those, the claim is empirical and could be false: nobody who meets all three toward someone harms them *at their expense, for his own sake.* That scope also handles the surgeon, the parent, and the bodhisattva captain, who cause pain while turned toward.
- *The interruption check* (proposed, untested): "Have you ever been in the middle of being cruel, and something made you actually see them, they said your name, their face changed, a child walked in, and it stopped you?" Here the seeing comes first and the stopping follows, so it isn't circular.
- *The receiving end, reworded*: "When someone was cruel to you, were they looking at you, or at a target?" (One reader said the dehumanizing gaze felt like being seen *too much*; "locked on" is targeting, the second sense.)
- *The public record*, which doesn't rest on introspection at all: obedience in Milgram's experiments fell as the victim got closer; soldiers resist killing at close range and distance makes it easier (Grossman); executioners hood the condemned; atrocities are preceded by campaigns to make the victims not count as people. If harm didn't take turning away, none of that machinery would be needed.
- *What would count against it*: someone who meets the three marks and harms anyway, for himself. The psychopathy findings (mind-modeling intact, the registering blunted) fit the thesis. Everyday sadism, where the victim's pain is felt and enjoyed, is the hardest case, and the honest answer is that it fails the second mark rather than that it doesn't exist.
**Contempt is addressed to the very thing it denies, so it admits they're redeemable.** 2.5 (strong), 3.9, the holding argument (why keep someone turned away unless they could turn back?), and **4.25 the shark** (proposed): "Do you feel contempt for a shark? A tornado? A rabid dog? However much harm they do, the feeling doesn't come. It comes only for someone you take to know better." Then the clue: we do this to ordinary wrongdoers, who plainly could turn back, and we do the same thing with the volume turned up to the people we call monsters. If contempt tracked how broken someone was, it would fall off at the far end, as it does for the shark. It rises. That's weak evidence, and only evidence of what *we* take them to be, but it's evidence. Both authors: the idea that would make the book matter, not yet planted well enough.
**It offers itself as the good kind.** 3.6, 4.16 the license, 4.8 the cause without the contempt.
**What you can see in someone tracks your own freedom.** 4.10 (strong), and its reverse, the tired day.
**Love at someone else's expense isn't love.** 4.11 (strong).
**The light is recognized, not defined, and across creed.** 2.2, 4.14, 2.9.
**Goodness looks like one thing across people** (weak evidence, and only for goodness). 2.2 (the second catcher felt aligned with you across every difference of creed), 4.14 (you thank a stranger for a good that doesn't reach you, even one from the other side), and Part 1's "didn't it feel like you were somehow serving the same thing?" The matching claim about the darkness is *not* that it is one thing; it's that two turnings-away have nothing of their own to differ by (2.8). This can't be proved. It can be shown that the reader already half holds it, and they can be asked to see what happens when they act on it. And *what happens when you don't believe it* is not a separate check: it's the whole delivering end. Moral contempt is what disbelieving it looks like in action (*they're a different kind from me*), and the reader has already watched the results.
**It spreads, and mostly through the audience.** 4.3 the clip, 3.11 the write-off, 4.21b two people contemptuous together (untested), 2.8.
**The knowing grows with the harm.** 1.4, 1.4b (untested).
**Self-contempt is the same verdict turned inward.** 1.7 (untested; asked for by two readers).
**The turning away extends to the world.** 4.20 (mostly felt).

**The seeing and the reasoning get bent** (spine thesis 26; thin in the short book, and wanted as its own section). From inside: 4.10 and the tired day; and, proposed, "recall a feud in which you later saw that you'd been reading everything they did the worst way. At the time, did it feel like reading, or like seeing?" From outside: "think of someone you've watched go down a rabbit hole. Notice that every piece of counter-evidence became more proof, and that from inside it felt to them like finally seeing clearly." The point to land: once the verdict is in, it doesn't feel like a verdict. It feels like perception.

**It behaves like an agent** (agency: something that looks like it's steering). It *aims* (3.4: the look is aimed, the reaction anticipated, the evidence kept ready). It *defends itself when approached* (1.2: "any time someone came close to exposing it… you found a way to deflect. Did that happen passively?"). It *evades and adapts* (3.4: every accusation gets turned around; 4.4: the shrug arrives on cue). It *leaves no record* (2.1: nothing the first catcher said could be quoted). The long book's "The Agent" adds: it closes every road but one, and it hides *by* showing. What the reader can check is that it acts as if it had purposes.

**It behaves like a parasite** (a separate set of checks: its relation to the host). Its purposes aren't the host's (4.9: when I could see the trade, more self-serving behavior for me in exchange for a darker world, I didn't want it; 3.8: robbed is not what I'd choose to feel). It hides from the host while showing itself to others (3.4; 2.1 from the receiving end). From inside it feels entirely like you. From outside it passes for the person (2.1: you took the first catcher to be *choosing* it, and that is what made you dig in). And it gains by passing: being taken for the person is what strengthens it (the loop: your verdict on them is what made them dig in). The claim isn't that it is an agent or a creature. It's that it fits both shapes so well that we're fooled about who is steering, in ourselves and in each other, and being fooled is how it keeps going.

**Each one benefits from the other's.** "Benefit" is the right word where "want" isn't, and it's checkable: watch two people in a feud and see whether either comes out less sure; 4.21b; 2.8. Once that is shown for pairs and for groups, a pattern that looks coordinated from above, with nobody coordinating it, is an inference from what's been seen and not a further article of faith.

**What is left as understanding rather than finding:** the old names for it (Mara, the Devil), and the traditions' claim that what they named was real; what the far end is like from inside (4.22 is a lens; 4.24 is an exercise "for those who dare"); and that care is what awareness *is*. None of the book's moral or practical claims needs the last one. They need only that turning away and evil go together in both directions, in the scoped form above. Every round of readers, practitioner and not, sorted these into a different kind from the rest. Note what is *not* on this list: that contempt is shared and amplified between people (4.21b, 4.3, 2.8) is checkable, and belongs with the findings. It's the agent-frame laid over it that belongs with the understanding.
