We're all hypocrites. 

We let ourselves get away with little (and sometimes not-so-little) things we'd never tolerate in others—especially in our enemies.

But what's harder to see is that the mechanism we use to do this is *exactly* the same one at work when people do genuinely awful things. I'm talking full-on atrocities.

What we're doing is shielding our minds *from their own contents*. Just pause and notice how bizarre that is. There's knowledge that's consciously accessible, but we're just... not looking at it.

The deepest hypocrisy of all is pretending the "bad people" are doing something *fundamentally* different. It really feels that way, doesn't it? Or even if it's not fundamentally different, they certainly deserve more judgement for it.

What we're doing in that case is letting ourselves get away with *contemptuous judgement* that we'd never tolerate from others. And why do we do that?

*Because we know this self-deception licenses theirs—which creates a world in which it's easier to hide our own.*

But contempt is special. It's not just self-deception—it's self-deception _about_ self-deception. We deceive ourselves about *theirs* to justify our *own*. That's what makes it self-propagating. A self-referential trap.

And it hides in our very language. "Judgement" can mean "wise discernment," in which case *of course* it's helpful. But we use that as an excuse to smuggle in contempt—the sense that we're inherently better.

I mean, what could it possibly matter how we *feel* about it? As long as we're stopping the bad guys, who cares whether we feel superior while doing it?

And that is **precisely** where the deception enters.

Because it **does** matter. We wear this sense of superiority on our faces and in our tones. And no matter how well we try to hide it, we secretly *want* it to get under their skin, but in a way that we can plausibly deny. (This is unpacked fully in [Part 1](https://monktastic.substack.com/p/the-pattern-part-i).)

We don't even have to express it to them directly. Simply by fomenting contempt amongst our allies, we're ensuring the spread of the very thing we will then later judge. 

To put it plainly: we inject a sense of inherent superiority into our interactions—subtly buried in genuine, valid criticism—in order to create a world we can feel superior to. But this happens through **extremely intricate** causal chains, so that even *we* won't recognize what we've done.

---

If this feels too exposing, that's a good thing. Because this mechanism—it doesn't want to be revealed. That's its whole thing.

Deep down, I couldn't possibly want it. I mean yeah, it may earn me wealth, or status, or even just the feeling of moral superiority—but at what cost?

Pause and think of whatever or whomever you think is "what's wrong with the world," and you'll find people lying to themselves in profound ways. Which means this thing is responsible for the **devastation of our entire planet**.

Yes, the proximal cause is a relatively small handful of especially bad actors. But they are merely "hotspots" where our collective shadow has accumulated—people at whom we can aim our noble vitriol, to ensure it stays alive and well while feeling flawlessly confident we were helping.

This thing is hiding itself from my mind, and acting through me to create a world that I hate—but making me feel okay, or good, or even righteous while engaged in it.

It's like a parasite. It's **using** me. It's using **all of us**—by convincing us that we're the ones using *it.*

It feels like we're *getting away* with something every time we do, doesn't it? 

*"It's a corrupt, faceless company anyway. Who cares if I cheat them a little?"*

*"They're destroying the world. We have to fight fire with fire."*  

*"You can't play fair with people who have no morals."*

And here's the hard part: it _is_ me doing this. I'm fully responsible. But it's not the _real_ me—not who I am underneath the forgetting. Which means I don't deserve contempt. I deserve to have my feet held to the fire with grace. And so do you. And so do _they._

But this parasite isn't just using us individually. Because mine licenses yours, which licenses others'... forming a tight, interconnected mesh. Constantly communicating with each other just beneath our conscious thresholds.

You can actually perceive this if you know how to look—how much of human interaction is just these things coordinating through us. In fact, that's the natural result as we progressively free ourselves from its maw.

That's because, remember, it's **not truly unconscious**. Which means that **every one of us is knowingly participating in something like a massive conspiracy to keep humanity bound in darkness.**

I know how that sounds. Believe me, I know.

It's not paranoia. I'm not exempt. And I'm definitely not the first to notice this.

It's why we have an archetype for the "evil global conspiracy." It's what the ancients were gesticulating at with the name "Satan." If humanity could point it out clearly and explicitly, we already would have. And we would have been free of it. But the whole point is that it carefully evades detection.

And they were right about the *opposite* of "Satan," too. When we fight evil with integrity—that is, without even a *trace* of malice, or contempt, or self-righteousness—we can feel ourselves pointed at something far greater than ourselves. Something that unites us—in defiance of this relentless force that separates us.

>*"Diabolos."* — From Greek: "to throw across," "to divide."

I'm not talking about some kumbaya sentiment, either. We have a **profound** duty to fight evil. Fiercely. Bravely. With everything we have. And sometimes there's nothing nice about it.

But there's a different *way* of fighting it.

That's what this series is about: reclaiming fierceness from the Devil.
