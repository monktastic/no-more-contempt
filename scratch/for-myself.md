Have you noticed how there's **nothing** more primally, viscerally satisfying than violently punishing evil? But if you look closely enough into your mind while feeling this way, here's what you'll find.

Something **predatory** in your mind is revealing itself to them, through your tone and gaze—showing them that what lives in them lives in you, too. But you'll tell yourself you're doing no such thing. You just want them to feel what it's like to be on the receiving end. Just showing them your teeth, so that they'll know what happens if they don't stop.

But you already know what will happen, don't you—that they'll *embrace* their darkness? But that's fine by you, because it only proves what you already knew: they really are beyond help. After all, what kind of monster reacts that way to *righteousness?*

Which means you won't have to feel bad when you do to them what you must.

Part of you sees this all even as you're doing it, but you'll never admit it to yourself—because then *you'd* be the monster.

And this unwillingness to confront your own darkness: that's what convinces them to embrace theirs. Because you're showing them that genuine goodness is a myth—that all that matters in this world is what we can get away with; what we can prove. After all, if even the so-called "good people" can't tell darkness from light in their own minds, what hope do *they* have?

The whole sordid plan—from start to finish—is embedded into the very start. Baiting them into embracing their darkness by showing them yours: that **is** the darkness you're showing them.

But no one can prove this, can' they? And that's exactly what makes it work. What makes the darkness increasingly indistinguishable from the light—until the latter no longer exists at all.
