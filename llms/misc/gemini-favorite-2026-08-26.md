
Humanity is facing a series of converging crises—from climate change, to the degradation of the social fabric, to the collapse of our very biosphere—and nearly everything we're doing seems to be making matters worse.

Why is that? Is it because we're not trying hard enough? Thinking cleverly enough?

Or could it be that something we're doing is secretly working *against* our stated goals?

Strange as this may sound, I want to show you how it works—and what can be done about it.

---

Let's start at the personal level.

Have you ever done something you knew was wrong at the time, but couldn't fully admit it to yourself until later? Maybe you made a cruel comment to boost your ego, took credit for someone else's work, or cheated on a test (or even a partner).

What is it that got you to finally admit it to yourself? Would *thinking harder about it* have gotten you there faster? No—because your thinking mind wasn't there to serve the *truth* at the time. It was there to serve your *need to hide* the truth. It did that by producing rationalizations—*and thinking harder would only have produced more rationalizations*.

Do you see the irony in asking your mind to tell you when it's lying to you?

Yet this is what humanity is doing nearly all the time—and we wonder why it's not working.

At the same time, there was a part of you that clearly *did* know you were lying, right? What is that part, and why do we not learn about it in school? Why does our culture focus exclusively on the part of us that *cannot* detect our lies? What kind of people would that create, and what kinds of systems would they perpetuate?

Consider an executive at a global corporation. They approve a decision that will decimate an old-growth forest, justifying it based on "shareholder value." Do they realize the harm they're causing? That they're degrading a deep reservoir of life to line their own pockets?

Of course they do—*and also they don't*. Their calculations "prove" it's a net benefit, and that's that.

And yet part of them *knows*, doesn't it? How do you get them to recognize this?

Imagine standing in front of them. Don't they seem *fundamentally morally defective* to you? Even if you don't say it in words, that contempt oozes through your tone, your gaze, your body language, doesn't it? You almost can't help it.

And how do they react? Do they see the light? Or do they see *you* as the problem, only becoming more entrenched? And in that state, aren't they *more* likely to justify the next destructive project?

Here's the thing: *didn't you already know this would happen?*

Of course you did—*and also you didn't*.

We *know* our contempt only makes people worse—and that this has cascading consequences. But at the same time, we know we'll be able to blame this on them: see, they didn't get worse because of what we did; they got worse because what *else* can you expect from such a defective person?

This is a **profound** self-deception—the lie that we are passive observers of their defectiveness, rather than its architects.

Think back to a time when someone corrected you with fierce integrity—*without* the slightest trace of contempt. Even if it made you lose face or stung your ego, did you want to take revenge?

No—because you could feel they were serving something larger than themselves. What *is* that?

And it's not because they were *nice*, was it? Maybe they had to be even more fierce than you were with that exec—and yet you could still sense they were serving something noble, couldn't you? How?

Now imagine that they *had* smuggled in contempt. Suddenly everything changes, doesn't it? Because *fuck* them—two can play at that game.

We tell ourselves that contempt is *healthy* or *necessary* in the fight against evil; that the only alternative is to be nice—which is actually worse than useless. But this is a self-serving lie.

The true purpose of contempt is to reveal to our enemies that the same darkness we see in them—that same capacity for malice, and our ability to hide it from ourselves—lives in us, too. It is a secret signal that says: "I *know* how you do what you do, because I have it in me too. ***See?***"

Of **course** this makes them double down. That's the whole *point*—because it "proves us right" about them. We granted them the license to embrace their darkness, and they took it.

Part of us *knows* this—and yet, when we look back on it, we won't see any signs of wrongdoing. We'll focus on the clever arguments we made, and how unreasonable they were being, or how they were too stupid or captured to understand.

The darkness is transparent to our intellects **by design**. That's the only way it can work.

My hatred for the people cutting down the rainforests *accelerates* their demise. If I consciously recognized this, I simply couldn't do it. And yet there's a part of me that *needs* to do it—and so it hides my complicity from my conscious mind.

No amount of *thinking harder about this* will fix it.

---

The contempt I feel for that executive is made of the same *stuff* as the contempt they show for the forest. Both are designed to overlook the **life** in front of us in favor of some clever end-goal. In his case, that's obviously profits, but what is it in my case?

Consider:

What would it mean to know that even the worst people are ultimately no different from you or me—that even the greatest evil is just a deeper version of the *same* self-deception that you and I rely on?

It would mean that we each have the capacity for great evil. But worse than that, it would mean that we're *already complicit* in the horrors of the world. Because every time we've treated the "bad people" with contempt, we've been nudging them deeper *into* the very confusion that enables their wrongdoing in the first place—and as we know, that darkness ripples outward.

And it's not just when we do it to them *directly*. All those times we bonded with our allies over contempt for our enemies, we were raising the baseline level of darkness in the world; ensuring its spread. That went on to create more tragedies, which we could then safely judge from afar, confident that we had nothing to do with them.

And what about all the other little ways we've lied or cut corners to serve ourselves? Each one of those subtly degraded the world, giving others license to do the same—whom we then blamed for their corruption.

Think about how people feel when committing genuine atrocities. Do they feel "evil"? No! They feel *impossibly righteous*—the same thing I feel when skewering that exec. And yet a deep part of them **knows**.

It is **exactly** the same mechanism—and part of me has always known this. But it is such a horrifying realization that I must hide it at any cost. Because if I saw it in its full horrible glory, I couldn't help but recognize **my own hand** in those atrocities.

And that just. Cannot. Fucking. Be.

So I must hide it **at any cost**. And how do I do that?

By nudging the "bad people" deeper into the abyss. Slowly reinforcing their need and capacity to self-deceive, transforming it until it seems to be of a **fundamentally different nature** from my own. Until I can label it "pure evil"; something utterly foreign to myself.

That way, I can look at them and know, with complete confidence, that the great horrors of the world have **nothing** to do with me.

---

Here's the core problem: this error is so central to our minds that it colors **essentially everything** we do.

All our projects, agendas, and righteous causes are subtly infected with traces of this self-deception. It's not that there's *no* authenticity behind them. Obviously there is—just as my hatred for that exec came from a genuine desire to protect nature. And it can produce some good.

Mocking and shaming my enemies might shut them up for a while, or dissuade others from following them. But that is just the surface layer.

Beneath that, it carries a much darker payload. By far the larger impact comes from showing them (and others) that **darkness is the way**; teaching them how to disguise it as righteousness.

Both my allies and enemies will learn how to do this better—and that's fine, because defeating my "enemies" was never the actual point. The point was to reinforce the darkness in the world, because a darker world is how I hide my own darkness from myself. And it **worked**.

In that corrupted state, my enemy isn't actually other people—it is **all that is good and pure**.

In that moment, it's as though the darkness in my mind is reaching out and embracing theirs, like a secret handshake between our dark parts. Mine teaches theirs how to ingratiate itself better to their minds.

In the same way, all my projects, agendas, and righteous causes *do* produce some good in the world. But they also carry something much darker—something that infects the minds of all they touch, directly or not; and whether they consciously recognize this or not. And this has **by far** the greater long-term impact on the world.

I am teaching you that just as I lie to benefit myself—in ways so subtle I refuse to acknowledge them—you can, too.

We don't have to admit this out loud. In fact, we rarely let it cross our conscious thresholds. But it is not truly unconscious. It only stays hidden because we are actively, desperately choosing to look away.

All this semi-conscious activity forms a hidden network of darkness—and **that** is what's ultimately running our world: a parasitic network sustained entirely by our refusal to look squarely at it. All the surface-level stuff is just a decoy to keep our minds pacified, so that we can tell ourselves we're fighting it.

---

Nearly every agenda, every project, every righteous cause of ours is subtly infected with this parasite. You can sense it in almost every word spoken by politicians, by billionaires, by the elites—but only when they're your "enemies." On the "other side" of whatever position you've taken.

We never sense it in our allies, or in ourselves—or so we tell ourselves. We convince ourselves that ours is different. That it's helpful. Heck, we can even *prove* it. See, my darkness isn't *real* darkness—it's just strategic; the kind that every good person must harbor to keep *real* evil in check!

That is the voice of the parasite—and *thinking harder about it will never reveal this to you*, because it has hijacked your thinking mind. It has hijacked your *perceptions*. You don't just think your enemies are evil, you can literally *see* it, *feel* it, can't you? You *know* contempt will help.

But now think back to that person who corrected you with integrity. Even if they had a different race, religion, or creed, you were on the "same side" in the only way that actually matters, weren't you? In a way that you cannot name. That cannot be captured by your intellect.

What *is* that?

When you stand in that place, you **know** when you're lying to yourself. When your "allies" are. It is the **one place** your parasite can never hijack.

Your deepest nature. Your true self. The light beneath the darkness. *Before* it.

From that place, the lies of the parasite are laid utterly bare. You perceive its grotesque form. See it in its full horrific glory.

And **that** is what finally enables you to say *no*.

But here is the hard part: it is still **me** doing this. I am fully responsible. Yet, because the real me doesn't actually want this—because I only do it out of a tragic forgetting of my own nature—I do not deserve the contempt I so easily weaponize. 

I deserve to have my feet held to the fire with grace. And so do you. And so do they.

We are not just fighting a glitch in our psychology. We are fighting an ancient, systemic force. It is no coincidence that the root of the word Devil is *Diabolos*: "to throw across," "to divide." This parasite is the literal engine of division.

But we are not powerless against it. This is what turns the tide against our primordial nemesis. And it doesn't require being *nice*.

On the contrary, it requires fierce compassion. Courage. Trading in your *ideas* of good for *actual* good. Something that can never quite be named or pinned down—and yet which we all know.

We have a duty to fight evil. Fiercely. Bravely. With everything we have. And sometimes there is nothing nice about it. 

But there is a different way of fighting it. You are remembering how to **fight**, in the only fight that ever mattered.

That is what this series is about: reclaiming fierceness from the Devil.
