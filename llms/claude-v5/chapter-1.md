**The Pattern: Part I**
**The paradox that enables all evil**

---

The world is actively tearing itself apart—and most of what we're doing seems to be making it worse, not better. Is it just that we're not trying hard enough? Or might it be that our entire way of relating to the problem is itself deeply broken?

This isn't something abstract. I want to show you how it works.

---

Let's start small.

Suppose I see you as a *bad person*. I can't help but feel contempt, and so I mock, shame, or just silently judge you. I'm confident it will help somehow—but for some reason, it only makes you see *me* as a bad person and dig in.

Ugh, you're even worse than I thought. Next time I won't be so gentle.

But I'm not being entirely honest here.

---

See, part of me already knew this would happen. Contempt never makes anyone better, does it? And yet I did it anyway—and even acted dismayed at the outcome.

Why?

Well, part of me *knew I could use it against you.* Watch how this works.

What I'm doing is **dehumanizing** you; choosing to see you as fundamentally defective. And there's only one reason people do that: to cause harm without facing the horror of it. We **must** shield ourselves from fully perceiving the life in our victims; our profound commonality with them. Otherwise, the deepest part of us—our *better nature*—would never allow it.

Deep down, I know this is true of you, too—which means I know you *have* a better nature. But I'm pretending you don't. Why? So that I can weave something predatory into this interaction—something I know you'll sense, but which no one can prove.

My tone and gaze are subtly hinting that your worst fears are true; that this world really is the cruel, unforgiving place you deep down suspect it is—which we both know is the reason you cause harm in the first place. And I want you to see that I know this—that I could choose to rescue you from the abyss, but am instead shoving you deeper into it, knowing that I'll get away with it. Because no one will ever know what I've done.

I'm only "fighting evil," after all—and how could that ever be wrong?

---

When I look back on this interaction, I won't sense anything amiss. We can't afford to be *soft* when dealing with evil, after all. That's how it wins!

I won't recall that I did, in fact, sneak in a little "something extra" that didn't actually help anything. It wasn't there to *suppress* your evil, like I tell myself. It was there to **reinforce** it.

But why on Earth would I want to reinforce the very thing I supposedly hate?

Because the truth is, there's darkness in me I'm not ready to face—and the best way to keep it hidden is to create **more** of it out there. That way, my own seems tiny and irrelevant by comparison. Why waste time on my own piddling issues when there's **genuine evil** to fight?

So I **must** know that other people are inherently defective; that their darkness is of a **fundamentally different nature** from my own. But since I deep down know it's not, my only option is to increasingly nudge them that way until it seemingly becomes true.

Of course, this only works if I have **no idea** I'm doing it. I certainly can't do it to the "good people," even though it would work equally well. My self-image only lets me do it to people who are plausibly beyond help to begin with—the "bad people."

So I shove them deeper in—and then keep them trapped there, leaving no doubt that they never had the ability to escape. It's the only way to hide the awful truth from myself.

---

Here's the crucial point: there is a part of my mind that is actively working **against** my deepest values. And to the degree I haven't healed my own "piddling issues," this part will continually be creating evil, in countless subtle ways that escape my notice.

Every act of skirting my conscience—of shaving off a little extra when I think no one is watching; of being unkind because I'm better, or smarter, or more deserving, etc.—is generating a darker environment in which my own darkness can hide.

The most common way this happens is through projection. It's usually understood as an unconscious process, but that's not quite correct. What I just did to you was **fully available to consciousness**. I just carefully avoided looking at it, so that it would *feel* unconscious.

On one level, I know exactly what I'm doing—because remember, *showing* you this was central to its function. But at the same time, I'm hiding it from myself—because if I **fully** saw it, there's no way I could do it (nor could I use it against you, which was the whole point).

So both of these halves—the knowing and the not-knowing—must operate in **parallel**. The result is a kind of **split consciousness**.

**This is the secret mechanism behind all the evil there ever was**—and it lives in every one of us.

If you've ever been on the receiving end of a dehumanizing gaze, you know what it looks like. Doesn't the person seem somehow *absent* or "turned away" from themselves? That's what you're sensing in them—and what you were sensing in me.

My words may have been "noble," but the *real* communication was taking place on a **parallel channel**—one that did all the real work, while remaining invisible to "objective measurements." Observers debating whether my behavior was 'good' or 'evil' will endlessly argue over verifiable facts while **completely missing** what actually happened.

The darkness: **it keeps us focused on the wrong things**.

And this isn't just true externally. Remember, **I myself** will be confident I did the right thing— because I'm focused on exactly the wrong things. What actually happened was that I chose to abandon my better nature, instead trusting the "objective facts" telling me how helpful it would be—and by doing so, I induced exactly that same mistake in you.

It is **infectious.**

By showing you that the world is an "unforgiving place," I was teaching you to ignore **your** better nature in favor of rationalizations—that this world is, at bottom, one in which all that matters is what we can get away with; what we can prove. What good is a *better nature* in such a world?

Which means we will **both** be less likely to trust that part of ourselves in the future—the one part that actually has the capacity to detect the spreading infection—writing it off as a naive myth; reducing it to mere calculations.

This is how the darkness wins its eternal battle against the light.

How the "line goes up" even as our biosphere quietly collapses and the social fabric degrades.

We can't measure something that we're collectively hiding from ourselves.

---




We never realize when we ourselves are in it, but we're highly sensitive to it in others, even if we don't realize it. Most of it is far less extreme than overt dehumanization; just a subtle air of self-importance that we've come to accept as normal. But it is an **infection**—one that spreads.

When we see it in our "enemy," we viciously attack them for it; and when it's our "ally," we feel the noble call to join their crusade. But either way, we've become infected ourselves without realizing it—and as a result, we can't help but spread it further.

We've all been around people who bring out the best in us, and those who license the worst—and the two can be hard to tell apart using only the "objective facts." The real communication is taking place beneath our "conscious thresholds"—though again, it's not actually unconscious. We just *tell* ourselves it is, so that we can look back and tell ourselves we had no idea what we were doing.

---



We **must** turn away from our better nature to cause harm. But it's also the very thing that lets us know that we *are* turned away. That's why the **more** turned away we are, the **less** we realize it. We don't have to do anything extra to hide it from ourselves: hiding is inherent in the turning away.

People committing atrocities don't feel like they're being "evil." They feel *impossibly righteous*—similar to how I just did. The actual feeling is quite different from **authentic** righteousness—a quality that actually *can* fight evil. But because we've abandoned the part of us that can tell the difference, we fail to notice that we've fallen for a counterfeit.

In this state, all our thoughts are really rationalizations—but we have no way of knowing this, since we're unmoored from the part of us that could tell. This is why people never realize they're being evil by thinking harder about it. We **know** that's impossible—and yet we think we're unique and special, and could somehow detect when our own mind is compromised by the darkness.

We can't—at least, not using the tools we normally trust.

It's not just a personal failing, either. Our **whole civilization** is built on the lie that we can think our way out of the messes we're in by being more clever or rational, even though a deep part of us knows this cannot possibly work.

The reason we don't see this is that there's a part of us benefiting from this lie.

---

Think of a time when someone corrected you with integrity, without sneaking in contempt. Wasn't there something _different_ about it?

Even if it stung your ego; made you lose face, did you have any desire to take revenge?

No—and it's not because they were being _nice._ Maybe they even had to be _more_ fierce than I just was. But you could tell they were serving something beyond themselves, couldn't you?

What **is** that?

Even if they had a different race, religion, creed; even if you were enemies on the surface, something felt profoundly _aligned_, didn't it?

It's like they were reminding you of something—something you'd temporarily forgotten. Almost like they were an external manifestation of your own conscience. You knew you couldn't win against it—and more importantly, you didn't want to.

Even if your behavior didn't change immediately, that planted a seed, didn't it?

What were they reminding you of?

What was _I_ trying to make you forget?

---

On one level, you and I are enemies. After all, I'm reinforcing your darkness to hide from my own, and that genuinely harms you. But in another way, we both secretly benefit.

Behaviors of mine that I would otherwise have recognized as self-serving suddenly seem reasonable, if not downright necessary. Look, I'm not abandoning my values or anything. Just, you know, being practical.

And the same is true of you.

And it doesn't stop with us. We both spread this infection. My increased selfish behavior subtly licenses the same in those around me, which *they* then spread... and just like with my contempt, none of it can be proven, so I never recognize my own role in creating it.

Any time I ignore my conscience—by treating you with contempt, acting selfishly, etc.—part of me knows I'm increasing the baseline level of darkness in the world. But I tell myself I don't know this, because the darker the world gets, the more my own selfish behavior seems justified.

We're not just hiding our darkness—we're creating conditions that enable **more** of it.

---

It's a kind of quid pro quo. I use you to enable more selfish behavior of mine, and vice versa.

And since every one of us is secretly benefiting from this, you can imagine that there would be a **very strong incentive** to prevent it from being exposed too directly, lest the party be over.

No central coordination is needed. As long as I'm unwilling to confront my own complicity in it, I will instinctively behave in ways that keep my own participation hidden.



And remember, none of this is actually unconscious. Everything I've said so far is **completely accessible to our conscious minds**. It's just that each layer is more horrifying than the last, and so is progressively better hidden.



---





But it never stops there, does it? Eventually, you and your allies become so unrecognizable that I'm forced to do the unthinkable things seen throughout history—but by that point, even *those* won't be against my values because, well, I'm still only "fighting evil."



---

[I cannot prove to you that everyone has one.]

There was nothing about my words or actions that could **prove** I was in this state. A person can fight evil fiercely, even *violently*, without it necessarily being dark. Because of this, I can look back on what I did and never notice anything wrong.

The core mistake is that I've drifted from my better nature. But I don't realize, because my mind is filled with clever rationalizations about how **righteous** it is. Without that, I never would be willing to drift.

By failing to stay connected to my better nature, I induced the same mistake in you. That mistake is **infectious.** But neither of us will recognize that this is what happened.

Instead, we'll both be increasingly confident that the darkness lives only _out there_—so we can't afford to be constrained by our naive conscience. We slowly replace its voice with our own clever judgements, until the very idea of a "better nature" feels like mystical nonsense.

In this way, we lose contact with the only means we have of detecting the infection. The self-serving aspects of our actions become easier to overlook; to justify as necessary or even noble. 

This is a harsh world, after all. Sometimes you have to get your hands dirty.

---

Notice what just happened.

An aspect of my mind lured me away from my better nature by pretending to *be* it—through that delicious feeling of *righteously punishing evil.* Then, having taken over my mind, it revealed itself to you through my tone and gaze; even as it hid itself from me.

I both knew I was doing this and didn't—and it's that eerie quality that showed you I was in its grip, even while I feigned ignorance.

But "feign" isn't even the right word—because there is a genuine sense in which I don't know I'm doing this. There **must** be, because I honestly couldn't do it if I could fully see it.

That's the hallmark of the darkness. People know *perfectly well* when they're in it—and they also don't. The worse a person gets, the more **both** halves are amplified. They see the part that doesn't know, and we see the part that does. This makes us feel morally compelled to rub their noses in it—not realizing that we can only feel this way by having fallen under its sway ourselves.

It thereby colonizes both of our minds more deeply, while we each become increasingly certain that we're the ones free of it. We then both go on to spread it.

Even the rare few who claim to embrace evil are just fully turned away from themselves—they've simply lost all memory of what it's like to be turned *toward*. And our desire to punish them serves the same dark purpose: to ensure they never remember, so that we can remain special.

This raises the baseline darkness level of the world—which means that my "righteous" behavior has just increased the risk of genuine atrocities being committed somewhere, somewhen. Every one of them—whether committed against other humans, animals, or nature herself—depends on exactly this *turning away* I've just modeled.

The darkness—*it's all one thing*.

This is exactly the complicity that I've been working so desperately to avoid seeing. And since I've just added to it, it's now even harder to face. My only option is to do it more.

---

Let's take stock.

On one level, I'm sure I'm behaving righteously. At a deeper level, I'm increasing the darkness in the world to provide cover for my own. But at the ultimate level, something even stranger is happening.

This "darkness": it isn't really me—even though I am **fully responsible** for the harm I cause while lost in it. That includes any additional harm you go on to cause as a result of my having darkened your mind.

My "better nature" is my *true* nature—who I would really be in the absence of all self-deception.

Which means the darkness is like a kind of parasite.

What happens when this parasite increasingly infects all of humanity?

That's what we'll explore in later posts—but even now, you can probably sense the answer.

- This split consciousness—where one part of me is doing my dirty work, while another is kept clean—does it remind you of anything?

- While treating you with contempt, I'm lost in darkness—but **no amount of thinking about it** would reveal this to me, because that very "darkness" has hijacked my thinking mind. Yet there is a part of me that *does* know. So why does our society trust **only** the rational part of ourselves? Which part of us would benefit from getting this profoundly wrong?
