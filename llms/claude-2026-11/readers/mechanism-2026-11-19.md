# Cold reads, November 19: the preface and Chapter 1, for The Mechanism

*Two readers, the preface and Chapter 1 as committed in cb6c544, read 30 lines at a time. Helen Marsh: line editor, Quaker, hot contempt. Tomás Reyes: ER charge nurse, Al-Anon, cold contempt. Letters verbatim.*

## Helen, letter 1

Dear Jo,

I've read the preface and I'm stopping, as instructed, before I open the chapter.

What happened: the first paragraph got me. The mushrooms, "literally," and then the dry little "I doubt anyone needed magic mushrooms to tell them that." I laughed. I thought, all right, he's not a guru, he's someone who's been annoyed by gurus. Then the second page tilted the other way. "Every one of us already knows, deep down." "Patiently waiting to be remembered." I felt the old prickle I get when someone in a workshop tells me what I'll discover. I don't like being told what I know.

But then he defined contempt as treating your enemies as though they were "inherently defective," and I thought of Carol, and I didn't like how fast I thought of her. That's the honest version. I don't think "Carol did a bad thing." I think "there is something wrong with Carol." I've said nearly those words to my sister. I've said them in the voice Maddie says I do.

And earlier, "refused to love, out of a confused sense of duty." I do tell myself that. Someone has to remember what she did. Someone has to warn people. It feels like a duty. I'd never have called it a refusal to love. I'm not yet willing to call it that now, but the word "duty" stuck to me.

The big claims he just says. Evil is the opposite of love. Contempt is "uniquely insidious." This confusion is behind "essentially all the horrors of the world." He says the book earns "evil." I'm holding him to that. He also says where something is only what he sees, he'll say so, and I liked that more than anything else on the page.

The question I'm carrying in: if love is the right response to literally everything, what is it, that it could be the right response to Carol? Because the only versions of love I can imagine toward her are either lying or rolling over, and I've done enough therapy to know rolling over isn't love.

A second, smaller question: what exactly does he think I already know?

More after the chapter.

Helen

## Helen, letter 2

Dear Jo,

I finished the chapter. I made tea twice, which for me is a measure.

I'll tell you first what I'm walking out with, because it isn't what I expected and it isn't in the part you asked about. It's one sentence: "A child can't refuse it; he believes it, and carries it for years." And then, a page later, the paragraph about the sigh, the "what's wrong with you?" he sends to people he loves, and "the look on their face that tells me it landed, which I pretend not to have seen." You know what Maddie said to me last year, about the voice. I have always assumed she stopped telling me about her fights because she didn't want me being savage about her friends. Reading that, I wondered for the first time whether she was afraid of something else: that she'd grown up watching people stop being *in there* for me, and didn't know what it would take for her to be next. He doesn't say I did that. He says what a child does, and leaves me alone with it. I've been alone with it since about four o'clock.

Now Carol. The two-button exercise caught me. Vengeance or a pill that changes her heart, nobody ever knows. My hand went to the first button before I'd finished the sentence. When I made myself press the pill, what I felt was disappointment. She'd get off without ever knowing she'd been wrong. So what I want isn't for her to change. It's for her to suffer and know why. I didn't like learning that, and I can't un-learn it.

You asked about the section called "The Mechanism." Here is what happened, in order.

1. Did it flow? It went up, down, and up. It starts well. "Now look at what this trap depended on" is a fair signal that we're switching from story to looking at the story, and I wanted that. The first click was "The look saying we were different kinds of thing depended on our knowing we weren't." That's the chapter's first line coming home and I felt it arrive. Then it got steep. "I knew he knew, he knew I knew he knew, and so on" put me in a philosophy seminar for a sentence; I counted the knews. "Common knowledge" I only understood because the sentence before it had done the work; in ordinary speech it means what everybody knows, and that isn't what he means. Then the bold sentence, which I'll come to, and then it doubled back. "And he had to see me keeping that knowledge in limbo" is the second "had to" from the top of the section said again. "I was making him see that I was counting on his seeing through me" I read three times and still lost my footing. "I was in limbo about provoking him—and revealing that I was in limbo was itself the provocation" was the same knot tied a third time. In between those, "He had nothing to quote... The look was the deed" put me back on the ground; that was the one place in the knotty stretch I didn't have to work. And the aside, "(Analytical readers may appreciate seeing this spelled out in Appendix 1.)", pulled me right out. I am an analytical reader and I felt sorted. After that it climbed again to the end. Earlier, just before the section, "the same basic trick they use to do wrong" was the third time he'd made that point in about fifteen lines, so I came into the section already a little full.

2. Where it peaked: two places, and the second is higher for me. The first: "I was making our sameness felt so he could feel me betray it." When I rehearse the cutting thing to say to Carol, I pick the line that will reach her as a colleague, as someone who knows what fair dealing is. You couldn't wound a "defective" thing with it. I need her to be my kind for the line to work. The second, and the truest: "Underneath the argument, my enemy and I were never fighting over whether a shared ground exists. We were fighting over which of us still stood on it." Carol says I'm impossible to work with. I say she stole my clients. We are making the same accusation from opposite ends: you broke faith with what we both know a decent colleague is. We agree completely about the ground. I'd never once seen that. I sat with the book closed for a while.

3. "What I had to turn away from in myself was the thing I was reaching in him." The first time it slid past me. I didn't know whether "reaching" meant reaching for, or reaching as in getting through to. The second time it caught, and only because of the buttons. I want Carol to know why she's suffering. Knowing why needs her conscience. So I'm aiming at the part of her that knows right from wrong, and I can only aim at it with mine put away in a drawer. That's what I take it to mean: the place I'm trying to hurt in her is the place I've stopped living in myself. So yes, recognition, but earned by the exercise, not by the sentence. Without the buttons it would have been an aphorism someone told me. And honestly, it was a few lines later, "Love and contempt are addressed to the same place," that made it fully clear. The later sentence explained the earlier one better than the earlier one explained itself.

4. "Open secret": by then I'd already found it. I found it at "depended on our knowing we weren't," and harder at the shared-ground lines, which come just after. So it named something I had, and the bold felt a bit like someone putting a label on a jar I'd already filled. It's also the third time I'd been told we're "the same kind of thing," and the phrase had gone slippery on me. So "In what sense?" did help; I needed the narrowing. And "not that we're equally culpable: we're not" mattered more to me than anything else in that paragraph. Carol did take my clients. If he'd said we were equally guilty I'd have shut the book. But the answer, that we both know how to "orient" toward goodness, was weaker than the question. "Orient" is a new abstract word just when I wanted something I could see.

5. The pondering line. I did pause, but it was a short pause. I remembered the preface's question without looking back, because I'd carried it in on purpose: what would love toward Carol even be? So the connection was live for me. But the sentence itself, "For now it's worth pondering the connection," was flat. It's the voice of a syllabus. It's also just after "is a question I'll come back to later," so two IOUs in one paragraph. And "a broader definition of love" isn't quite what the preface asked; the preface said the word means nothing like what we usually mean, not more of it. Then he answered it six lines later ("Love and contempt are addressed to the same place"), before I'd done any pondering. So the question mattered, and the line asking it felt tacked on. The answer was the good part: love toward Carol would be addressed to the same Carol I'm trying to wound. It could be harsh. It could be Ruth across her desk saying "You rushed this. You don't rush." That cleared up the thing that had worried me since the preface.

6. Where I was handled: not much, in that section, and I want to say that plainly, because I came in braced for it. The aside about analytical readers was the closest, a small pat on the head. "All of that had to be in place for my contempt to be coherent at all" told me a conclusion I couldn't check; I don't know what an incoherent contempt would be. "The structure my identity stands on" is therapist's-whiteboard language, and that kind of phrase makes me feel managed even when nobody means it to. But mostly he says "I" and lets me decide whether I'm in it. That's why it got through to me. Elsewhere in the chapter: "deep down" in the preface, "it turns out to be one more instance," and "it still wouldn't have made you dig in" (he asked me, then answered for me). Small.

7. Cut: "I was making him see that I was counting on his seeing through me." It's the place where I lost the thread, and the sentence on either side holds the point without it. Never cut: "We were fighting over which of us still stood on it." Close second: "He had nothing to quote... The look was the deed."

8. What I'm carrying out: the child who carries it for years, and Maddie. The sigh. And one thing I hadn't expected to find. The exercise near the end asks for a time I felt the "of course you did" coming and didn't send it. Maddie at sixteen, after she backed the car into the Hendersons' fence. "Of course you did" was right there. I said "Are you hurt?" instead, and she cried and told me everything, including that she'd been on her phone. I'd forgotten I had that other voice. I have it. That's the recognition: I already know how to do the other thing. I've done it with the person I love most. I just don't do it with Carol, and I do the first voice in front of Maddie, which is how she learned which one I use.

9. Was any of it obvious? Some. Limbo, the knowing-without-admitting, I've known since my women's group made me say the Hollis thing out loud. That a judge who despises you makes you dig in, everyone knows. What changed is that the obvious thing now has consequences I can't wave off. I knew I enjoyed Carol's stumbles. I didn't know the enjoyment was leaking through a voice my daughter has been learning from, and that every time I "warn people" about Carol I'm teaching whoever's listening that people can stop counting. So this week: I'm not going to tell the Carol story at Sunday dinner, which I was planning to, because Lise is bringing up the Pemberton thing. And I think I'm going to call Maddie and tell her I heard what she said last year, and that I'm working on it, and not make her do anything about it. That's as far as I've got. I don't know what love toward Carol is yet. I think it might start with shutting up about her in front of my daughter.

The chapter ends on a question, "What direction *is* that?", and I was a little startled to turn the page and find nothing. But it's the right question. It's the question we sit with in meeting, more or less.

Thank you for this. I mean it, and I'm also a little cross with you.

Helen

## Tomás, letter 1

Dear Marisol,

Read the preface on my break. Short, which I appreciated.

Honest first reaction: the mushrooms opener put my back up a little. You know what I see on a Saturday night. But he kind of laughs at it himself, so I kept going.

What actually got me was one line, about times he'd "refused to love, out of a confused sense of duty." I put the pages down for a second. I don't think of what I do with Rick as refusing to love anybody. I think of it as not taking the bait. Being the grown-up at the table. But if you'd asked me why I don't engage with him, the honest answer is something like duty. Somebody has to not dignify that garbage. So that line landed somewhere I wasn't expecting.

Then he says the main thing is "moral contempt," treating people "as though they were inherently defective." That's the Rick file. Not worth it. Defective model, don't bother. I don't yell. I don't post. I'm polite. But the file is real.

What I didn't like: he tells me a few times that I already know the big thing deep down, and that the book "earns" the word evil, and that if Chapter 1 "provokes the recognition, the rest is detail." That's a lot of promising before showing. In Al-Anon we'd call that telling somebody their story for them. There's a sentence or two that sounded like a pamphlet ("uniquely insidious expression of evil"). And he calls them "enemies." Rick isn't my enemy, so I'm already wondering if this is about the screaming kind of contempt and not mine.

What I liked: he admits he's not living it, he says it's not finished, and he ends with "Nothing here is a verdict on you." I believed him on that one.

The question I'm carrying in: is my kind counted? The calm kind. The kind where I'm not angry at all, I've just stopped. Because if he only goes after the hot kind, I'm going to feel fine about myself, and I don't think I should. Matteo told me I "do the face." I want to know if this book has anything to say about the face.

Also, he says love "means nothing like" being nice. I want to see what it does mean, because nice is mostly what I've got for Rick, and I suspect that's the problem.

Going into Chapter 1 now.

Tomás

## Tomás, letter 2

Marisol,

Finished Chapter 1. It took me two sittings because I had to get up a couple of times. I'll tell you what happened and then answer your questions about the Mechanism part, since you asked.

The short version: it got me, and not where I thought it would.

The opening is good. He doesn't start with Rick-type people. He starts with me doing something wrong and knowing it the whole time. I went to a night years ago when I blamed pharmacy for a drip I was late hanging. I knew while I said it. "Did some part of you know you were doing that, even as you did it?" Yes. That's Al-Anon ground and I was comfortable there.

The two strangers: I didn't have to imagine either one. The first was an attending who said "this is why I double-check everything nurses do" in front of a patient's wife. I hid from him for two years. The second was Delia, my first charge nurse, who was harder on me than he ever was and somehow it made me go back and apologize to a patient. The teacher story, "searching my face for some sign that I knew it too," is Delia exactly.

Then the long middle part is the hot kind of contempt, him confronting some guy, and for a while I was translating. I don't confront Rick. I don't say anything. I kept thinking this is a book about people who yell.

Then three things happened.

The buttons exercise. Vengeance on your enemy, or a pill that changes their heart. I hesitated on the pill. If Rick changed, I'd have to deal with him. Talk to him. Admit I wrote him off. Part of me wants neither button. I want him to stay exactly who he is so I can stay exactly where I am. Nobody told me to find that. It was just there when I looked.

Then "Recall the last time someone's contempt really got under your skin." I went to the attending first. Then it hit me: Rick. The COVID thing. "Nurses who got sick should've been more careful." Jenny was on a vent for eleven days. I put my fork down that Thanksgiving and I've been flat with him since. I never once thought of my flatness as starting from his contempt landing on me. It did. It reached the part that knew Jenny counted. I'm still sitting with that.

Then "The Quiet Kind." "A quiet filing away, a politeness with nothing behind it, and a small satisfaction at being done with them." I use the word filing. And then: "Everyone who witnesses it is being taught the same lesson." That's Matteo. That's the face. He didn't learn it from Rick. He learned it from watching me give it to Rick.

So yes, the chapter reached my kind. I wish it had reached it sooner. I spent the Encounter section feeling half off the hook, and I think a guy like me could stop reading there and feel fine about himself.

Now your questions.

1. Did the Mechanism section flow?

Up and down. It doesn't build in a straight line. It has the best lines in the chapter and the most rereads, sometimes in the same paragraph.

The start was fine. "Now look at what this trap depended on." Then "I had to know he had a better nature I could turn him away from" made me stop, and then agree. You can't make somebody worse unless there's something better in them to pull them off of. That followed from the section before it. Good.

Then I lost the thread at "I knew he knew, he knew I knew he knew, and so on." My eyes glazed. That's a logic class. I got what it meant, but I'm not in a logic class. Right after it, "for my contempt to be coherent at all." Coherent? I'd say "to work."

"I only had to *evoke* that common knowledge." Common knowledge is a phrase I know as "everybody knows." He uses it like a technical term from some book I haven't read. I half-guessed it meant the I-know-you-know thing.

Then it doubled back. "I was letting him feel that I knew he wasn't what I was calling him—and meant him to know I knew" is the "I knew he knew" sentence again. And "I was making him see that I was counting on his seeing through me" is the end of the first paragraph again ("I had to count on him recognizing what that allowed me to do"), only harder. I read that one three times. Then "I was in limbo about provoking him—and revealing that I was in limbo was itself the provocation" is the same thing a third time, in a different coat. Reread it twice.

So: the second half of paragraph two through the end of the "He had nothing to quote" paragraph felt like going around the same block three times, and each time the street names got longer.

Then "(Analytical readers may appreciate seeing this spelled out in Appendix 1.)" pulled me out. It felt like he turned to talk to somebody else in the room.

After that it picked back up. "In what sense?" and the two "not that" lines brought me back in. And "We were fighting over which of us still stood on it" is clean.

2. Where did it peak?

"He had nothing to quote."

That's the cold kind in five words. I have never said one word to Rick. Not one. And the next lines, "If he tried to name it, I could make him sound unhinged. The look was the deed." If Rick ever said to me "you always look at me like I'm stupid," I'd say "Rick, I've never been anything but nice to you," and my wife would back me up, and he'd look crazy. I felt sick. The good kind.

Close second, for understanding instead of recognition: "We were fighting over which of us still stood on it." That's Thanksgiving. We're not fighting about masks. We're fighting over who's the decent one.

The bold line, "I was making our sameness felt so he could feel me betray it," took two reads and then landed, but only after I put it in Rick terms myself. The face only works on him because he's family. It wouldn't do anything to a stranger in my waiting room.

3. "What I had to turn away from in myself was the thing I was reaching in him."

In my words: to give Rick the face, I have to ignore my own decency, and the face works by poking his. Same thing on both sides. I'm stepping on mine to get at his.

It was half recognized, half told. I got it on the second read. It felt more like a summary of things already said in the section ("better nature I could turn him away from," "sameness felt so he could feel me betray it") than a new step. It didn't slide past, but it didn't hit like "nothing to quote." If anything, coming right after three rounds of the knowing thing, it read like a fourth way of saying it. A good way, the shortest one. If he'd said it this way and cut two of the others, it'd hit harder.

4. "Open secret."

By then I'd found it, so it named something I had. But I didn't find it from the knowing-knowing stuff. I found it from the pill, and from "nothing to quote." The phrase itself also told me "we are the same kind of thing as our enemies," which is the chapter's first sentence again. I didn't mind that. It felt like the chapter keeping its promise.

"In what sense?" helped a lot. I was about to bristle. Me and Rick the same? He said that about Jenny. Then "Not that we agree about what's good: we don't. And not that we're equally culpable: we're not." That's what let me keep the thought instead of defending myself. "Orient toward goodness" is a seminar word, but I got it. Rick's a dad. He knows how to do right by his kids.

5. "For now it's worth pondering the connection to the question in the preface."

Honest answer: I paused for a beat and kept going. Did I remember the preface question? Roughly. I remembered "love doesn't mean nice." I'd forgotten the "literally every situation" part until I thought about it later. "It's worth pondering" sounds like a teacher assigning homework, and I didn't do the homework. I was tired from the knowing-knowing stuff.

Also the sentence before it, "Why we should all know this about each other, and know they know it about us, and so on," tripped me. I read "should" as "ought to," like we're supposed to know it. And "and so on" again. And "a question I'll come back to later" is him keeping a ledger out loud.

If the chapter had moved on to something else there, it would have felt tacked on. It didn't, because six lines later he actually did it: "Love and contempt are addressed to the same place." That's the payoff. So the pointer was weak, but the thing it pointed at was the strongest line in the chapter. I'd honestly rather he skipped the pointer and just got there.

6. Where was I handled, or told what I'd find?

Not much in this section, honestly. Less than the preface. The "I knew he knew" paragraphs felt more like being lectured than handled. A few small ones: "the thing we all recognize as dark," right after the section, "we all." And "Analytical readers may appreciate," which isn't handling, but it sorted me into a group. In the rest of the chapter, "Check if that's true of you, too" in The Need, and "Check if it felt like he was reminding you of something: that *you're better than this*," which tells me what I'll find. That one matched Delia, so it didn't bother me much.

7. Cut one, keep one.

Cut: "I was making him see that I was counting on his seeing through me." Three reads, and it's already been said.

Never cut: "He had nothing to quote." And the two sentences after it.

8. Did anything produce a recognition I'm carrying out?

Two, maybe three.

The exercise at "Recall the last time someone's contempt really got under your skin, and feel for *where* it was trying to reach." That's where I found that my flat thing with Rick started as his contempt landing on me, about Jenny. I didn't know that walking in.

"Everyone who witnesses it is being taught the same lesson." Matteo.

And the buttons. Wanting neither button so I don't have to move.

Did it reach my kind? Yes, finally, in "The Quiet Kind," almost word for word. But late. And "The Mechanism," which is the heart of it, is told through the hot kind. It still got me there ("nothing to quote" is pure cold), but I had to do the translating myself. Earlier, at "*and therefore your wellbeing has ceased to matter*," I thought of Rick's chest pains, and how I, an ER nurse, said "he should get that checked" in my waiting-room voice. That got me through the hot example, not around it. But I had to bring it.

9. Obvious? Newly consequential?

Some of it I already knew. Inventory, conscious-but-not-looking, the difference between guilt and remorse, "sick not bad." That's nine years of Al-Anon. The guilt/remorse bit was a clean "of course." The innocence part ("not as in blameless") is how I finally got to my father. So none of that was new.

What was new was pointing all that at Rick. I've done inventory on my resentments for years. I never put Rick on the list, because it doesn't feel like resentment. It feels like being done. Calm. Above it. The book's word for that is contempt, and I'm not going to argue.

What changes this week: I'm going to talk to Matteo. Not about Rick. About the face. I'm going to tell him I've been doing it and I'm going to try to stop, and that he doesn't have to learn it from me. Last month when he failed that chem test I felt "of course you did" coming and didn't send it, and he asked me to quiz him. First time he's asked me for help in a year. The Other Look exercise made me see that wasn't luck.

Rick, I don't know yet. The book says "even if I don't know how," and that's where I am. I tried to think of one time I held the extra back with him. I couldn't find one. He used to ask me medical questions. He stopped about three years ago. I'd like to know if he'd start again.

The last page ends on a question, not an answer. I turned it over looking for more. You said relief comes later. I'll take you at your word.

Tomás
