On the mushrooms, I was taken through my entire mind, from the surface down to the deepest layers. I saw how far the harm I had caused traveled, and I felt how each act was evil, and yet simultaneously innocent—the paradox I still find hardest to hold.

I was also shown what it was like to drift beyond my normal state: to become progressively more confused, further from the Light, trusting the voice of the darkness. I was taken the whole way, on a tour of what a mind must go through to get from the Light to pure darkness. The pain, the confusion, the insanity—it broke my heart a million times over, and then a million times again. It was the first time "compassion for evil people" made sense. People can only want that state to the degree they've forgotten what the Light is like.

I saw how terrifyingly quickly I could slide down that path myself, and how it wasn't my personal qualities that protected me. *There but for the grace of God go I*. What would stop the slide isn't becoming a "better person" in the conventional sense—fighting for the right team by hating the wrong one. Quite the opposite. It's refusing contempt in all its forms, at all times: staying firmly committed to the Light.

And I was shown my drift from outside, too. When I was closer to the Light, more people "loved" me, and the further I drifted, the more they started to hate me and feel justified shoving me deeper in, just as I had done in their shoes. Eventually I hit rock bottom. I could feel what others were telling me, because I had told it to others so many times. It was the message from the wood-chipper comments, and this time it was meant for me.

That's what the people we call evil are for. René Girard saw the scapegoat at the root of human groups; what I saw was what the scapegoat is told. The physical cruelty may be the smallest part of it. The greater part is letting them know that they're where we keep what we've disowned, that all of us know it, and that there's nothing they can do about it. That's how the road back gets cut off. (The person who hurt you is still the exception Chapter 3 made; this is about what I did.)

But a tiny handful always showed, through their eyes and their behavior, that although what I was doing was unacceptable, and they would stop it, they would never forsake me. They weren't just refusing to let me misbehave; they were refusing to let the darkness claim another soul. And it wasn't a personal decision on their part. It was the Light acting through them. I now think of them as its keepers.

That's when I realized I could be the same for others. To hold the Light for them, even (and especially) when they couldn't hold, or even see, it themselves. Truth be told, I'm still a *long* ways off from being that person, but there's clearly no greater goal in life.

Under all of it ran the background hum in me, and I saw what it had been doing the whole time. It was nudging reality, continually, into a state of forgetting: getting it to reflect otherness back to me, so that I would "know" otherness was the truth. Contempt for an enemy was the clearest case of that mechanism, the one place it can be shown step by step. That's why I wrote a book to take it apart: we believe what we can check.

The deeper layers are harder to describe, but the very deepest is the simplest. There was only a pure singularity: blinding white Light; infinite Love. It was self-evidently the Source of creation, and I was That, and so, I saw, is everyone. It's still there in me now when I look, and as best I can tell, there in you too.

That Light is the thing we're all oriented to, whether we know it or not, because it's what we are. It's Home.

If awareness were neutral between care and harm, why would harming someone require turning away from it? Why couldn’t cruelty proceed in full, undivided awareness? My own direct experience is that care belongs to awareness itself, and that's also the simplest explanation I can find. Darkness, as I read it, is what's there when awareness turns away from its own nature.

I saw how the reality I experience is a construction of my own mind—and how "my own mind" doesn't refer to meat in my skull, which is itself part of the "reality I experience."

I somehow construct all of reality, and you do too, and when I saw it, both were plainly true. I don't know how to turn that into an internally consistent metaphysics, and I now think reality is far too strange to be fit inside one. But this isn't solipsism: you are every bit as real as me (which is to say, only conventionally so).

At some point, for no discernible reason, there was a forgetting of what I was: a lapse, not a crime, and not a real departure either, since there's nowhere else to be. Along with it came the fear that it was real: that I had betrayed God, and lost my goodness, or never had it.[^course] That was unbearable. My only option was to hide, which I did by creating an *other* to blame and judge. If he's the bad one, I'm not. That process spiraled and got out of hand, creating the entire reality where I normally live, which is dominated by projection onto each other of this primal guilt. We now wander that reality, feeling forsaken at a level we rarely cognize. I've been on the lam from my "original sin" ever since. Ironically, I'm only running from my own Light.

None of this unfolds in time: the appearance of separate people at all, me here and you there, is what that first forgetting looks like from inside, and it only ever happens *now*. Even now I can see the sense in which I'm constructing time. I believe other people are also me because I saw it clearly once, and it's not the kind of thing I can simply unsee.

(Some traditions put it as not-one and not-two. Not one, because you aren't something I'm imagining; not two, because nothing tells what's looking out of you apart from what's looking out of me. When I say other people are also me, that's what I mean.)

From this perspective, we are keeping each other from returning Home because we're afraid we don't deserve it ourselves. I'm trying to get Home by the one road that can't get there: keeping others from it. Every look of contempt is a step down that road, and every step makes the separation more real in the world I experience. For me, the only way back is forgiveness, given and received, which turn out to be one act, since there was never anything to forgive but the turning. No one who was hurt owes it to anyone. This, I think, is where the idea of *original sin* comes from, and why the only solution is, indeed, to reorient to that which is most true; why only "he who is without sin" may "cast the first stone." Why "judge not, lest ye be judged." And why none of that means being a pushover, or abandoning judgment in its sense of "wise discernment."

Part of me always knew where the darkness was headed. Chapter 1's deepest layer was that I needed my enemy to carry my darkness. Under that was one more: my mind isn't separate from the world, so it was never only reaching him. Every turn added to the darkness of the whole, and a world that has forgotten the Light was what it had been after all along. That's why I see one Devil and not many. The darkness in every mind is after the same result.

Ideas that live in our collective shadow manifest "externally," taking shape in our physical reality. The ones that are most deeply buried never solidify into a single thing we can capture. They stay distributed, always lurking just out of sight, where people sense their presence but attribute different meaning to it, depending on disposition.

The most deeply buried is the sense of the *evil global conspiracy.* Our collective self-deception, the background hum, takes on a structure that, for all intents and purposes, functions exactly the way you'd expect a real one to function, but that will never quite take the shape we desperately want, where we can violently punish the people in charge. That's what I mean by the Devil.

I have met the people in charge, and they are I.

Real conspiracies exist, too. But as far as I can tell, this is the one they all ultimately depend on. Every time I weaken my own contribution to it, I make them the tiniest bit less able to function. I saw my own contribution plainly. My hatred for the people destroying the rainforest was creating a world in which the rainforest would be destroyed. All sorts of hypocrisies were laid bare, from hating Amazon.com while constantly relying on it, to hating shadowy government cabals that my first-world luxuries probably rely on. I still haven't cleaned up my act, so make of that what you will.

I saw why hiding stuff from myself could never work. Part of me always saw that I was doing it, and that part was essentially God, and God is reality. So knowledge of my wrongdoing always takes physical shape in my "external" world. (That world is yours as much as mine, made by all of us at once. So nothing that happens to anyone, an illness, a loss, what someone else did to them, is anyone else's to read as a sign of what they did. This is about the harm I do.)

The feeling of *getting away with something* now elicits a laugh—which is not to say that I never try. It's a long learning process.

My mind sometimes wants to object; to believe that there's no universal orientation I'm bound by. The way I evaluate it now is to look, very closely, at whether some part of me knows I am deceiving myself. When the answer is yes, I can already see the pain it will cause and how I will eventually have to own it, in this life or whatever comes after.

As best I can tell, all the harm I've caused through my self-deception still lives in the world somewhere, waiting for me to clean it up. It got there via chains of causality I'm normally incapable of recognizing (and perhaps am not meant to, while still lost in the "dream"). And the surest sign it's still out there is that I still contemptuously judge others—which I still do, a lot.

The same holds the other way. No act of love done when nobody is watching is lost; it goes out by the same chains. There have been times I was about to break, about to give up on the part of me that knows, and something held, just enough. I've come to believe that some of those times, what held was someone I'll never meet, doing something decent with nobody watching. I like to tell myself that every time we do this, we give the Devil the middle finger.

What let me see any of this was that I was held in the Light the whole time I was being shown. There is still plenty in my mind that would elicit shame if it weren't held properly.

I also saw, unmistakably, that I'll be back in that place one day, facing no one but myself. It's not a process that unfolds in time, so in a sense, that "one day" is already here and now. If I want to know how I'll fare when "judgment day" comes, all I have to do is face myself with full honesty right now. Which is all too easy to avoid when my belly is full and the sun's shining on my face. All the ways I distract myself are ways of avoiding *that*.

I can't imagine that a framework like this one will ever be popular with most people, even if it turns out to be true. But I do believe that certain aspects of it, most importantly the universality of Good and the recognition of evil as its absence, will be necessary for our species to make progress toward the kind of world most of us long for. Whether that best comes from religion, spirituality, science, or some mix I cannot say. But I hope it comes soon—and I find that the more firmly I orient toward the Light myself, the more real that possibility looks.

It is my intention to always stay oriented; to remain turned-toward. I hope you will hold my feet to the fire when I drift.
