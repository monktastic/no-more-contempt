Defining goodness

---

Say I'm an oil executive who's been harshly criticized for deceiving the public—in one case, with my dignity intact; in another, with contempt. Later, a proposal crosses my desk that will benefit me while potentially degrading a sensitive ecosystem. There's no conclusive proof that it will, and my PR team has provided ample counter-evidence.

How am I likely to choose in each case?

If I'm honest, I'm more likely to license myself to act out of self-interest after the contempt—because *fuck those self-righteous hypocrites who use my product and then throw me under the bus for it*. I don't tell myself I'm using that as an excuse to cause harm. The counter-evidence just feels a little more convincing. I give one side of a genuinely uncertain question slightly more weight, and feel I've made a reasonable decision. No one can prove why I tipped the scales—not even me.

My choice remains mine. Their contempt didn't force it. But it did make my own turning away feel more natural, and others will suffer as a consequence. Will my contemptuous critics factor in that longer-term outcome? No. They'll pat themselves on the back for a job well done: a liar exposed.

And yet, I think, part of them can sense that's not the whole story.

When I'm in their shoes, my conscience is warning me about *something.* I don't know what—whether an ecosystem will suffer, or my target will just take it out on his wife and kids. So I tell myself the warning is a lie. I mean, how could it possibly know?

By turning away from myself, I turn away from the full implications of my behavior—and that acts as an invitation for my target to do the same, which helps those very implications come to pass.

---

Turning away from myself, from my better nature, from the life in others, and from the full implications of my behavior—they're all facets of a single move. It is that move that we recognize as *evil*.

When those implications come to pass, I see a darker world that has no connection to anything I've done. And what do I feel for such a world? Well, what do *you* feel for the people engaged in corruption, lies, self-serving behavior, etc.? When you see that governments and corporations are cheating you, do you feel a pull toward integrity or contempt?

I'll be honest: it lets me feel like I can get away with more—which others then sense, licensing them to do the same, which I can then judge them for, because *I had valid reasons*. "Evil" may be too strong a word for what I'm doing, but it is the same basic thing.

Cutting in line, cheating a customer, selling pharmaceuticals that I know causes addiction, genuine atrocities: they all require the same basic *turning away*, and each gives license to people to engage in whichever version best suits their personality.

That's why my contempt felt *malevolent*. There is no "good kind" of darkness, because it is that which devours life; that which extinguishes light from the world. Contempt for any one being is contempt for *life in general.* 

In the very worst people, we find something like *delight in devouring innocence*. It's an extreme form of the same essence, not a different kind of thing.



---

When I measure outcomes, I get to choose where to draw the boundary. The effects on other people's minds, and thus on what they do later, are among the hardest to count—and yet they can be of far greater consequence than what I *do* measure. This, I've found, is where my darkness has the greatest opportunity to inject itself into the world. 

Imagine a politician lying his country into war for profit while encouraging dehumanization of the enemy. Beyond the physical damage, the people on the other end are being taught the same lesson I taught my enemy: *they're evil*. They feel morally compelled to retaliate, with less concern for what lines they cross. He then points to that as proof that he was right, justifying more war—and more profit.

Does he know he's doing this? Yes and no: he "honestly" believes his reasons (some of which may be entirely valid) while another part of him shapes them to get what it wants. Another politician might well have fought the same war without spreading that contempt, likely producing less corruption in all minds involved, and thus less long-term harm.

I have the same choice when I'm his supporter: if his lies benefit me, I stop asking what it costs others. I can point to its real benefits, downplaying the invisible harms.

*Every* project and agenda has this potential. I can start a righteous cause and imbue it with contempt, providing me with an endless supply of enemies to fight. I can create a company that provably accomplishes good by our chosen metrics, but whose existence is rooted in my self-aggrandizement. The people who join me can sense it, and it subtly influences how they hold themselves—whether they're serving themselves or something greater. That then leaks out into the world via countless invisible channels, making it worse in ways that "require" me to assert even more control.
