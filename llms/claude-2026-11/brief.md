People often come back from psychedelics saying that love is the most important thing in the world, that it's the right response to literally every situation. But how can that be? Are we supposed to love even *evil people?*

What would that even mean?

---

Part of me needs to know I'm better than my enemies, that they're defective in a way I'm not. But this doesn't feel like a need. It just feels like the truth. So I feel justified treating them with contempt. When they dig in, I take it as proof I was right about them all along.

Is their reaction just coincidentally helpful, or might it be the *purpose* of my contempt? Here's what I find.

Contempt says *you're a lesser kind of thing, so whatever I do to you doesn't count as harm.* I can get that across with my tone, gaze, and manner, even if every word I say is about what they did wrong.

At some level, I know I'm injecting something dark that doesn't need to be there. I've been confronted without contempt—*fiercely*—and it was harder to dodge what those people were saying. So what's the extra for? It's not just to deter others, since I'd enjoy it even if nobody else were around.

Something in me **delights** in making bad people suffer, *over and above* any good it might do, any justice it might serve. But for that delight to feel okay (and not *evil*), I need to know they're truly beyond help. I would feel sick to my stomach if I found out they still had the capacity for good, so I must rule out that possibility.

So I flash my enemy this *delight in gratuitous harm*—evil—knowing he'll recognize it for what it is. I'm revealing to him that I know we're the same kind of thing, while claiming we're not, because the maddening hypocrisy will make him embrace his own darkness, yet without leaving an audit trail for what I've done. The lack of evidence is part of the taunt. I'm letting him know I'll get away with it. *I was only fighting evil, you see! How could that ever be wrong?*

He digs in, in response to my "righteous" behavior. I feel a brief flicker of relief or satisfaction, just before I relabel it disgust or dismay. *Ugh, I knew it. People are even worse than I thought.* And there's only *one way* to deal with people like that...

If he had instead owned what he'd done and begun to make amends, there's a tiny part of me that would have felt *cheated.* I hate to admit it, but it's there. It's the same part I secretly reveal to him, nudging him toward becoming what it needs him to be. The same thing in him does it right back. Neither of us can admit it.

Notice what seems like a contradiction: on one hand, I say I'm *knowingly* doing this; but on the other, I must be unaware of my own role in it—because how could I sincerely hold against him evidence that I know I planted?

It turns out not to be a contradiction at all, but the crux of the insight.

---

Have you ever done something you knew was wrong at the time, but couldn't fully admit it to yourself until later? Maybe you took credit for someone else's work, said something cruel to someone who'd done nothing to you, or cheated on a test (or even a partner).

Recall what it was like to finally own up to it. The knowledge that it was wrong: did it feel like new information? Or like something you had always known, but somehow hadn't *let* yourself know? That knowledge was hidden, but not unconscious. It was still there in your field of consciousness; you just weren't looking at it. You could say you were **conscious** of it without being **aware** of it, and acting on it all the same.

When someone came close to exposing it, could you sense where their question might lead? Did you know what you could say to get them off the subject? Say you took credit for a colleague's idea. Someone asks how you came up with it. *We talked it over yesterday*, you say. It might be perfectly true. But you choose that answer because it lets them go on thinking it was yours, all while telling yourself you're just explaining how the work got done. That's what I mean by *conscious and intentional, but unaware*.

All evil *is* is our unwillingness to face ourselves; our ability to hide things from our own minds. What the "evil person" hides is that *my victim is just like me.* If he felt the full weight of that recognition, he simply couldn't do it. His victim must be *fundamentally other.* It's the same confused state I must enter to treat him with contempt.

If you've ever been on the receiving end of a dehumanizing gaze, you know what this is like. Did the other person seem somehow *absent* or *turned away*? That's what you were sensing. It's the same thing we reveal to them when we feel contempt.

And what are we turned away *from*? From the deepest part of ourselves; the part that would never let us gratuitously harm others, because it literally *sees* ourselves in them. That seeing is hidden the same way: still there, just not looked at.

Go back to the man I'm confronting. He accuses me of using a nasty tone. *He's just tone policing me*, I tell myself. If that won't do, *Fine, I'm being a bit cruel. But I have to be, with someone like him.* My explanation changes as he reacts. My verdict stays put. I'm conscious enough to steer around his reactions. But if I let myself see how I'm provoking them, I couldn't take it as proof of what he'd been all along.

I'd like to call this behavior "unconscious." No one can blame me for what I couldn't see. My enemies know exactly what they're doing. When I do it (if I even do), it's an accident, or I couldn't have known. That's why they deserve contempt while I deserve understanding.

This asymmetry is what drives the whole process. My darkness is hidden from me but revealed to him, enticing him to attack me for it, not realizing what he's actually doing. Except that part of him *does* realize it, because that's *his* darkness revealing itself to me, making me feel righteous in doing it right back.

Imagine I treat you with contempt. How can you tell what I'm doing? It's because you recognize the move from the inside, I'm counting on you to recognize it, and in my look you can see that I am. I know you know what it is to turn away, you know I know it, and so on. My look depends on that common knowledge. I'm showing you that I know better and that I won't let myself know. What I have to turn away from in myself is the very thing I'm reaching for in you. **I’m making our sameness felt so you can feel me betray it.**

---

Contempt often runs cold. I write the person off. There's a quiet filing away, a politeness with nothing behind it, and a small satisfaction at being done with them. I want them to feel my verdict, or I want whoever's watching to see it.

And it needn't be about right and wrong. Someone slow, ignorant, or clumsy can get the same verdict—*you're a lesser kind of thing*—and I call it "just being honest." I may even be right about them. But what my honest assessment never needed was the eye-roll, or being above them.

Most of my contempt is quiet: a vague sense of *well, I could never do that*. It doesn't feel like contempt at all, and it can happen a thousand times a day without my noticing. I even send it to people I love. The *what's wrong with you?* that needs them to know they're broken. The sigh that tells them what I really think of them, dressed up to make me seem like the reasonable one. The look on their face that tells me it landed, which I pretend not to have seen. For one second the verdict flashes through my eyes, and I make sure they see it, and then I go back to loving them as if nothing had happened.

The most subtle contempt I still harbor is the one for people who don't get it yet—the ones who still hold others in contempt. *They know exactly what they're doing,* I tell myself. Or pity, which passes for compassion and is contempt with a soft face. Or equanimity, when it's a way of filing someone under _deluded_ and feeling nothing more.

As long as I hold even a trace of it for anyone, for any reason, my behavior is secretly spreading evil through channels I refuse to see.

Nothing changes until I become aware of my own capacity for darkness. It hides in the very last place I'd ever look: my conviction that I'm one of the good ones, valiantly fighting the bad guys. And what lets me confront my own darkness without collapsing into shame? It's remembering the part of myself that is deeper than it; more fundamental.

A teacher once caught me stealing. His look was fierce, and I braced for the verdict on my being, and it never came. He was standing for something and searching my face for some sign that I knew it too. That made me look for it in myself. I knew I couldn't win against it, and more importantly, I didn't want to. In that moment, any differences about politics, or religion, or other human-invented rules wouldn't have mattered one whit.

His look said *I know you're in there.* Contempt looks right at that same somebody and says *there's nobody in there*—and often exactly to the person who most needs to remember that there *is*. What my teacher revealed was the opposite of the darkness. Call it the *light*.

When you finally owned what you'd done, did it feel like coming back to yourself, even if admitting it cost you something?

---

Being held in the light was what first let me see the evil in my contempt. That I'd been looking directly at the part of my enemy that I knew could be rescued, and choosing to smother it, knowing I'd get away with it. In a flash, I saw how people can commit great evil while feeling okay with it. I'd always known the move from the inside, and I'd been taunting them with it. I had to do that so I could go on believing I was a different kind of person. For me, it was psychedelics that allowed this all to surface. I'm not sure a piece like this would have revealed it to me.

I can only choose darkness when I've lost sight of the light. Then goodness looks like something nice or soft that won't hold up in the world I live in. That's what my contempt invites my enemy to believe. *If even the "good people" do this and get away with it, then what does goodness even mean? Why should I be the only one playing it straight?*

Thinking back on the wrong you found it hard to admit: did some part of you feel you *deserved* something? That everyone does it, and only a fool plays it straight?

That's what I'm teaching him: that "goodness" just means whatever we can get away with; what we can prove—and right now I'm proving that I'm the righteous one.

Contempt is only coherent because we share a standard for goodness. Not its details, of course—we may never agree on those—but how to *orient* to it. That it's about not turning away from the part of ourselves that knows better; that sees ourselves in others. I needed my enemy to know what I was betraying—and that it's something we both served—before I could make him feel like a fool for honoring it. 

Think of the worst person you can imagine. Can you visualize them lying awake at night, wracked with remorse over having been decent; having gone against their evil nature? Perhaps they lack a "better nature," but I doubt it's ever reversed. And even the belief that they don't have one: I think it's just an excuse to let me feel contempt.

The darkness is like an infection that spreads. It is what robs the world of light. As far as I can tell, the only way to stop the spread is to always remember that there is a light to orient to, and to remain oriented to it at all costs.

I think we're all at different stages of remembering. The more I remember, the more I help you remember, and vice versa. And the more I remember, the more I can literally see it in you, instead of having to believe a nice-sounding idea. And I think this is what love means: not some sappy idea layered over top of my other beliefs, but simply how I behave when I'm fully in touch with my deeper nature. It is what's there prior to our self-deception.

So all I ask of you is that you remind me when I forget. Not by being *nice*, but by being kind, however fierce that kindness must be. By staying oriented. And all I can offer you is to do the same.
