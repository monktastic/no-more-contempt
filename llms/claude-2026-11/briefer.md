Say you and I are enemies. I think you're a bad person—not just that you hold bad ideas or do bad things, but that you *are* bad. So I treat you with contempt. My words say I'm just holding you accountable, but my tone and gaze convey something much darker. They say *you're a lesser kind of thing*, the implication being that *whatever I do to you doesn't count as harm.*

What do you feel toward me in that moment?

Probably exactly what I want you to feel: that *I'm* bad. You can sense that what I'm doing is underhanded, and feel it's your moral duty to resist me, right? But you already know I'll use your reaction against you, by retreating to my righteous words: *I was just holding you to account. What kind of person reacts poorly to that*? But if you name *that*, I've got an answer prepared too: *of course you'd think that.*

I am provoking you into seeing me as bad so that I can use your reaction against you. As far as I know, I really am just holding you to account. But this seems contradictory: if I'm aware that I'm provoking you, how can I sincerely hold it against you? And if I'm not, then in what sense can it be intentional like I'm suggesting?

I do it by *compartmentalizing*. I know I'm provoking you, but I don't *let* myself know. I am conscious of what I'm doing, but *turned away* from it, so that it feels unconscious. You could also call this state *conscious but unaware.* Here's a way of pointing at that state.

Have you ever done something you knew was wrong at the time, but couldn't fully admit it to yourself until later? When you finally admitted it, did it feel like new knowledge, or like something you had always known but didn't let yourself know? Any time someone came close to exposing it, did you find a way to deflect? Did some part of you know you were?

This is how any of us does wrong while feeling okay about it: by not letting ourselves feel the full weight of what we're doing. Have you ever been on the receiving end of a dehumanizing gaze? Could you feel the sense in which the other person was somehow absent or turned away from themselves? I think that's what you're looking at.

I know this, but don't let myself know. It's a horrifying thought, because it means my enemies aren't fundamentally different from me: they're just like me. That's why I refuse to let myself see it.

And I know that on some level, *you* know that this is how it works, too. I'm relying on you knowing it, or else you wouldn't be able to read my tone and gaze for what they are: proof that I'm doing it to you right now. Contempt is me showing you that I refuse to let myself see we're the same, and relying on you to recognize it, so that you'll dig in, "proving" that we're not the same. And I won’t let myself see that I’m baiting you, either, but I need you to see that I am (and that I won't let myself see it). And I want you to see I’ve already got an answer ready if you try to name it. _Of course you’d think that._ Every attempt to step outside this hall of mirrors just puts you deeper into it—which is why you dig in.

For any of this to work, it had to already be common knowledge that turning away means we're causing harm, and that we can *only* cause harm while turned away.

If you didn't already know it, you wouldn't be able to recognize my look for what it is. If I didn't *know* you knew it, there would be no point in sending it. If you didn't know *that*, you wouldn't feel incensed by my blatant hypocrisy. And so on. If that recursion stopped at any level, you could still think you'd caught *me* out. But my look says, *Nope, I'm one step ahead: just try naming it and see what happens.*

The standard I’m holding you to is the one I’m making sure you see me betray.

I can't let myself know that I'm doing this because I've been doing it for so long. I've been driving my enemies deeper into the dark, where I can then take pleasure in their suffering, knowing that I'm only harming people who "deserve" it. That's *evil*.

This explains why I must keep doing it, ever-harder. If I ever let up and they got better, I would have to face what I've been doing, and that's unthinkable. But why did I start in the first place? 

I'll give you my metaphysical answer.

Suppose it's really true that we are all one consciousness, looking out through separate eyes. By the self-reflective nature of consciousness, some part of me knows it, and you do too. By oneness, it also means that we each know the other knows it, and that they know that, and so on—not necessarily at the level we'd normally call "conscious," but *somewhere*.

This, I think, is what "conscious but unaware" is pointing to. It is why, in certain mystical states, there is often a forehead-smacking sense of "of course, I've always known this!" 

Now look at what this entails.

I can never make that knowledge truly vanish from consciousness; I can only turn away from it. When I do, I see you as fundamentally *other*, even as part of me knows it's a lie. I cannot (maliciously) harm you without turning away, and I can *only* harm you while turned away. And all the above is *common knowledge* too—something we each know the other knows, etc.

In other words, it provides a basis for contempt to work the way it does. When I'm unable to see our unity, I cannot help but act in ways that create more evidence against it. And when I can see it, I do the opposite: I hold others to account without delivering that verdict on their being, which at least gives them the chance to remember what we are instead of forgetting it further. That's called *compassion.* It's the natural result of seeing the truth, and can *only* be done while seeing it. All else is only an approximation at best.

So why did I start *turning away* in the first place? Here's where it gets even more tentative and removed from everyday experience. All I can offer is that I've seen it clearly myself. (The version I offer comes from Kenneth Wapnick's interpretation of *A Course In Miracles*.)

In the beginning—which wasn't long ago, but *right now*, as the process I'm about to describe is what creates the illusion of time—I was one with God / Love / Reality. But there arose a "tiny, mad idea" that I could be separated from It. I took it seriously, and thereby projected the illusion that I had, in fact, separated. Fearing I had committed the gravest of sins (rejecting God), I felt I had to hide. I did so by creating a world of *others* to judge, continually projecting my primal guilt onto them. The more I did this, the worse it got—until eventually I'd had enough. I realized how foolish it was to think that I could hide from All That Was. So I finally gave up.

It was the most painful thing that ever was, or could ever be. I was forced to confront "lifetimes" of transgressions—all the things I had ever held against anyone, because I *was* them—until finally, and gloriously, I remembered the truth that could never be forgotten: I had always been Home, and belief in my departure had been an innocent mistake. That's all evil *was.*

Alas, as best I can tell, returning Home once does not mean we stay there. We return to the illusory world of our creation, where we begin the long, hard, journey of cleaning up all we had seen; all that we had made. What does this look like in practice?

It means taking full responsibility for all my projections. Refusing to see "others" as other. Holding the whole world in Love—not as some sappy emotion, or by being soft; but by acting from the deepest of integrity at all times, whether anyone is "watching" or not.

Because "someone" always is—and it is I.

I can only forsake you when I forget this: that I am only ever forsaking myself.
