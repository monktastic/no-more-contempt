# Cold reads of general-audience-abductive-argument.md (essay-draft.md) (16 Sept 2026)

Three readers with no context beyond the file, each with an adversarial brief. Saved verbatim.

## 1. Analytic philosopher (hunting overclaims)

**1. The three worst overclaims**

*(a)* "The piece works by showing you that your behavior is only coherent if the above claims are true." That is a necessity claim. What the page delivers is a guided introspection followed by an inference to the best explanation in which five rivals are each dispatched in one sentence. An abduction yields "best available explanation," not "only coherent account." A careful author: "I'll argue these claims are the simplest account of some things you can check for yourself."

*(b)* "Kindness is its opposite, and the only thing that's good without exception." Nothing addresses honesty, courage, justice, or attention as candidates; nothing addresses the case where "keeping the person in view" is exactly what enables the manipulator. Section 7 adds "Kindness isn't wisdom," so it is not "good without exception." A careful author: "I'll claim only that this stance never itself feeds the thing."

*(c)* "Everyone knows this. It's why 'he did it while looking me in the eye' is the most damning thing you can say about someone: it means he had to look *away* from you while looking *at* you." The idiom means the opposite of what the essay needs: he *did* see the victim and did it anyway. The gloss is the thesis inserted into the evidence.

Runner-up: "The most lost person is carrying *more* of that knowledge... It's arithmetic." There is no arithmetic. "Tracks, exactly" is unfalsifiable.

**2. Strongest objection to section 5.** The seven "pieces" are already explained by (i) several motives including status, belonging, dominance, and a retributive instinct that is a motive in its own right; (ii) moral cognition as a signaling system, so punishing must look justified to the group and to oneself; (iii) theory of mind, so everyone can tell when a look demotes them. None of this needs "one thing in both of you that registers another person as mattering." From "wrongdoing borrows the knowing" you cannot get "there is no second thing that wants harm"; a predator borrows its prey's visibility without being an absence. The essay's rebuttals ("Cruelty doesn't need the hurt to feel like justice"; "a double standard doesn't explain why the message lands") are definitions chosen so the objection fails. The "isn't a circle" paragraph relabels: claim 1 contains "and you know it," which is what claim 2 asserts.

**3. Counterexamples waved off.** The sadist (needs the victim's mattering; the essay's own account of contempt describes someone who sees the target very well). The negligent or institutional harm-doer (no limbo; never looked). The scrupulous or depressed reader, for whom "You never read your own wrongs as proof of what you are" is false; self-contempt is absent.

**4. Cornered/lectured, quoted.** The shadow-work caveat (Kafka trap, in the intro). "Notice that some part of you wants that to land... Can you feel that?" "somewhere you knew that too." "notice how fast *well, who can really say?* arrives... That shrug is the hiding." "Both of you know all of this." "You'll notice how much you want there to be one. That wanting is the whole subject of this piece." "Everything in Part I was checked in your own case." "Three things, and you've now seen each of them from inside." The last sentence of Part II: even declining is a symptom. "That's how it went for them." The pill passage is the exception: it includes the author and doesn't corner.

**5. Machine-made or jargon.** "in recursive form"; "one level up"; "no equipment of its own"; "limbo, aimed at a person"; "two lookings-away have nothing in them to differ by"; "shadow work"; "orientation"; "identification"; "the securing"; "a stance"; "holdable"; "the thing" as a proper noun; six "Not X. Y." fragments; "Here's the part that makes it work" and other paragraph announcements; "tracks, exactly"; "It's arithmetic"; "precisely"; "shook hands underneath the two of you."

**6. Verdict.** A skeptical reader finishes Part I, stalls at section 5 where the essay claims to have shown what it assumed, leaves Part II early. Afterward: a real phenomenon, closely observed, but the essay confuses "I can get you to notice this" with "this is the only account," treats every objection as evidence for the thesis, and wraps a modest psychological claim in a metaphysical one it neither needs nor earns. Cut the necessity claim, drop "only" from kindness, take the sadist and the bureaucrat seriously, stop diagnosing the reader's doubts.

## 2. Moral psychologist (checking the empirical claims)

**1. Claims false, exaggerated, or true only sometimes.**
- "Shaming people doesn't make them sorry. It makes them dig in, and worse." Half true. Tangney and Dearing support the direction (shame predicts defensiveness; guilt predicts repair). But reintegrative shaming, restorative justice, and everyday experience show shame producing apology, especially when the relationship is valued. The essay relies on this later with the "harsher" second person.
- "They could only have done it by looking away from the person they hurt." False as a universal: sadism depends on registering the victim's suffering as a person's (Baumeister; Hare, Blair). Instrumental violence under duress, motivated ignorance, sincere mistaken belief don't fit. The rescue ("look away while looking at you") is a definitional stretch.
- "Did you choose to look away while still looking? There's no such moment." One common sequence, not the only one (Ainslie; Baumeister on self-regulation failure).
- "Is there a flicker... There is in me." Retributive impulse is widespread (Carlsmith, Darley, Robinson) but varies; some honestly answer "the pill, no flicker." "Notice how much you want there to be one" makes absence itself suspect: unfalsifiable by construction.
- "And they can see it... that you've arranged not to know it." Weakest claim. Targets detect hostility and status demotion; they do not reliably see the sender's self-deception (illusion of transparency; Epley on mind-perception in hostile encounters). A target's certainty that "you know what you're doing" is itself a hostile attribution.
- "You use the reaction you caused as evidence." True often (Snyder and Swann); essay says "you did," not "people often do."
- "It gets stronger, not weaker, the closer you came." Sometimes; self-affirmation work shows defensiveness dropping when threat drops.
- "The most lost person is carrying more of that knowledge." No evidence; psychopathy shows reduced, not suppressed, affective response.
- "You never read your own wrongs as proof of what you are." False for shame-prone and depressed people.

**2. Strongest rival.** Moral emotions as status-regulation and coalition-management devices (Rozin, Haidt, CAD; Fiske; Gervais and Fessler). Contempt tracks hierarchy and exclusion; the pleasure in their digging in is a coalition confirmed; righteousness is reputation management (Kunda; Mercier and Sperber); self-deception is adaptive (Trivers, von Hippel): no prior knowing suppressed, only a belief never formed because forming it was costly. Every item on the section 5 list is covered. Section 5's treatment of rivals is not fair: one sentence and a rhetorical question each; "same behavior" confused with "same underlying architecture."

**3. What it gets right.** Act-versus-person condemnation maps onto guilt/shame, and the prediction holds. Punitive satisfaction exceeds deterrence (Carlsmith). Behavioral confirmation is real and "you made the bed" is accurate. The two-people contrast is a good description of what makes correction land. Reasons after decision is Haidt's model. Contagion across a dyad scaling to intergroup conflict is standard conflict-spiral theory. Downward-comparison self-esteem needs the target to stay down. Implicit moral competence outruns articulation: right and underappreciated.

**4. "You already knew."** Partly fair (implicit moral knowledge; felt sense on confession, though partly hindsight bias). The trick is scope: used for (a) knew the act was wrong (often true), (b) knew the target was redeemable (theory smuggled in as memory), (c) knew contempt makes people worse (an empirical generalization the reader may never have held). Only (a) is candidate implicit knowledge. Section 8's "You'll notice how much you want there to be one" pre-diagnoses every disagreeing reader: a closed system.

**5. Verdict.** An unfalsifiable theory dressed as introspective evidence. "Check this in your own case" functions as a demand characteristic. The one testable prediction (shaming never produces remorse) is false in universal form and self-contradicted. The central claim is contradicted by sadism, psychopathy, and ordinary instrumental harm, and the response is to redefine "looking." What survives is a well-written restatement of guilt/shame, social intuitionism, behavioral confirmation, and retributive surplus, in a framework that adds no predictions and immunizes itself.

## 3. Ordinary skeptical reader (a lawyer; despises a senator)

**1. First "oh come on."** Intro: "And you already knew that, too." First "not true of me": "The side that argues is the side with something to get past." I argue for a living. The one that stopped me: "You had to, because you couldn't have done it while looking." I ran the exercise, picked rounding up a time entry, held it in view, and could do it. Section 4 then cites it as settled. Flatly rejected: "you do it because it makes them worse." With the senator I want him gone, not worse.

**2. Tab-closer.** The shadow-work caveat: "That's an insulation clause... 'Shadow work' is the exact vocabulary I'd have expected from a piece a friend sends me that I then don't read." Runner-up: "Mara. The Devil. They were seeing something real."

**3. Confused.** "Recursive form." "The two claims that seemed to chase each other's tail" (never mentioned before). "The flash" (no flash mentioned; and "he" appears mid-passage). "Trying is the securing." "Is anything missing that you'd need to add?" "The fear underneath it" (fear never introduced). "Two lookings-away have nothing in them to differ by" (two holes in different walls are different holes). Sections 3 and 13 contradict on knowing shrinking/growing. "Everything in Part I was checked in your own case" is not true of section 7.

**4. Exercises.** Recall (section 1): worked; had one ready; "That question is the best thing in the piece, and it's asked and then the essay stops, which is why it worked." Picturing the senator: half; the "something darker" line landed, but the provoke-and-cite model assumes two people in a room; "The senator doesn't know I exist." The pill: "this one got me... I'd have sworn I wanted the senator to stop, and the pill offer exposed that I also want him to hurt." Works because it gives a choice and admits the author's own answer. Two people who caught you: half; real, but "the passage narrates my reaction to both." Staged: the locker room ("nobody says 'you're all disgusting'"); the section 2 exercise, which fails for small wrongs and makes the reader distrust the essay.

**5. Talking down, quoted.** "Everyone knows this." "That's how it went for them." "That's a heavier standard than the one you were using." "Notice that some part of you wants that to land. To register, deep inside them. Can you feel that?" "You knew that before you sent it." "and somewhere you knew that too." "notice how fast well, who can really say? arrives" (scripts my reply, then names it as hiding). "Both of you know all of this." "You've been on the receiving end of the difference." "Three things, and you've now seen each of them from inside." "You'll notice how much you want there to be one." "the moment you hold it, your own looking-away has control of you." "the urge to do it comes from yours." The pattern: best when it asks and stops; worst when it asks and answers in the next clause. Section 4 does the second thing about a dozen times.

**6. To the friend.** "The middle is better than the wrapper. Cut the intro entirely." Sections 1, the pill, and the two-people passage are genuinely good and one caught me out. The hole: the whole account is two people in a room, and the contempt I actually carry is for people who will never see my face. Around section 4 it felt like being cross-examined by someone who'd already written the verdict. Part II: skip, once it gets to Mara it's asking me to sign something. Would send sections 1 through 6 to my brother, who does the "what's wrong with you" sigh to his kids. Not with the intro attached.

## 4. Second cold read, after the first round of fixes (editor with a philosophy degree; despises a cable-news host at a distance)

**1. Sentences that report the reader's finding, or treat disagreement as a symptom.** Exhaustive list of about forty, including: "Did that happen by itself? No: that behavior took place in limbo, too." "So here is what your own case shows, and none of it is theory." "And you can see this now. The part that knew is the part reading this sentence." "Did you choose to look away while still looking? There's no such moment." "So notice how it feels when they do. Like relief." "Listen to what you tell yourself about it: they made their bed..." "(If you don't, if you're the type who reads them as proof, that's the same move aimed inward.)" "Which one do you remember, and what did you do afterward? With the second, most of us don't dig in." "If you find you want there to be one, that wanting is worth a look, because it's the same wanting this piece has been about." The whole closing run ("You knew equal standing well enough..."). The four sentences that do it right: "See whether it arrives for you." "There is in me." "I can only ask whether it matches your own case." "If it went that way for you, that's the best reason you have to think it went that way for them." "The rest is the method described and then not used."

**2. Unearned universals.** "The source of all wrongdoing" (intro) versus section 5's "best explanation I can find, and not a proof." "To do wrong, you must hide." "It never lived in a clear-eyed moment... because there wasn't one." "Most of the harm in the world runs on it." "There is nothing more satisfying than punishing evil." "The darkness has no equipment of its own" (the abduction's conclusion stated as fact). "Every calculation you'll ever do..." "Any rule can be followed while turned away." "Kindness... the one stance that never itself feeds the thing": said three times, argued zero. "Every tradition that looked hard ended up naming one adversary." "Two lookings-away have nothing in them to differ by": two absences differ by what they're absences of.

**3. Exercises that failed when run honestly.**
- Recall: cheating on an exam at nineteen. "It did not sit in limbo. I knew it was wrong, weighed the risk, and did it because I wanted the grade more than I minded the wrong." A third option the piece doesn't offer: known, weighed, overridden by wanting.
- The padded invoice: "The exercise stipulates its result... If I hold them as mattering and strip the justification, of course I can't. The question is whether I can hold them as mattering while wanting the money." Run without the stipulation: could pad a large company's invoice while picturing the clerk, and feel a little bad.
- The sadist test: section 2's test was "in full view, as somebody who matters"; section 3 quietly changes it to "wanting them to be all right," a different and easier bar to fail.
- The pill: worked, partially. Relief, plus "a small, distinct 'but he should pay.'" But "that flicker is after something that justice doesn't need" is a contested position on punishment presented as what the flicker reveals.
- Two people: "Run honestly, it fails. The person I remember is the first kind... and I did not dig in. I was ashamed for a year and stopped doing the thing." Also "Almost the same words" contradicts "Says more, in front of more people."
- Section 7 check: "Honest answer: it's something I'm doing. Turning toward someone I dislike is effortful, and it lapses when I stop."

**4. The distance case.** "The piece's engine, the rigged test in section 4... doesn't run at a distance." The distance paragraph replaces the engine with an uncheckable transmission claim and a one-line test, which the reader passes (relief, small flicker). "So by the piece's own definition... most of what I feel toward him may not be contempt. That leaves the headline claim with no path to my case: he isn't made worse by me." The man is also closer to the cold form, which section 3 sets aside; and section 13's "conscience under crushing load" is unfalsifiable for a cynical operator, admitted as the picture talking, then used for the most important practical conclusion. Not covered: the reader who wants the person stopped, judged, and punished, in full view, and reads punishment as owed rather than enjoyed.

**5. Unparseable or no antecedent.** "He needs their personhood the way contempt needs the part of them that knows fairness" (section 3 forward-references section 4). "For the reason section 2 found" (doesn't follow: open malice has been sent by plenty of people). "It has to be this way." "You felt it go by. He needed you to" (which "it"; motive from nowhere). "You've watched what holding it does to you" (section 4 is about what it does to them). "There's more harm for it to register" (the knowing becomes a harm-counter). "Equal standing" first appears in the closing paragraph. Kindness defined by a need blurs the one distinction. Darkness defined as an absence in Part I, then given purposes in Part II, with no statement of which definition Part I rests on.

**6. Tics.** "Not X. Y." / "It isn't X. It's Y." at least a dozen times. Paragraph announcements ("Put the pieces on the table," "Stay with that," "Follow that one step further," "Watch it at scale"). Precision words with nothing measured ("precisely," "exactly"). Superlatives doing the work of argument. Composed-sounding lists of three and four. "Held up to a window" is the one image to keep.

**7. Verdict.** "Not as it stands. The essay's whole claim to legitimacy is its method... Then it reports the result of every check itself... By its own account in section 4, that is the rigged test: set it up so the reader can't come out anywhere else, then cite where they came out." The one change: "strip every sentence that states what the reader found, felt, or will feel, and leave the question standing. Where the author wants to say what he found, say it as his." That cut shortens the piece by a fifth and exposes the second problem: "the reader whose contempt is at a distance and passes the pill test finds the argument has quietly stopped being about them. The distance paragraph in section 4 needs to be a section, not a concession, or the intro's 'all wrongdoing' needs to come down to what the body actually earns."
