# Novelty and Risk Audit

*This is not a statement of the book's argument — `argument.md` does that, and does it better, in the book's own voice. This document answers three different questions: what in the book is actually new, how much weight each claim carries, and where the book is at risk of overclaiming. Read that one to know what the book says. Read this one to know what to defend.*

## The one sentence

**Contempt is not a response to evil. It is evil, and it spreads by recruiting the person who feels it.**

And the consequence that gives the book its title and its purpose: **the only thing that turns anyone toward what they already know is a person who is turned toward themselves — which makes your own turning-toward the only lever, necessary but not sufficient.**

The first sentence is the identification. The second is what to do about it. The Devil material is the first sentence at full scale; it's the biggest claim, not the thesis.

## What's already out there, so you know what you're not claiming

- Self-deception as motivated not-looking (Fingarette, Sartre's bad faith, Trivers).
- Projection and the shadow (Jung).
- Shame-versus-guilt and stigmatizing-versus-reintegrative shaming (Tangney, Braithwaite).
- The scapegoat as social glue (Girard).
- Contempt as the most corrosive relational emotion (Gottman).
- Moral disengagement as a catalog of rationalizations (Bandura).
- Nonviolence as strategy (King, Gandhi, Chenoweth).
- Evil as privation (Augustine); the ego as the belief in separation (ACIM).

None of these is the book. The book's contribution is the *mechanism* that connects them, and a handful of claims I have not seen made anywhere.

## The differentiating theses, in order of how much weight they carry

1. **The hiding is the revealing.** Self-deception aimed at a person telegraphs itself *because* it's hidden from the self. The tell is the absence. Nothing extra has to leak; the state signals itself. (Trivers treats leakage as a cost of self-deception. Here the leak is the payload.)

2. **Contempt is malice plus the pretense that it isn't, and it's delivered from the state it accuses.** That's why it's maddening rather than merely frightening, and why it's contagious: it shows the target a "good person" doing the thing, which is the lesson.

3. **Contempt produces what it accuses, and not by accident.** A part knows. The third answer between "villain" and "innocent": limbo. Limbo contains itself, so he knows he's overriding his conscience, so he can't rule out that you are too, so he must prove you aren't like him, so he provokes.

4. **The dark part survives by being seen as him.** The identification (*this is what he is*) is what gets others to attack him, which is what keeps it hidden in him. Contempt for the contemptuous is therefore the mechanism's reproductive strategy, not its cure.

5. **Two acts identical from outside can have opposite effects.** The auditable layer doesn't decide outcomes; the unauditable one does. This is why proof-culture can't see the problem and why policy debate rarely touches it.

6. **The feeling of contempt is a tug to be recruited.** It uses accurate perception of darkness in another as the hook. The more accurate, the harder to refuse. (This is the one-sentence takeaway.)

7. **It hijacks reason, then perception.** By the end, the world looks indistinguishable from one where the verdict is true. That is where "evil people" live, and holding the verdict on them locks the cage.

8. **The only lever is your own turning-toward.** Anything done from turned-away turns others away regardless of appearance. Corollary: using the book as a weapon spreads the thing.

9. **Evil is the result of turning away, not its cause; the further in, the more certain.** Hence all evil is innocent, in the specific sense that nobody chooses to stop seeing while they can still see.

10. **Contempt from below is the same thing as contempt from above.** It doesn't care about direction; it recruits the person with the least reason to serve it. The dangerous thing to it is fierce refusal with no verdict.

11. **The darkness is an absence masquerading as a presence, and it is one absence in many places.** That is why coordination across minds is free (same argument as free common knowledge), why the "global conspiracy" is real and has no members, and why every tradition personified it.

12. **The enemy getting better is the threat.** The arrangement requires the carrier not to improve, because the carrier holds what we couldn't.

13. **The escape hatch has to be nailed shut.** Judgment covers two different acts, and we keep them blurred on purpose, so that whoever is caught doing the contemptuous one can retreat into *"are you saying I shouldn't correct bad behavior?"* Naming that retreat as the mechanism's own move — not a reasonable objection — is a contribution, and it's the one the book can most concretely deliver.

14. **The dark preference.** We don't mock our enemies despite it making them dig in. We do it because it does. Test: punishment button versus enlightenment pill. Something would rather they stayed evil. Most treatments of moral psychology don't have this, because it can only be found by introspection, not measured.

15. **Conscience detects asymmetry, not harm.** The cheating case has no victim and no calculation, and it fires anyway. What's detected is *I am more*. Contempt is *you are less*. One faculty, both directions. This is the most load-bearing revision of the last two days: it makes "contempt is evil" an identification rather than a rating, and it unifies Chapter 1's two halves into one mechanism. (See `correspondence.md`, addendum.)

16. **Contempt attacks the premise, not the feeling.** The badness of another's suffering follows from same-kind. Contempt asserts different-kind. Hence the necessary escalation to *irredeemable*. Independently derived by the appendix (evidence and verdict with opposite signs) — two routes, one necessity.

17. **The ego exists, and the agent is it.** Profile from Chapter 1: aims, tracks, adapts under scrutiny, isn't embarrassed on exposure, benefits from misattribution. Bias does none. Abductive, with the rival named.

18. **Explicit common knowledge dissolves it.** Naming the act in the moment, with a shared word, removes the cover it depends on. (Scoped: the word alone doesn't; and the concept will be weaponized.)

## Marked (Tier III), stated in the book's own terms first

- There is one thing looking through countless eyes. Conscience is that thing knowing itself; the turning-away has nowhere to go because you are the world.
- The root fear is the fear of having left what you are; contempt is the search for your goodness in the wrong place.
- The not-looking goes all the way down to how we see matter.

## Where the book has to be careful

- "All harm" is Tier III. Tier I is "every harm I've caused required turning away." Hot vs. cold is what lets institutional harm count without overclaiming.
- "Parasite" and "absence" have to be sequenced, not chosen between. Use the frame (it does real work: it releases shame, and it makes the thing evictable), then dissolve it in the same chapter. What must never happen is the frame sitting unresolved long enough for the reader to start a war with a part of themselves.
- "Evil people" must include the case of contempt from below without triggering the reader, and psychopaths honestly deferred.
- Compassion and wisdom are separate skills; freedom from self-deception is not competence.

## Where the upload and the current draft conflicted (now resolved in the master)

Three places. All three are corrected in `argument.md` and marked ▸. Kept here so the reasoning is on record.

**1. Willingness / choice.** `argument.md` §5 says ignoring conscience is *"a choice, made freshly each time"* and *"we choose to follow the justifications."* §11 says the darkness is *"nothing but our willingness to turn away."*

The draft now argues the opposite, and I think the draft is right: **if it's a willingness, it's a choice; if it's a choice, it's an evil one; and now you need something in them that makes evil choices — which is the intrinsically bad part, reinstalled one level down.** The regress doesn't terminate. It also contradicts the draft's other load-bearing claim, that *you cannot simply decide to look*, because deciding is done with equipment that's already turned.

The reconciliation, which keeps what the upload was reaching for: **not-seeing isn't chosen, but not-looking-again is trainable.** There's no moment where someone with a clear view of the harm decides to do it anyway. There's a moment where the view goes. But the practice of catching it earlier each time is real, and that's the sense in which anything here is up to you. "Willingness" should be struck from the metaphysics and replaced with "not-seeing" throughout.

### Corroboration: Dzogchen has this exact structure

Worth knowing, because it means the reconciliation isn't a patch invented to save the argument. Four correspondences, all structural:

- **Marigpa** is literally "non-rigpa": the absence of awareness recognizing itself. Not a substance, not an act, not a decision. The absence claim and the no-willingness claim in one term.
- **Adventitious stains** (*glo bur gyi dri ma*): the obscuration is never intrinsic to the nature, which stays unstained throughout. The you/not-you distinction, load-bearing there for the same reason it's load-bearing here — it's what makes looking survivable.
- **Familiarization** (*goms pa*, the word usually translated "meditation"): the path is repeated recognition, not repair. Exactly "not-seeing isn't chosen, but not-looking-again is trainable."
- **Non-fabrication** (*ma bcos pa*): any effort to fix it is more fabrication. Exactly "you cannot simply decide to look, because deciding is done with equipment that's already turned."

And two things beyond corroboration:

**Pointing-out instruction (*ngo sprod*) is the second stranger.** Their answer to *how does a turned mind turn itself?* is that it doesn't — recognition can't be produced conceptually; it's transmitted by someone already in it, and it works by producing the recognition directly rather than by argument. That is "he didn't put anything into you; he made it safe to remember," described from inside a tradition that has been refining the move for twelve centuries. It's the strongest external support the book has, and it lands on the part that otherwise has the least.

**They hit the regress and declined to resolve it on purpose.** The first straying is held to be beginningless, and several texts explicitly refuse a causal origin story — because any cause assigned to the first ignorance reinstalls the thing being explained. A tradition confirming the *shape of the problem*, not just the answer.

### What Dzogchen does not support, so don't claim it does

- **No coordination across minds.** Not the entity itself — an empty appearance that behaves like an agent is unobjectionable there, and if the Devil is held as empty display there's no conflict at all. What Dzogchen doesn't develop is the claim that non-recognition *coordinates between people*. That's the middle of this book and it is genuinely the author's. Saying so precisely makes it stronger.
- **No motive — and this one creates a real problem to solve.** Marigpa doesn't want anything. The Aspiration Prayer of Samantabhadra describes the first non-recognition as a momentary blankness with no motive assigned, and has self-and-other arise *after* it, not before. ACIM runs the opposite way: fear and guilt come first, and projection follows. The draft currently uses ACIM's version in §15. Both can't be the deepest layer. **Proposed reconciliation: the blank is the origin; fear is what the blank feels like once there's an "I" to feel it; projection is the first thing that "I" does.** This preserves the regress fix, because the fear is then the first *content* of the self that non-recognition produced, not the cause of the non-recognition.
- **Borrowing risk.** Non-fabrication is the most misread idea in that tradition, and its standard failure mode is quietism — *nothing to do, nothing to fix*. That is precisely the complicity half of the paradox. Take the structure, not the vocabulary.

**2. Limb language.** The upload's §11 says darknesses are *"limbs of a single organism."* The draft doesn't say this, because a limb implies a body that has parts, which reintroduces the presence you're trying to dissolve. The draft's version: one absence appearing in many places, like shadows in a valley pointing away from one sun. Same claim, no organism.

**3. Where unity is claimed.** The upload puts *"they are the same one thing"* in Thesis 3, whose stated evidence is observable coordination. Coordination is observable; identity isn't. The draft splits them: cooperation-so-seamless-it-behaves-as-one is checkable and goes early; one-absence-in-many-places is Tier III and waits.

## What the upload has that this document was missing

Both now folded in above (items 13 and 14): the escape hatch, and the dark preference. Also worth flagging as strong and not in my list because they're consequences rather than claims: *goodness is what remains in the absence of self-deception* (the punchline), and *the only people with any right to contempt are the ones it has rendered incapable of it.*
