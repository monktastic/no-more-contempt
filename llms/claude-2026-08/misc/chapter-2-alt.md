Imagine looking into a stranger’s eyes long enough that their face stops being something you’re looking at and becomes a place somebody is looking out of.

Stay there until you feel what comes with it—not a conclusion, but a recognition:

> _This one is like me. I want them to be all right._

Call this being _turned toward_ them. With it comes a sense of their innocence. Not that they're incapable of causing harm, but the sense that there's something innocent *underneath* it. If the looking deepens, it almost feels like you can see *yourself* in them.

Now erase that meeting. Run it again—but imagine that this time, the person you're meeting had just done something atrocious. Something deliberate, perhaps to someone innocent—maybe even to someone you love. 

Keep looking into the same eyes. Can you still find that same recognition?

It's much harder, isn't it? All the goodwill has drained from us. We are *turned away*—though this isn't the only reason people turn away from each other. [TODO: what I'm trying to get at is that there is also a "cold" turning away, where we don't feel rage but still use them.]

Where before there was someone whose wellbeing mattered, now there is only something rotten. Something less than human. 

We don't just feel anger. Not just *I will stop you*. There's something darker in it, and it feels like:

> *You are less than human, and have forfeited the right to be treated like one.*

Test that out in your own experience. Something in you wants this verdict to *land* on them; to register deep inside. Can you feel that?

Now ask what state they had to be in, in order to do whatever they did. Could they have done it while fully turned toward their victims? Or could they only do it while being turned away, too?

As you keep looking into their eyes, ask yourself what *you* look like to *them*. How does it compare to what you looked like in the first encounter?

Like it or not, your own state leaks through. Can you feel that? And ask yourself: when they see that you perceive them as *less than human*—in which direction do they feel compelled to turn? Back toward? Or further away?

And when they turn further away, how are others likely to treat them?

So you sent them a message saying they had forfeited the right to be treated like a human, and you needed that message to land. And they became someone who's harder to treat like one.

Do you think that’s a coincidence?

Look at it this way. Maybe you believe there's no path back for them. That even if one existed, they wouldn't take it. Fine. But was your message meant to offer one? Or only to make sure they heard the door slamming closed behind them?

There's a reason it's so hard to confront this question. It's because if you could see clearly what your message was really doing, *there's no way you could have done it.* It's *horrifying.*

That's why your dark part *needs* you not to see what you're really doing.

Why do we believe that turning away from people can ever turn them back?

We don't. But there's a part of us that almost *cannot help it.*

Now imagine the wisest person you know. Not a soft person, necessarily. When they look into those same eyes, are they delivering the same verdict?

Are they just *suppressing* it—or might they know something we don't?

I'll tell you how it works in my case. You can check this against yourself.

There's still some darkness left in me. It's painful to admit, but it's there. And when I see it in others, I feel an almost irresistible pull to reveal my own—as if it's *resonating* with theirs.

Here's what I think the wise people know. That the darkness they see in the other person is authentic. The verdict isn't. _Subhuman_ isn't something you perceive in them. It's your own darkness, answering theirs.

It's not that we see them as subhuman *and then* our darkness comes out. Seeing them as subhuman *is* our darkness.

We already believe that the innocence is more fundamental, and our language gives us away. When someone we love turns bitter or cruel and then, without being caught, sees it and stops, we don't say they turned into somebody else. We say they came back. When a generous person becomes callous, we say they lost themselves. Deep down. Better than this. Not himself. Came to his senses. One direction feels like recognition and the other like departure.
