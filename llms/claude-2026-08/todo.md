**NEW THESIS — the far end (added to `roadmap-for-beta-readers.md`, Part C).** The faculty needed to notice drift is the faculty being drifted from, so access shrinks. But the *knowing* doesn't shrink; it grows, because there's more harm to register. So the deeply lost person knows most and least at once, at maximum intensity. Consequences:
- What horrifies you when you look at such a person is not an absence. It's an enormous suppressed presence — a conscience under crushing load, showing through. You read it accurately.
- It shows itself *so that* you'll press harder, because your contempt is what the thing doing the suppressing requires.
- "They need the most love" is therefore the flat reading, not sentiment: most carried, most pressure, least access, and every instinct we have adds to the load.

This is the foregrounded version of the appendix's "evidence and conclusion have opposite signs" (what conscience detects is the *presence* of a conscience being evaded). Consider making the appendix point to it.

**OPEN — the Jung/Girard/Arendt chapter.** Recommendation: write it, but place it late, not as 1b.
- *For writing it:* analytical readers will pattern-match to shadow projection, scapegoating, and the banality of evil, and if they file the book under one of those they stop reading carefully. Pre-empting a misread is service, not positioning.
- *Against placing it early:* after one chapter the reader can't judge the comparison, and it reads as jockeying for position. Late, they can judge it, and it reads as scholarship.
- *The substance, when written:* Jung has projection but no mechanism for how the shadow is *received*, and nothing about cross-mind coordination. Girard explains the group and not the dyad, and has no account of the tell. Arendt's "thoughtlessness" is limbo described from outside — she saw the not-looking exactly and never asked what maintains it or why it's motivated. Each stops in a place that is itself evidence for the mechanism.
- Keep it framed as *what each got right and where it stops*, never as *how I differ from them*.

**The inside/outside asymmetry is what makes the arrangement stable.** From outside it looks monstrous. From inside it feels righteous — not tolerable, not excused, *good*. And nobody ever gets both views of the same instance: you have the inside of yours and the outside of everyone else's. So we are all accurate about each other and blind about ourselves, permanently, by construction rather than by character.

**Consequence the book needed and didn't have:** this is why we can't just tell each other. The outside view, delivered, arrives on the inside, where it lands as an attack on the best thing in the person. That is the first stranger in one sentence. **Stockholm, correctly stated:** he isn't struggling, because from where he sits nothing is holding him.
