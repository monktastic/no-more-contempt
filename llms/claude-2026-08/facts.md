# The argument as a list of facts

*Stateless. One line per fact, in the order they're used; under each, how it's established (the reader's own check, the public record, definition, or inference from earlier lines) and what it can't carry. `argument.md` says what the book claims; `spine.md` lists the theses; this is the short chain from a stranger's contempt to "addressed to the part it denies," with the status of every link. When a line changes status, change it here. The check IDs are `../../scratch/key-points/scenarios.md`.*

Read the chain as Aditya put it: I'm turned away from you, which is a milder form of the gaze harm comes from; you can see it, and we both know what it is; I'm calling it righteous; and some part of me knows. Everything below is that, with the joints exposed.

## The chain

1. **Contempt is a turning away from a person, and it is the same thing as the dehumanizing gaze, milder.**
   Status: the reader's own check. The day-one whiff against the far end, "same contempt, turned up a few notches" (the progression in short Chapter 3; the political-enemies draft). Continuity is what's checked, not identity; readers grant it once the far end is in view.

2. **Harm comes from turning away. Nobody harms a person while turned toward them.**
   Status: the reader's own check (1.5: try to picture harming someone with genuine kindness in your eyes), plus the public record (1.5c: hoods, dehumanization before atrocity, Grossman's distance). Mixed for small harms unless the person is held as mattering. This is the load-bearing universal; the book states it for the reader's case and lets the record carry the rest.

3. **Both of us can read turning away in a face, instantly.**
   Status: the receiving end (2.5, 3.9): you knew what the look was before you could have said. Not a claim about a theory of expression; a claim about what every reader has done.

4. **So when I show you contempt, I show you the thing harm comes from, in me, and we both know what it is.** (from 1–3)
   Status: inference. "Both know" here is approximately common knowledge: an announcement with the record removed. The out-loud test carries it for a reader: if he had said it in words, what would you have learned? Nothing; only that now you could quote him. Exact common knowledge needs line 11 and is not needed for anything before it.

5. **I'm calling it righteous. Contempt is malice that needs to be seen and needs to deny itself.**
   Status: definition, and the reader's check (2.7, the taunt; 4.5 the extra). Malice alone doesn't need the mask. Deniability faces third parties and my own account of myself, not you: the mockery is on the record; the whiff isn't.

6. **Some part of me knows I'm turned away.**
   Status: the reader's own case, from the aiming. You delivered it where it would land, in the form that could be denied, and you knew what they'd feel (3.4, 4.5). Nothing aims what it doesn't know. Also the relief when the verdict lands (3.8) and "who can really say" (2.8). Not provable about anyone else; the book says so.

7. **So the message is: here is where harm comes from, in me, aimed at you, called good, and I know it, and you can see that I know it.** (from 4–6)
   Status: inference. This is the taunt. It differs from a masked threat in one way: a threat needs no recognition from you; a taunt does. The recursion (you know, I know you know, you know I know you know) is only what "taunt" means.

8. **What it does: it invites you to turn away from me, and your doing so is what I take as proof.**
   Status: the reader's own check from the receiving end (you dug in; 3.11 the write-off) and from the sending end (you'd have reacted the same way; the doubling-down-as-proof line). The wanting (3.3, 3.7 the pill) is separate evidence that the proof was wanted.

9. **The harm this does, beyond the ordinary leak: it teaches you that the part of you that knew, and couldn't prove it, isn't worth listening to.**
   Status: stated; supported by 3.9 and by what readers report of being on the receiving end. This is the harm that needs line 7; the ordinary leak (defensiveness, mirroring) needs none of the recursion.

10. **What it's addressed to: the part of you that can be hurt by it is the part that still holds me in view. A taunt lands only on someone who still cares what you are.**
    Status: the receiving-end check (2.5: it reaches further in than pride; an insult to pride stings differently). The weakest link in the chain, and the hinge: it identifies "the part that reads the message" with "the part that cares." The rival reading is that you read the threat with fear, not care. The check that separates them is the sting's address: fear would make you want to get away; this makes you want them to have been better.

11. **So contempt presupposes, in you, the thing it denies; and in me, because I had to find it in you to aim.** (from 10)
    Status: inference, and the sentence readers liked in short Chapter 2. The fixed point in Appendix 1 is this written out.

12. **The caring was not reduced. It was covered.**
    Status: the reader's own check: after the encounter, did you find you no longer cared about them, or that you had to keep not caring (the case kept open, the reasons rehearsed)? If it had gone there'd be nothing to maintain. Turning away takes upkeep (index §1); turning toward doesn't. Caveat the book keeps: practiced hiding stops feeling like effort.

13. **Everything after this is inference from 2, 10 and 12:** if care can only be covered, never removed, and harm needs the cover, then care belongs to what's looking (short Chapter 2's claim). That's where the short book's Buddhist reader arrives already and the general reader arrives by these steps. Nothing before line 13 needs it.

## What the chain doesn't carry

- **Contempt with no wrongdoer** (the stupid, the crazy; spine 39). Lines 1–12 hold with "harm" read as the verdict itself; the cover changes from "fighting evil" to "being accurate."
- **Cold contempt.** Line 5 changes: cold doesn't need to be seen, so lines 7–9 don't apply, and the leak is the ordinary one. The far end of line 1 is cold.
- **Collaboration, the parasite, the one goodness.** Downstream of 13, and marked as such in the short book's "What I'm claiming."
