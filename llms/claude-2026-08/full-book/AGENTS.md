## Drafting standard

- Track what the reader knows at each sentence. Introduce people, situations, actions, and referents before relying on them. A concrete-sounding noun without an established situation is not an example.
- Give a passage one developing task. Let its next question arise from what the reader has encountered. Avoid assembling topics with connective prose and calling the result a narrative.
- Let the reader inspect an experience before naming a general pattern or introducing an analogy. One example does not establish prevalence. "The low-grade infection" failed when used as an opening conclusion before the pattern was established.
- Prepare and follow through important turns. Do not place a chapter's later discovery in an opening question and then abandon it. A question mark does not earn the inference it contains.
- Go first where the finding is difficult: the author's own case can accompany the reader without pronouncing on them. Use actual supplied memories, explicitly hypothetical situations, or a genuinely open reader recall. Do not invent autobiography.
- Preserve distinctions between anger and contempt, care and niceness, responsibility and intrinsic badness, compassion and wisdom, and shared mechanism and equal harm or power. Put the distinctions where they do work; do not repeat every qualification in every paragraph.
- Keep the claim's basis and scope visible: what the reader can inspect, the explanation offered for it, and the author's wider testimony or metaphysics. These can be braided in the narrative. Do not use the fullest ontology as an unacknowledged premise of a claim said to stand without it.
- Do not make a reader's failure to find the predicted experience into proof that the mechanism has captured them. Reader-reception forecasts are editorial judgments, not actual cold-reader results.

## Compression pass

After establishing the sequence, cut explanations the scene or recall has already made unnecessary. Keep another example when it reveals a genuinely different operation or increases the pressure; cut it when it merely illustrates the same point again. Keep useful pauses, but remove repeated instructions and summaries. Preserve the specific encounter before preserving abstract commentary about what it means.

Read the opening continuously for undefined terms, unsupported generalizations, and premature conclusions. Then read the chapter continuously for actual development and repetition. A coverage map and a successful preservation check cannot substitute for this reading.

## Execution and scope
