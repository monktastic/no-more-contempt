# Chapter 2 map

*Stateless. What each section of `chapter-2.md` is for, the order they come in,
and why. Each section is named by the words it opens on, and the dividers in
the chapter fall where the sections change. Paragraph numbers go stale with
every edit, so none are used. Claude's fixes of October 30 are marked in the chapter
as `[TODO(Aditya)]` with the original wording quoted; this file says why the
order is what it is, not what's owed.*

## What the chapter has to do

Chapter 1 closes on "something we serve that transcends sides. But what is
it?" Chapter 3 answers why I keep doing what darkens the world: because I need
it dark. Between them this chapter has four jobs, in Aditya's words: define
good and evil, or show why that can't be done; show how we know it anyway;
show that we know it can't be pinned down and use that to our advantage; and
show that the knowledge sits somewhere in us and that the hiding is what
harms the world.

The landing it climbs to, in the reader's words: *I knew enough to hide what I
was doing. The gap in the record that I cite as the reason I couldn't have
known is the gap I was using to hide it. And the hiding is what I've been
putting into the world, the world I then point to as proof that goodness was
naive.*

## The organizing rule

Finish identifying the turn; then follow what it puts into the world. The
chapter is two investigations, and the second doesn't start until the first
is complete. Every boundary is a question the next section answers. If a
boundary ever needs "a line to carry it," that is the order being wrong, not
the prose; four reorders in one day were spent learning that, and this is
the one where no boundary needs carrying.

## The shape

**A. What good and evil are (the face through "And contempt is the turn
made into a message").** The reader meets what both strangers serve before it
has a name, then the name: an orientation toward life, and what naming it does
("so hating him is good"). Straight from that failure to naming into what
"evil" tracks: not outcome, not damage, not size; the exception, which nearly
defines it; not looking; the one move at any size, and the list that runs it
from the padded expense to the village. Then what kind of thing that move is:
not a drive, not an essence, not nothing. Then contempt: the move made into a
message, and the check that ends the anatomy (oriented, or telling myself I
am?).

**B. What the turn puts into the world ("Ask a soldier" through "Compare that
to the second stranger").** The soldier: degrading life with no one hurt. The
strangers: same intentions, same outcome, one did wrong; where's the wrong?
The executive answers: in his next decision, the ecosystem, his family. The
first stranger's wrong, named with that in hand. Why I can't count it, and
that I know anyway. The proof I know: I sneak in the thing. The politician,
and my share in him when his lies pay me. The two faces of the permission: the
formula I follow instead of looking, and the shrug when there is no formula.
The advisor that isn't one. The watcher, me. The refusal that is already the
harm. What I won't face, finding expression in the world. The reckoning. The
second stranger, who had nothing to hide.

**The door ("Part of me knows that").** Why, knowing what it puts into the
world, do I keep choosing what the first stranger chose?

## Where the seams are

Structural facts about this order, for whoever edits it next.

- One boundary changes investigations: the check that ends the anatomy into
  the soldier. The soldier paragraph now opens with a line announcing the act
  ("Now look at what the turn does with nobody there to be hurt"), not the
  finding.
- "So what is it that my enemy and I agree on?" shifts Chapter 1's "serve" to
  "agree on," which sounds like shared beliefs until the next line corrects
  it. Yours; noted here only.
- The chapter owns a definition of "in view" in the face, and later says that
  defining goodness hands the self-serving part a letter. Those are
  compatible: the first gives the reader a usable meaning for a term, the
  second rejects a formula that could replace looking. The two sentences
  that used to say so on the page are in the pen.
- The soldier appears once, at length, where he opens the second
  investigation. The one-line run from the cases and the check that followed
  the single move are both in the pen.
- "What I've decided for myself" is the chapter's one application, kept for
  its consequence (a benefit to me no longer excuses the harm I sense). Its
  method half (judging whom to support by orientation) is in Chapter 7, after
  the faith-in-humanity paragraph.
- The door asks why. Its answer is Chapter 3's, and the three earlier doors
  (the skull, the village, the distance) each asked something the chapter
  had already answered: the executive leaves the skull, the list has the
  village, the ecosystem is the distance.
