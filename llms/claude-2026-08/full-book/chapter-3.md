# Chapter 3: How Far It Reaches

Evil must be fought, with everything I have. But when I do it with contempt, I now see what I'm doing: using the other person as cover for my own darkness to seep into the world, undetected.

The problem is, it's never truly undetected. The great lie we tell ourselves is that it is.

The harm doesn't stop at him. It passes to the next stranger he won't give the benefit of the doubt, and to the kid who watches him do it. What gets passed along is the turning away behind my look, and the permission it gives. And I send it *about* people, to my allies, with nobody's face in view. Most of all, I do it on social media. Everyone in the conversation learns that the verdict is safe to hold, and leaves a little more practiced at it.

Little of my contempt is for the "bad people" at all. It goes to people I've decided are stupid, or crazy, or weak, or slow: the coworker who doesn't get it the third time, the relative who believes something ridiculous. They've harmed nobody. The verdict is the same one, *this is all you are*, and the cover is better. With a wrongdoer I tell myself I'm fighting evil. Here I tell myself I'm only being accurate, and I may be: they may really be wrong, or slow. But being right about what someone can't do doesn't call for the eye-roll, or the sigh, or the need to be above them. Something in me is constantly sensing who it's safe to treat this way.

Here's a check. Think of someone who can't do a thing through no fault of theirs: a toddler who can't add, a grandparent losing their words. Is the contempt there? For me it isn't. It arrives once I've decided they *should* know better; that they could, and won't. So even this contempt seems to carry a moral charge underneath. I've found them guilty of being what they are.

And it leaks the same way. A person treated as stupid stops asking questions. A person treated as crazy stops trying to make themselves understood, and I take that as proof.

And much contempt isn't performed at all. It's consumed. Bring up a scene of somebody getting exactly what they had coming, delivered with relish. It feels good, and it wants more: a hot little *fuck yeah*. Something in me was waiting for that. It's why this wrong, of all wrongs, spreads: it leaves no record, it comes dressed as duty, and it's rewarded in public. Comment sections, the shared post, the outrage we pass around: each one negligible, and together they set how dark everyone assumes the world is.

It has catchphrases, too. *Darwin award. FAFO. Play stupid games, win stupid prizes.* Each one enjoys somebody's harm and files it as deserved: they were stupid, so it doesn't count. The darker the world looks, the easier it is to stop listening to the part that knows, and every time I stop, I make the world a little more like the place I needed it to be. Turning away isn't binary, and most of it has no enemy in it at all. It's the water we swim in: the ordinary day, the small self-serving move with nobody in front of you and nothing you'd call a wrong.

That's where I live most of the time. I'm not holding anyone in contempt right now, and I'm nowhere near free of it either. Most of what I do is quietly self-serving, with justifications I never notice: the choice that helps me *and*, I tell myself, the world; the corner I cut with a company because it would do worse to me. And sticking it to the man: the padded claim, the return I wasn't entitled to. The company is soulless, so it doesn't feel like taking. It's the same verdict aimed at something with no face, and it makes the thing more like what I said it was. The insurer that's been gamed tightens the rules on everyone. This is what the buttons in Chapter 1 were measuring. Not whether you've done anything terrible, but how much contempt you're still capable of, toward anyone, for any reason. To the degree it's there, the road to the worst is open, and what's left is time and conditions. The murderer didn't start there, and nothing about you guarantees you'd have turned out differently. That isn't said to frighten you. The road is open in both directions, and the way back is the same looking.

When I speak contempt to my allies, or into a crowd, something in me is looking for others to resonate with.

Remember two people you know tearing into someone who wasn't there. Watch what happened between them: each a little bolder than they'd have been alone, each handing the other permission, and a warmth between them that had nothing to do with the person they were discussing. Now remember a time you were one of the two. What did it feel like when the other person agreed? What would have happened if someone had said, quietly, *I don't think that's fair*?

I call rooms like that "shadow spaces." Someone tests the water, and part of you knows at once which signal to send back. *I'm really worried about Lisa,* and now two people can dig in, both of them only concerned. A friend after basketball "forgot" some cash on his taxes: *the government wastes it anyway, right?* A strategy meeting among allies takes one joke to turn into *have you* seen *what they're posting?* Or imagine a locker room. Ten men, gathered around. One makes an off-color joke, testing the water. If nobody opposes it, the space opens up, and what each of them was holding back comes out. Each man is a little bolder than he would have been alone. The moment the "wrong person" enters the room, the space shuts down. Nobody speaks of what just happened. If someone had opposed any of those rooms with contempt ("you're all evil!"), that too would have fed the same thing in everyone, including the objector.

Fierce is where the darkness finds it easiest to get in. From outside, fierce and dark look alike, and it counts on our not wanting to tell them apart. But we can tell, from the sending end, if we're willing to look: that little something extra, added to the fierceness on its way out, that you knew they'd feel. I've almost always believed I was doing the clean one, including when I wasn't.

Here is what the healthy version sounds like. *That was out of line, and it stops now.* It aims at the darkness in a person, never at the person, so it carries no malice. And the same words with the extra: *That was out of line, and it stops now*, followed by the half-second pause to watch it land. *I don't think you understood what that did to her.* With the extra: *I don't think you're capable of understanding what that did to her.* *You lied to me.* With the extra: *Of course you lied to me.* Each time the difference is one word or one pause, and each time you know, on the way out, which one you sent. And the clean one does something the other can't. Fierce and turned toward, I'm not only stopping him. It's as though the light in me is reminding the light in him that it has a partner in the fight against what has him, and I can do that while still fighting the man. One more check, on the scenes in this chapter. Which of them made you feel contempt, not *that's wrong* but contempt for the person? For me, the ones I could judge safely were the shadows that weren't mine. The ones that made me wince were closer to home.

Corrupt leaders meet a hidden need in all of us. Their visible wrongdoing lets us establish our own relative goodness by contrast, while providing cover for our smaller betrayals: _At least I'm not like them. I'm just sticking it to the man!_ If integrity were the norm, in our institutions and our leaders, our small self-deceptions would stand out, and we'd feel the pressure to look at them. Every time I claim they're a different kind of thing, part of me is helping keep them there. So we help produce the "bad people" whose actions justify our contempt; all of us build that same division into institutions; and those institutions provide new objects against which our contempt can feel righteous.

What I've been hiding isn't that I *could* do what the "bad people" do. It's that I'm *already doing it*, by using them as a place to hide the fact that I am.

Put those together and it's a loop. The need from Chapter 1, to be a different kind of thing from the people I despise, can't be met by anything true, so it makes its own evidence: my contempt pushes them further, and their hardening is the difference I needed to see. Then a darker world makes the need feel like perception, and hides it better from me: *I'm not making anyone bad; look at them.* And the darker the world gets, the easier my own small exceptions feel. The need makes the darkness, and the darkness feeds the need. [TODO(Aditya): Claude's draft (the loop, from Appendix 1's "What the need was looking for"). Rewrite in your words or cut.]

When I feel a fundamental difference between me and my worst enemy, I ask which part of me needs there to be one.

Most of what we now recognize as evil was once respectable. Plenty of it still gets a pass: greed gets praised as ambition, and indifference as realism. But contempt is the one we still praise as *goodness itself*, as what a good person is supposed to feel toward bad people. And that's why the others are so hard to stamp out: we fight every one of them with it.
