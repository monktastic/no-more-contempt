# Chapter 12: The People We Call Evil

**What it's for.** The reader brought the worst people to mind in the Preface. This chapter pays that. Evil as result not cause; the gradient; the far end where the knowing grows while access shrinks; the hostage at the window; the trap assembled (you're right, you're right, and the last step is the one you can't see); the sadist as the place the account could fail; psychopaths; all evil is innocent, and responsibility.

**Seeded in Chapter 5 by:** further along the road; the hostage; innocent and responsible; where I could be wrong.

## Outline

1. Evil is the result of turning away, not its cause. Build the worst thing you know of without the perpetrator having stopped counting the victims as the same kind of creature; you can't. (Map §10.)
2. The gradient: the further in, the less it feels like being in anything; more harm, more knowledge, more shielding, more certainty. (Map §10; Chapter 2's trip.)
3. The far end: the knowing grows; knows most and least at once; what you're looking at is a conscience under crushing load, shown to you so you'll help hold it down. The hostage at the window. "They need the most love" as arithmetic. (Map §10.)
4. The trap in one piece: right, right, and the last step. Accuracy is what gets harvested. (Map §10.)
5. From inside there's no captor; it presents as his judgment; it pays him. Nobody gets both views. (Map §10, "The hostage.")
6. The sadist, at full strength: in-view defined by three checks; the kind-gaze test; the suppression signature as a prediction; what losing would cost and what it wouldn't. (Map §10, "Two objections.")
7. Psychopaths: I don't know; access, not amount. (Map §10.)
8. All evil is innocent: the regress; if wrong can only be done from turned-away, turning away can't be chosen. And fully responsible: catching, repair, arranging your life. Your heart would break. (Map §10; key-points §8.)
9. Chapter 2's trip as the one instance from inside: subject in view, same-kind gone.
