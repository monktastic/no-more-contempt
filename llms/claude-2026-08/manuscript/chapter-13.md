# Chapter 13: Contempt From Below

**What it's for.** For the reader who thought of somebody who hurt them, or their child, or their people. Rage right, force right, only the verdict on trial; who is relieved when you hold it; the same parasite runs the one above you; fierce refusal without a verdict as the thing it fears; King; remorse versus shame; and the most common contempt of all, turned on yourself.

**Seeded in Chapter 5 by:** if the person you're carrying hurt you; use force; one thing on trial; the devil on your own shoulder.

## Outline

1. Nothing here asks you to feel less. Rage, fierceness, force. (Map §11.)
2. One thing on trial: the verdict. Who benefits: the parasite in them, which needed you to see them that way. (Map §11.)
3. The same parasite runs the person above you; it doesn't care which direction contempt flows; the tug at its strongest because the perception is most accurate. (Map §11.)
4. What frightens it: fierce, unyielding refusal with no verdict in it. King: hate too great a burden. Not sentiment; strategy, with a record. (Map §11.)
5. Remorse versus shame: what the second stranger produced. (Map §11; Chapter 5.)
6. Self-contempt: the verdict turned inward; feels like accountability; produces hiding. The devil on your own shoulder speaking to you. You'll hold their darkness the way you've learned to hold your own. (Map §11.)
7. The "wait, so I shouldn't correct bad behavior?" swap. (Map §12.)
