# Chapter 8: The Water

**What it's for.** The real impetus of the book. Subtly turned away is our default state, most of the day, and the reader has to come to feel that gently and without shame: not "you are bad" but "oh, I'm in the grip of this thing most of the time, and so is everyone, and that's why it's winning." The chapter widens turned-toward from the single face to the fierce wanting for all of it, and then asks the question that exposes the default: when you decide what to do with your day, is it from thought, or from that?

**Seeded in Chapter 5 by:** "Most of the day."

## Outline

1. Turned-toward isn't only the stranger's eyes. It's the same recognition aimed at all of it: the part of you that wants nothing more than for life to go well, all of it, and would defend it. You've felt that too, and it's fierce. (Chapter 5, Two looks; key-points §12.)
2. The question: when you're deciding what to work on, what to say, what to buy, is it based on your thoughts about what will help, or on a felt connection to that? Not "should it be." Is it. (Key-points §2: "the most important thing we bring to our work is our connection to the Light"; §10.)
3. Gently: for nearly all of us, nearly all the time, it's thought. That is what turned away is at its most ordinary. No heat, no enemy, no verdict. Just steering by the arguing part with the deep part out of the room. (Map §4.)
4. What it looks like: the action that helps the world and also happens to help you, and a deeper part knows which was the point; the small thrill of getting away with something; seeing all of this in your enemies and none in your allies; being unwilling to hear somebody's experience; the people around you as extras in your movie; the uncharitable reading; the agenda look, the hardening everyone in the room can read and nobody names. (Map §4; key-points §3, §10.)
5. "My clarity about them proves I'm clean." The mechanism using its own detector as cover. (Map §4.)
6. Everybody believes they're free of it, that it's something only bad people do; that belief is the surest sign it's operating. The turning point when the storehouse is unveiled. (Key-points §1, §10.)
7. Moral self-licensing, reversed: much of the good we do is to hide the recognition of the dark. (Key-points §7.)
8. Why it matters at this scale: the Devil, capital D, isn't winning through the monsters. It's winning through the default. It has all of us as minions most of the day, and none of us has to intend anything. (Chapter 5, The room and the world.)
9. The point isn't to make you feel rotten. It's scope. (Map §4.)

## Key points to carry (L0/L1; [5] = seeded in Chapter 5)

- Limbo isn't a rare exception; it's the water. [5]
- Turned-toward is the fierce love for all of it; actions not rooted there are turned away, however good the plan. [5]
- The what-to-work-on question. [5]
- Dual purposes; the thrill; enemies versus allies; unwilling to hear; NPCs; the uncharitable reading; the agenda look.

- The storehouse unveiled: after that, self-deception becomes painful and you clean it up bit by bit.
