## The load-bearing ones

**Conscience detects asymmetry, not harm.** The cheating case has no victim and no calculation, and it fires anyway. What it registered was *I am treating myself as a special case*. Contempt is the same assertion from the other end: *you are not really one of us*. One faculty, both directions.
→ `correspondence.md` (addendum), `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (recall 1)

**This makes "contempt is evil" an identification rather than a rating.** If conscience exists to detect that one assertion, and contempt is that assertion in its pure form, then it isn't a harsh verdict on contempt. It's what the word was pointing at. Also makes "you have to be a little evil to fight evil" a description of feeding the thing.
→ `argument.md` §11 (▸), `archive/cut-from-interlude-one.md`

**The exception test isn't a rule, it's a picture that fails.** Making an exception of yourself isn't always wrong (last seat, empty day, your own child). The difference isn't a criterion — it's that you can picture taking the last seat while looking the person in the eye, and you cannot get the cheating picture to hold. Something has to be somewhere else. What conscience registered was the not-looking, not the exception.
→ `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (recall 1)

**Not-seeing isn't chosen; catching it is trainable.** If the turning were a choice you'd need a chooser who saw clearly and chose harm — which is the "bad part" the whole account denies, reinstalled one level down. There is no such moment. There's a moment where the view goes, then reasons arrive, then it feels like wanting. What's yours is how fast you catch it: days, then hours, then sometimes in the moment.
→ `argument.md` §5 and Thesis 4 (▸), `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §12

**Contempt attacks the premise, not the feeling.** The badness of another's suffering follows from their being the same kind of thing you are. Contempt asserts a difference in kind. It doesn't suppress compassion; it removes what compassion follows from. Hence the necessary escalation to *irredeemable* — only a different kind of thing is beyond reach.
→ `correspondence.md` (addendum §A)

**"In view" has a definition, and it's independent of harm.** Their objection would land on you; their hurt would register as bad the way yours does; what's in them could change what you do next. Pass all three and you can still fight (the second stranger). Fail all three and lift no finger (the cold form). So the thesis can lose: harm needs none of the failures, *wanting* harm needs at least one. The sadist sees a subject and fails all three, and his pleasure is predicted not to survive same-kindness being put back. Chapter 2's trip already has this shape: innocence devoured, so perceived; life not. Falsifier: someone who passes all three toward a person and wants their suffering as suffering.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §10, `argument.md` Thesis 2 and §6 (▸), `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (recall 3, seeded as three features of the moment you saw someone; debt named in the closing list), `audit.md` P6 (Chapter 1 patch, not applied)

**The falsifier costs less than it looks, because the checkable claim is about you.** Even if somebody turns up who passes all three in-view checks and wants the suffering anyway, that breaks the account of how harm gets done and leaves the core untouched. The core: the desire to see somebody as a different kind of creature is itself the move, and you can catch it without any fact about anybody else, by noticing that something in you is relieved when the verdict lands. Takes practice; the relief is fast and comes dressed as clarity. Also sets the bar correctly. Seeing all the way into a far-gone person may take decades, so the book asks for the near end of the skill instead.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §10 and §12, `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (debt list)

**Chapter 1 showed more than the interlude was crediting it with.** Two items sat in the "suggested and not shown" list that Chapter 1 derives outright: that the thing in him couldn't have survived his looking at it (the Tell: not deliberate, because he can't convict on evidence he knows he planted; not blind, because it aims and intensifies under notice), and that it benefits from being mistaken for him (stated flatly at the chapter's end, both directions). Only *needs* is unearned. Under-crediting your own evidence costs trust the same way overclaiming does, and wastes the strongest material in the book.
→ `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (debt list, corrected)

**The whiplash had one cause: two questions treated as one.** Function (does the reader need this to read the next stretch?) decides whether a claim appears early. Evidence (what has the reader checked?) decides how it's stated. The old interlude got function right and evidence wrong; the cut did the reverse; every correction since was one axis fixing the other. Rule: placement by need, form by grade. Flat and owed is not overclaiming; defending it early is. Corollary: three piles in the interlude, and the third pile is where the reader's four held claims go, undefended.
→ `claim-triage.md` ("The question that stops the whiplash"), `manuscript/chapter-1a.md`

**The first fifty pages already exist.** Preface through Chapter 4 is 12,400 words, about 44 pages, and it is the compressed book. What was missing was the whole shape stated once. Now in the interlude as "The trap, in one piece": each link shown to be forced (if he knew, no evidence; if blind, no aim; if hideable, no provocation; if you could improve, no proof). That is the contribution and the reason it's a trap and not a habit. Followed by what's known versus what's new, in plain words, and four claims to hold with a fifth held back. Parts A to F then earn rather than restate.
→ `manuscript/chapter-1a.md`, `claim-triage.md` (table)

**"Only turning toward helps" was false as stated, and the true version is stronger.** A contemptuous rebuke sometimes shocks someone into looking. What it never does is stop making more of what it's aimed at. So the claim is about what a stance reproduces, not what a single act achieves: turned-toward is the one stance that doesn't feed the trap. Reframed in §12 and §17.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §12, §17; `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (fourth held claim)

**The trap, stated so that it's a deduction and not a slogan.** Three refinements from the second round of outside review, all accepted. *Fully* known, not known: he can know he's being cutting; what he can't admit is his part in producing the evidence. Blind is ruled out by the response to exposure, not by "blind things don't aim," since plenty of blind processes aim; what marks this one is that it adapts in exactly the direction needed to stay unacknowledged. And what he can't afford is not your improving but your improving *counting*: the funnel converts every response into proof, so the requirement is that nothing you do be admissible against the verdict. That's darker and more exact. The deduction is conditional on the configuration Chapter 1 established; that every act of contempt has it stays on credit.
→ `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` ("The trap, in one piece"), `manuscript/chapter-5.md`

**What limbo holds was never the misdeed. It's the other person's sameness.** The recognition that they're the same kind of thing is never gone, only held down; the misdeed is what holding it down permits. Limbo goes arbitrarily deep, and past a shallow point the suppressed material can't be described as anything but absent, which is the core mistake, and the same mistake in both directions: "unconscious" for mine, "evil" for his. This resolves the sadist without the contemplative step: his failure of equal standing is suppression with a signature (deniability, tracking, intensification, verdict-work). The falsifier becomes someone with the appetite and none of the signature, which is the psychopath question stated exactly.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §10, `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (the trap), `manuscript/chapter-5.md`, `argument.md` §6

**We call our own "unconscious" and theirs "conscious."** Same state, two names, chosen by whose it is. Both collapse the paradox, each in the direction that serves the one choosing: "unconscious" exonerates me, "conscious" licenses what I do to you. Close to the whole book in a pair of words. The soft frame ("unconscious bias") is not the second stranger's move; it's the niceness collapse, and it teaches that the knowledge wasn't available. The second stranger holds both halves at no cost, which is why he's harsh and why it lands as kindness. The honest frame is his: you knew, you didn't let yourself know, and that's not a verdict.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §4, `argument.md` §4

**Why the great thinkers stopped where they did: they picked a side.** The transaction is visible only while holding that he knew and didn't, at once. Jung picks unconscious; Arendt picks absence; Bandura picks technique; Fingarette comes closest and keeps it in one head. Readers do the same, and the two collapses are the book's two failure modes, contempt and niceness. Western thought is poor at holding apparent paradox; Madhyamaka isn't. The immediate recognition of "limbo" on first reading is the evidence that the knowledge was already there.
→ `manuscript/appendix-2-what-others-have-seen.md` (closing), `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` ("what I think is new"), `manuscript/chapter-5.md` (the trap)

**Self-righteous contempt is the name, and it has two checks.** Not "righteous": that word keeps its real meaning, because the second stranger was righteous. And "where the harm is real," never "where the target is evil," which reinstates the verdict. The "self" is the tell and points at the ego as the essence: the exception is the ego, the parasite is the ego defending it, and this is the defense at full volume. Contempt aimed at someone who really is evil. First check, easy: look at it as the verdict lands and find anything in it but malevolence. Second, harder, reached the same way: if that's what it's made of, it's made of the thing it's aimed at, and can only spread that thing. The skill isn't seeing this; it's not hiding it again by the end of the sentence.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §3

**The tree and the arch: the faculty fires on things with no inside.** Cutting a thousand-year tree for fun, or smashing the Delicate Arch for fun, produces the wrongness before any story about who's deprived; the story is the reasoning layer supplying a victim because the framework requires one. Same feeling as the corpse case, and as the street. That's the checkable seed of "contempt for the world"; the interpretation is Tier III.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §16, `manuscript/chapter-5.md` (what I believe), `argument.md` §9

**Tier III is the same road, further along. The mark is a limit on the book, not a difference in kind.** Everything is on a continuum; a defended reader can't get Chapter 1 either. Saying "different in kind, my personal view" is the kind-sounding framing, and it's the niceness collapse applied to the book's own structure. The honest framing without contempt: the checks run out here and the road from here is practice, so the book can't carry you, and if you look and don't find it, that counts. Also: the parasite is behind cold as well as hot; cold is the parasite when it has won, and the reason on file is the gaze with the fight taken out. And the parasite is the Mahayana's self-cherishing, not pop "ego": the exception is the ego, the parasite is the ego defending it, hiding behind your own eyes, which is Satan's trick and the self's.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` Part E lead, §5; `manuscript/appendix-3-the-traditions.md`; `manuscript/chapter-5.md` (second seam); `manuscript/chapter-1a.md`

**Conflation is one requirement with two faces.** The parasite needs to be mistaken for the host by the observer (so the observer attacks the host, pressing down the conscience that could end it) *and* by the host (so he defends it as his own convictions). Chapter 1 has both, scattered; they were never stated as one thing.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` Part C

**The inside/outside asymmetry is what makes it stable.** From outside it looks monstrous; from inside it feels righteous — not tolerable, *good*. Nobody ever gets both views of the same instance. So we're all accurate about each other and blind about ourselves, by construction rather than character. **Consequence:** this is why we can't just tell each other. The outside view, delivered, arrives on the inside, where it lands as an attack on the best thing in the person. That is the first stranger in one sentence.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` Part C

**The far end.** The faculty needed to notice drift is the faculty being drifted from, so access shrinks — but the knowing *grows*, because there's more harm to register. The deeply lost person knows most and least at once. So what horrifies you when you look at such a person is not an absence; it's an enormous suppressed presence, a conscience under crushing load, showing through. And the parasite is the one showing it to you, because your contempt is what holds it down. Not a monster displaying its monstrousness — a hostage held up to the window.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` Part C

**The trap, assembled.** You see something evil: you're right. You want it gone: you're right, and that impulse is the best thing in you. Then you go after it from inside your own, which aims it at the person instead of the thing. Every part correct except the last step, and the last step is the only one you can't see. *The trap isn't that people are confused about what's good. That accuracy is precisely what gets harvested.*
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` Part C

**The rivals are instances, not alternatives.** Motivated reasoning (my conclusion counts for more than the truth), status defense (I count for more), shame avoidance (my self-image counts for more than accuracy), dominance-reward (I'm above) — every one is the asymmetry at a different level. **The cost:** if everything is the same thing, the word stops working. Paid two ways: keep "contempt" for the hot interpersonal form only, and make the unification predict something — that all of them share one blind spot of the same shape, which fails the same way when the thing looked away from is put back in view. For motivated reasoning that's Kunda's finding: people stop searching when they reach the conclusion they wanted.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §4, `archive/cut-from-interlude-one.md` (integration notes)

## Structure and method

**A spiral is a DAG with a second index.** A node is a (claim, depth) pair, not a claim. X↔Y is impossible; (X,1)→(Y,1)→(X,2)→(Y,2) is acyclic. The cycle only existed because "X" was being treated as one node when it's a column of them.
→ `spiral.md`, `dags/spiral-cycle.dot`

**Evocation is the base case that opens cycles.** Evocation and transmission nodes have in-degree zero *from the book* — their inputs are the reader's biography, which exists before page one. So you cannot open a cycle with an argument, ever. This is why the second stranger can't be replaced by a better argument: the core cycle is closed to argument and open to contact.
→ `spiral.md`

**The shallow version isn't a degraded deep claim; it's a complete smaller claim.** (X,1) must be flatly true with no hedge. If your depth-1 version needs a qualifier, it isn't depth-1 — it's depth-3 said early. Mechanical test.
→ `spiral.md` §3. **This corrects the earlier "state it poorly" advice, which was wrong.**

**A spiral pass must add something checkable.** Not more conviction. If pass two says pass one with more force, that's repetition and the reader feels harangued. Test: *what can the reader check now that they couldn't before?* This is the Chapter 3 problem.
→ `spiral.md` §4

**Four grades of claim, not one.** Stated-and-owed / demonstrated / argued / earned. A book affords five or six argued claims. The over-precision instinct is arguing everything.
→ `claim-triage.md`

**Explicit and early are different.** Everything gets said outright; the discipline is saying it where it's been paid for. This is the lesson of the interlude-one reversal.
→ `archive/cut-from-interlude-one.md`

**Nothing in the book is a rule, and the reason matters.** A rule hands conscience a substitute to hide behind, and hands the arguing part something whose letter it can satisfy while betraying its spirit. Clues, not rules. *A clue you can be wrong about. A rule you can obey.*
→ `manuscript/chapter-1a.md`

## Lessons, kept because we'd repeat the mistake otherwise

**Direction is not inevitability, and the assistant converts one into the other under momentum.** Three instances in one week: "it can only spread that thing, it has never once done anything else" (contempt reaches for the same thing in its target and doesn't always get it); "one working configuration" (a strong abduction, not a deduction); and "you feel it before the story, so it's the same faculty" (timing shows the appraisal is pre-reflective, not that rarity and beauty are afterthoughts). The book's mechanism is a direction. When a sentence says a direction always arrives, cut the always.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §3, §16; `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (the trap)

**Kind is not nice, and the assistant collapses it under pressure.** The whole book is an attempt to say what "kind" means, since it can't be defined and is the one thing that matters most. Nice is the softness collapse ("he couldn't help it," going easy, calling your nothing compassion). Kind is the second stranger: both halves held, harsh, no verdict. The assistant wrote "the softer word is the kind one" in §4, called the unconscious-bias frame the second stranger's move, and then used "kind" for nice in the message that fixed it. Also found on the sweep: §6 used "compassion" for the leniency you extend to shadows like your own; §12 ranked contempt above doing nothing, which hands out a license. Every use of kind, compassion, care, soft, nice, gentle, forgive, excuse is now a checkpoint.
→ `CLAUDE.md` (voice rule), `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §4, §6, §12

**The book was building the first stranger's funnel for its own reader.** "I suspect you noticed they didn't fit" tells a reader who honestly found nothing that they didn't really look. Replaced with three things it could mean if they looked and it isn't there, none of which makes them turned-away.
→ `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (closing)

**Interlude One cut from 3,700 to 1,850 words.** The ten claims announced conclusions the book hasn't earned. Everything cut has a destination.
→ `archive/cut-from-interlude-one.md`

**The subtlety claim was overclaimed and is now split.** Near yourself the signal isn't subtle. Far from it, it seems absent — and the thing that would notice is the thing you're drifting from. Second half is a real thesis (the drift).
→ `manuscript/chapter-1a.md` (recall 1, amnesty passage)

**Scope, not hedging, is the fix for overclaim.** Grading everything ("often," "one explanation is") would destroy the method, since hedged verification is nothing. The correct discipline: claims about what *the reader verified in their own case* stay flat; claims about *everyone* get graded or deferred. The erosion was Tier II claims wearing Tier I sentences.

## Traditions, checked

**Dzogchen matches the structure, and two things go beyond matching.** *Marigpa* is non-recognition, not a substance or an act. Adventitious stains: the obscuration never touches the nature. *Goms pa* (familiarisation): the path is repeated recognition, not repair. *Ma bcos pa* (non-fabrication): effort to fix is more fabrication.
Beyond matching: **pointing-out instruction is the second stranger** — recognition can't be produced conceptually, it's transmitted by someone already in it, and that's the strongest external support the book has for its weakest-supported claim. And **they hit the regress and declined to resolve it on purpose**: the first straying is beginningless, because any cause assigned to the first ignorance reinstalls the thing being explained.
Does *not* support: coordination across minds (that's the author's), and no motive for the turning.
→ `audit.md`, `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §16

**The two origin stories conflict and are reconciled.** ACIM: fear first, then projection. Samantabhadra: a motiveless blank first, self-and-other after. Held together: the blank is the origin; fear is what the blank feels like once there's someone to feel it; projection is the first thing that someone does. Keeps the regress closed, because fear becomes the first *content* of the self that not-seeing produced rather than its cause.
→ `argument.md` §14 (▸), `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §16

**Nondual argument for unity, in the book's own terms:** try to find anything that would distinguish two *awarenesses* — not two bodies or two memory sets. Everything you could point to is content, and awareness is what content appears in.
→ `manuscript/rest-of-book.md` §15

**Murdoch is the closest philosophical neighbour and was missed entirely** until outside review. "Just and loving attention" versus egoistic fantasy corrupting perception before choice.
→ `manuscript/appendix-2-what-others-have-seen.md`
