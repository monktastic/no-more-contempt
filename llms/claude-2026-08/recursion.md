# Appendix: The Recursion

*For the reader who wants the machinery. Nothing in the book rests on this — the book rests on what you found when you looked. But if you noticed the shape of the thing and want it stated precisely, here it is. One metaphysical claim is held to the end and marked; nothing before it needs it. Sections are numbered, and I refer back to them as §1, §2, and so on.*

---

## 1. One faculty, many copies

Everything below follows from a single structural fact, and it's one you verified rather than one I'm asserting.

The faculty that tells you something is wrong is the same faculty that notices when you're turning away from it, and it's the same faculty that recognizes its existence in others. Call it **C**. Chapter 1 used all three jobs without naming them: you knew you were doing wrong before any calculation (job one); you felt yourself deflecting whenever anyone got close (job two); you read a stranger's limbo in seconds, on no evidence, and weren't surprised to be read yourself (job three).

Here is the only assumption I need: **C is the same faculty in every person.** Not similar—the same, in the way your visual system and mine are the same system, so that what one of us can see, the other can see. That's *type*-identity: same equipment. Whether it's also *token*-identity—one thing, appearing in each of us—is the metaphysical question, and I'll get to it last.

Everything below is that one assumption unpacked, in this order:

- **§2** — why the infinite mutual knowing costs nothing to build, and why nothing in it can be located at a particular level.
- **§3** — what, specifically, is mutually known.
- **§4** — why his hiding and his showing turn out to be the same act.
- **§5** — why the harm can't be named, and why naming it wouldn't help.
- **§6** — why it spreads to you.
- **§7** — three things that fall out of all this.

If you only want the shape: shared equipment makes an infinite tower free (§2–3), which makes concealment self-defeating (§4), which makes the harm unprovable (§5), which makes it contagious (§6).

## 2. Why the mutual knowing costs nothing

Game theorists call something *common knowledge* when everyone knows it, everyone knows that everyone knows it, and so on without end. Normally that tower has to be built by a public event: a trusted party says the thing aloud where everyone can see everyone hear it. (The child in *The Emperor's New Clothes* isn't giving anyone information. Everyone already knew. He's making it common knowledge.)

In the contempt encounter nobody says anything and nobody is trusted, and yet the whole tower is present in a glance. How?

Because of §1. Two people share the faculty, and the faculty knows that—job three *is* the recognition of C in someone else. So take any fact the faculty knows about its own operation, and call it φ. Then:

- A knows φ. (C knows itself.)
- A knows that B knows φ. (A knows B has C, and C knows φ.)
- A knows that B knows that A knows φ. (Same step again.)
- …and so on.

Every level is the *same step*: C recognizing C. The tower isn't built level by level; it's one move that already contains all its iterations, the way two facing mirrors don't compute each reflection [or rather, wouldn't, if c were infinite]. That is the fixed point: a knowledge state that contains knowledge of itself, reached in a single application. **Under identity, the base is the tower.** [what does this mean? the base appears to be that A knows phi.]

This also settles something that otherwise looks like a puzzle: why no single level is *the* one where the thing happens. [What is "the thing"? I think this section needs more context. Maybe: where does the harm originate? or something]

Is the injury in his accusation? The accusation was defensible. In the lie behind the accusation? He doesn't experience himself as lying. In his hiding of the lie, then? In your seeing him hide it? In his seeing you see it? In his hiding *that?* …

Every level you check has the same content as the one beneath it, because every level is the same step. So there's no floor. Ordinary recursion bottoms out—factorial descends to 1 and stops, and the base case is simpler than what sits above it. This one hands you the entire structure again at every depth, which means there is no simple offense down there that the rest is decoration on. [This needs clarification: the bullets above make it look like a sequence.]

Which is why a mind that goes looking doesn't come back with anything. That isn't a metaphor for confusion. It's what a non-terminating search feels like from the inside: the hall of mirrors is the stack overflow.

## 3. What is mutually known

What does C know about its own operation? Take the lattice the encounter runs through, in order: [what makes this a lattice?]

- **Equality.** There is nothing morally superior about any bearer of C. That's job three's content.
- **Pretense.** When a bearer acts superior, C knows it's pretending
- **Projection.** Seeing the other as "bad" is the pretense pointed outward.
- **Harm.** Turning away from someone's humanity harms them, and C knows exactly how it lands.
- **Inexpressibility.** None of this leaves a record. C doesn't testify, and no other faculty saw it.
- …

The list doesn't close, and that matters. These are the rungs this particular encounter happens to run through; another encounter would run through others. What makes the set open is the next paragraph.

Each is a fact about C, so each is common knowledge by §2. And common knowledge is closed under shared inference: every bearer runs the same moral logic, so whatever follows from a common-knowledge fact is common knowledge too. The derived facts come free—that the turning away is a lie, that it causes harm, that it can't be proven, that the unprovability can be used against you.

Drawn out, this is a lattice with five rungs and three depths each (known / mutually known / common knowledge). Under identity [what does that mean?] the three depths of every rung collapse into one. That is why the whole lattice fits in a look.

## 4. Why the hiding is the showing

Now add his denial to the picture, and watch what it has to work on.

At the first level he hides what he's doing. But you see it—so now there's a second thing to hide: that you're seeing it. Which you also see. So he hides that. Each level of denial takes the level below it as its object:

> His denial at any level is the hiding of what is mutually known one level down. > 

[Should be clearer about levels. Both 2 and 3 seem to have levels.]

Or, if you want it compressed: with **D(n)** his denial at depth *n* and **K(n)** the mutual knowledge there, **D(n+1) = hide(K(n))**.

But §2 established that K(n) has the same content at every *n*. So although the denials appear to stack, they all have the same object. It isn't a tower of hidings. It's one hiding, performed again and again.

And here is what makes it a trap rather than a secret. That one hiding is exactly what you see. Job two means C registers its own being-turned-from; job three means every other C reads that registration straight off him. The concealment is the transmission—two strands, hide and show, with identical content, wound around each other. [This needs to be spelled out much more clearly.]

The shortest way to say it: **the signal is the pretense that there is no signal.** It doesn't carry a message and then conceal it. The concealment of the message is the message.

## 5. Why the harm can't be named

Gödel built a sentence **G** that says, in effect, "G cannot be proven." It's true—and true *because* it can't be proven; a proof would make it false. The construction is general: for any property P, there's a sentence saying "I have P." (That's the diagonal lemma.) Self-reference, plus a property that turns on its own status. [what does turns on mean?]

The taunt has this shape with P = *unprovable harm*. Define:

> **H** = being made to know, with no recourse and no proof, that H is being done to you.

Circular on purpose. If the harm had any other content—a slur, a shove, a lie you could catch—you'd have something to point to. This is a second and stronger reason for the unlocatability in §2: there, no level of the structure was privileged; here, the act has no non-self-referential residue anywhere in it. But it isn't empty, the way *this sentence is false* is empty. H has an independent effect: being held in that state teaches you that clear knowing counts for nothing, so you demote the faculty that knew and promote the one that can prove. That's a real injury with real consequences—it's what the second half of Chapter 1 traces. What the self-reference adds is only that *this delivery is an instance of it*. Saying it does it.

And it's self-sealing in a way G is not. Your only evidence for H was C's unprovable knowing. H's lesson is that unprovable knowing is worthless. So accepting the lesson erases your evidence that it was taught—you conclude you were being oversensitive, and the trap closes behind you.

§2 and §5 are two fixed points that are really one fact. The knowledge is common because C is shared. The harm is unprovable because C is the only witness, and C doesn't testify. Same faculty, seen from the knowing side and from the proving side.

## 6. Why it spreads to you

One more thing follows from §1 that the chapter performs but never states.

Because C is common equipment, treating you as though you lack it is false—and known false at every level. But it does something besides being false. It *shows you a move*: here is a bearer of C treating a bearer of C as other. And C recognizes the move, because it's the one C makes itself, in limbo. Recognition invites reciprocation: you now have a reason, and the look becomes available to you.

That reciprocation is not a side effect. It's what he needed. If you reciprocate, you look like what he said you were, and the verdict he needed becomes true on the evidence. His decision to see you as other *induces* the same seeing in you—and he wants it to, because your otherness is what keeps him other. The chapter calls this the bait. Formally: the move is contagious among bearers of C, and the contagion is the point.

## 7. Three things that fall out

**The verdict has to reach "irredeemable."** What C detects in the target is the *presence* of a conscience being evaded. What his conscious mind delivers is *absence*. Evidence and conclusion have opposite signs—which tells you the dark part isn't a perceiver but an editor: it keeps C's finding and deletes the half that carries the resemblance. And "bad" won't hold, because any verdict that leaves your C in the picture leaves the resemblance in the picture. So it escalates until there is nothing left to resemble.

**The loop has no interior stopping point.** Each act of suppression is a fresh perception for C to register, demanding fresh warrant; the only warrant available is a worse you. Strictly increasing, no fixed point short of a target with no C at all. Dehumanization isn't an excess of the process. It is its limit.

**This book is the announcement.** The tower in §2 was built without one, which makes it *implicit* common knowledge—and that predicts something. Say the thing aloud, in public, and the pretense stops working, because now everyone can see that everyone sees. No information is added; one kind of common knowledge is converted into the other. That is what this book is for, and the epigraph is the prediction stated in advance: *when it is recognized, it is dissolved.*

[In other words, if it is made (explicit) common knowledge that contempt is "the thing" (evil), then it loses its power. It becomes something that can be named.]

## Part Two: what's inferred, not verified

**The agent.** Something tracks, adapts, forecloses, and defends itself while staying unavailable to its host. Nothing requires that it *be* an agent; everything requires that it *function* as one. Inference to organization, not ontology.

**The teaching.** The encounter reproduces its preconditions in the target: C demoted, the demotion arriving disguised as maturity. Darkness doesn't just provoke darkness; it trains it.

**The two anchors.** *How else would they recognize my darkness for what it is? Why else would it so reliably empower theirs?* Two regularities, one candidate mechanism that accounts for both. Ordinary hostility produces neither.

**The arrangement.** Everyone knows; naming it is punished; the designated carrier keeps carrying. A stable equilibrium — and the mechanics add that it requires the carrier not to improve.

**The awareness objection.** *If it requires not-knowing, how was this written?* Limbo is availability turned away from, not inaccessibility. The confession is the turning-back, done on the page. The book's existence is a datum: the trap has an inside handle.

## Part Three: what I believe, marked

Now the claim I held back. I think C is not merely the same equipment in each of us but *one thing* — token-identical, not just type-identical. That when two bearers of C regard each other, it is one thing looking at itself in another form. That what the traditions call light is what it's made of, and that the darkness is a privation: an absence organized into a function.

**Can I show that this follows? No.** A shared evolved architecture for moral perception and mind-reading explains everything in §§1–7 without remainder. Two documents I've read argue otherwise — that the recursion's perfect connectivity *proves* a shared field of awareness, since separate minds couldn't reflect each other with such precision. I find it beautiful and I don't think it holds. It mistakes *same equipment* for *same self*. It may still be true that we are one; I think we are. But this road doesn't get there, and taking it would be exactly the move the book warns about: recognition dressed up as proof.

What I'll say instead is that something odd happened on the way here. The mechanics were worked out with no theology in the room, and they landed on a darkness with no perception of its own — an editor, every one of whose materials is borrowed from the faculty it suppresses; a force whose entire operation is a turning-away, which is to say an absence, maintained. That is the shape the privation tradition has been describing since Augustine. I went looking for a mechanism and found a hole with a job.

Whether the hole in each of us opens onto the same darkness, and the light onto the same light, is the question the rest of this book lives inside. The chapters don't need the answer. And if you felt the recognition, you already have the only evidence I ever claimed to offer.

**On the tower** — Lewis, *Convention* (1969); Aumann (1976); Schelling. Pinker, Nowak & Lee (2008), "The logic of indirect speech," on content conveyed *so as not to* become common knowledge — the trap is an innuendo whose subject is itself.
**On limbo** — Fingarette, *Self-Deception* (1969): self-deception as declining to *spell out* what you're doing. Sartre's bad faith; Mele as the deflationary rival.
**On the leak being functional** — Trivers; von Hippel & Trivers (2011); Kurzban (2010). Adjacent, with one inversion: here the leak is the payload.
**On the arrangement** — Girard on the scapegoat. Bandura, Haslam, Baumeister's *Evil* (1997) for the exterior view of the license, the terminus, and the myth of pure evil.
**On the shadow** — Jung.
**On privation** — Augustine, *Enchiridion*.
