# The narrative engine

*Stateless. What drives the book from the preface to its last page, and the rule for ordering chapters. The dags say what rests on what; this says what pulls the reader forward. A linearization of the DAG that doesn't follow this reads as a list of interesting ideas, which is the failure the drafts of Chapters 6 to 17 currently have.*

## The spine, in one paragraph

Goodness is what you are, and it was there before anything you did. Darkness is what happens when you turn away from it: not a second thing, but your own faculties run without the part that sees. Turning away hides from the one doing it by showing itself to everyone else, and what it shows recruits them, so it spreads. That is its purpose, not a side effect, and contempt works only because everyone knows it's the real thing. The message it carries, under every version, is not that goodness isn't real but that you get to decide what it means, so there's no North Star to have left. So the whole of the cure is remembering, and the only place that can happen is in you.

Every frame the book has tried is a lens on that paragraph, not a replacement for it.

## Two questions, braided

The preface asks one question and Chapter 1 asks another, and the book is the two of them converging.

**The preface's question: does what's in my skull matter?** The reader admits a flicker that wants the worst people to hurt, and the preface says the natural objection out loud: it's confined to your skull, so how could it matter? The preface promises that it isn't confined, and that the book will show how it gets out. This is the *reach* axis: how far does the thing travel?

**Chapter 1's question: what is this thing?** A glance passes between two strangers. The chapter is a detective story: what did he do, why, how, and what is it in him that did it. This is the *depth* axis: what is the thing made of?

These are the two indices of the spiral (`spiral.md`): the same claim at greater depth, and the same mechanism at greater reach. The train is the two questions getting bigger together, and the crescendo is where they become one answer: **it reaches everywhere because of what it is.** Nothing is confined to your skull, because the thing in your skull is an absence in one light, and the light is not divided. Every last thing you do matters. That's Chapter 16, and the preface's question is answered there at full volume, and nowhere before it in full.

What's wrong with the drafts is not their order.

## Why the drafts read as a list

Each of Chapters 6 to 17 was seeded from a section of Chapter 5, so each begins from a topic instead of from the previous chapter's last question. A chapter that begins from a topic is an essay. A chapter that begins from a question the reader is already holding is a chapter. Chapter 1 never once begins a section from a topic; every section opens on the question the last one left, which is why it reads as a train and nothing after it does yet.

## The two rules

**Intensity is the open question. Breathing room is the check.** A page stays intense as long as the reader is still holding a question they want answered. It breathes when the reader is sent to look at something in their own case. So never drop the question, and never skip the check. A chapter that holds the question and skips the check is the manic version. A chapter that gives checks with no question is the disconnected version. Chapter 1 alternates them every few paragraphs; that's the rhythm to copy.

**Every chapter opens on the last one's question and closes by raising the next one's.** The hand-offs, written out so they can be checked:

## The crescendo, and how it stays honest

Chapter 16 is the terminus, and it's marked as belief. It has to do two things at once: deliver the preface's answer at full volume, and not corner the skeptic. The way it does both: the reach claim ("every last thing you do matters, witnessed or not") is stated as mine, with the reason I hold it; the check offered is only the reader's reaction to the sealed room, which is not neutral and which nobody has to interpret my way. The chooser problem (a real choice at one point, no chooser after) is held the way the parasite is held: no creature there, and treat it as if there were. Dzogchen has held both for a thousand years, and that's said as a correspondence, not an authority.
