# The correspondence problem

*Why does the part that knows turn out to be right?*

---

## 1. The problem, stated properly

Chapter 1 establishes that one part argues and the other doesn't, and that the one that doesn't argue never budges and turns out to have been correct. That's an extraordinary claim and the book currently walks past it.

The question isn't rhetorical. It's a known problem with a name. In the ethics literature it's Sharon Street's **Darwinian dilemma**: if there are moral facts that hold independently of us, then our moral faculty was shaped by selection pressures that had nothing to do with tracking those facts, so any correspondence would be a staggering coincidence. Either the faculty tracks something real, and we owe an explanation of how, or it doesn't, and its confidence is worthless.

Your book needs the first horn. Everything rests on conscience being *right* — not merely sincere.

## 2. Why your current formulation doesn't survive

Your proposal: *goodness just is the reaching-toward, so the correspondence is guaranteed.*

Two problems.

**The sincere monster.** Someone can reach earnestly toward what he takes to be good and commit atrocities. On this account he's good, or at least not bad, which is false and which the rest of your book denies. You'd be forced to say he wasn't *really* reaching — and then "really reaching" is doing all the work and hasn't been defined. That's the No True Scotsman charge you already worried about, and here it would be deserved.

**Vacuity.** If goodness is constituted by the reaching, then goodness has no content of its own. It can't tell you anything, can't be gotten wrong, and can't ground the claim that contempt *is* evil. You'd have secured the correspondence by making it trivial.

The instinct behind the proposal is right. The formulation puts the weight on the wrong thing.

## 3. The fix: conscience is perceptual, not normative

Here is the move, and it's already latent everywhere in your book.

**Conscience is not a detector of moral properties. It is the perception of another's reality.** What it delivers is not "this act is wrong." It's *there is someone there* — a locus of experience, a being to whom things happen.

That reframing dissolves the correspondence problem, because it changes what kind of fact is being tracked.

1. Conscience's deliverance is a **factual** claim about the world: there is a subject in front of me. That claim can be accurate or inaccurate, and it usually is accurate, for the same unmysterious reason your other perceptions usually are: there really are other subjects, and you are equipped to detect them.
2. Given that the claim is accurate, the wrongness of harming them is **not a further fact requiring a second faculty**. It follows. To be a subject is to have a good — things go well or badly *for* you. Damaging a being with a good is bad-for-that-being. There's no extra spooky property to track.

So the "coincidence" evaporates. There is no gap between what conscience knows and what is the case, because conscience is not making a normative report that has to line up with a normative fact. It's making a perceptual report, and the normative follows analytically from the perceptual.

In the technical vocabulary this is a **third-factor** solution: both the deliverance of the faculty and the moral fact are explained by the same third thing — the reality of other subjects. No tracking miracle required.

## 4. Then what is "reaching toward"?

Your intuition survives, relocated. Reaching-toward isn't what *makes* an act good. It's the orientation of someone acting from accurate perception. It guarantees you're aimed at what's actually there. It does not guarantee you'll help.

This is exactly your compassion/wisdom split, and now it's derived rather than asserted:

- **Conscience (perception):** there is someone there, and this damages them. Truth-apt. Usually accurate. Cannot be argued with, because you can't argue someone into seeing.
- **Wisdom (competence):** what would actually help. Fallible, learnable, and not supplied by clean perception.
- **Reaching-toward:** acting from (1) without (2) settled. Guarantees you're not adding to the thing. Guarantees nothing about outcomes.

And this is what blocks the sincere monster. He isn't a good-hearted man with bad information. He is **factually wrong about what is in front of him**. He does not see the person. That's a perceptual failure, not a values disagreement — which is precisely why your book says it required a turning-away first. Sincerity was never the criterion. Accurate perception was.

## 5. This isn't ad hoc — it's what your book already claims

The test of a repair is whether it predicts things you independently believe. This one predicts six:

1. **Conscience never argues.** Of course not. Perception doesn't argue. You can't prove to someone that there's a person there; you can only get them to look.
2. **It can't define the good.** Of course not. Your eyes can't define red either. A sense isn't a definition.
3. **You can't do it while looking.** This is the strongest one. If perception were merely *evidence* about wrongness, you could see clearly and proceed anyway. The fact that clear seeing is *sufficient* to stop you shows that the perception isn't evidence for the wrongness — it's constitutive of your relation to it.
4. **Harm requires turning away first.** Necessarily, if harm requires the other's reality to be out of view.
5. **The dark part is an editor, not a perceiver.** It has to be, because there's only one perceptual channel and it's the one being suppressed.
6. **Evil is privation.** Failure of perception is an absence by definition. Nothing needs to be added to a person to make them capable of atrocity; something needs to be subtracted.

Six independent claims falling out of one repair is the sign that the repair is the thing that was always underneath.

## 6. What still isn't answered — and where it goes

The above is Tier II. It answers *why does conscience track the good* by making the good downstream of a factual perception. But it leaves one thing untouched:

**Why does the reality of another subject make a claim on me at all?**

That there is someone there is a fact. That their pain matters *to me* is not obviously entailed by it. A hard-nosed naturalist can accept everything in §3 and still ask why he should care, and nothing in Tier II compels an answer. This is the is/ought gap in its final form, and I don't think it can be closed from inside Tier II.

Which is exactly where your metaphysics earns its place — and note the shape of the argument, because it's stronger than an assertion.

Under nondualism there is no correspondence to explain, because there is no gap. Conscience recognizing life in another isn't one thing detecting a separate thing. It's one awareness recognizing itself, wearing a different face. The question "why does my perception of you line up with the truth about you?" only arises if we are two. If we aren't, the alignment isn't a correspondence at all. It's an identity.

**And here's the part worth stating explicitly: the correspondence problem is *predicted* to be insoluble from inside the assumption of separation.** If separation is the illusion, then any framework that assumes it will generate exactly this puzzle — a faculty that reliably knows something it has no business knowing, and can't say how. Which is what philosophy has in fact been staring at for three hundred years.

That's not proof, and it must not be presented as proof. It's the same standard you hold elsewhere: a hypothesis that dissolves a problem rather than solving it is worth noticing, and noticing is all you're entitled to.

## 7. Suggested placement

**Tier I/II** (probably Chapter 2, where conscience gets characterized): the perceptual reframing. *What conscience knows isn't "this is wrong." It's "there's someone there." The wrongness isn't a second thing it has to detect — it comes with the someone.* That's one paragraph and it strengthens everything downstream.

**Tier II** (near compassion/wisdom): the three-way split in §4, and the sincere-monster case as the reason perception rather than sincerity has to be the criterion.

**Tier III** (Part E, near the nondual argument): the dissolution, plus the meta-observation that the problem's insolubility is what the hypothesis predicts. Two paragraphs, clearly marked.

## 8. Key point, for scratch/key-points/index.md

**L1 — What conscience actually detects.** Conscience is not a moral rulebook or a detector of wrongness. It is the perception of another's reality — that there is someone there, to whom things happen. The wrongness of harming them isn't a second fact requiring a second faculty; it follows from the first. This is why conscience can't define the good (a sense isn't a definition), why it never argues (perception doesn't argue), why seeing clearly is *sufficient* to stop you, and why every harm requires a turning-away first. It also blocks the sincere-monster objection: the atrocity-committer isn't reaching toward the good and failing on competence, he is factually wrong about what's in front of him.

**L3 — Why there's any correspondence at all.** That another's reality makes a claim on me is not entailed by the bare fact that they exist. Nothing in Tier II closes that gap. Under non-separation there is no gap to close: conscience recognizing life in another is one awareness recognizing itself. The correspondence problem is what the assumption of separation generates, and its persistence is evidence of nothing except where to look.

# Addendum: the asymmetry revision

*Written after Aditya found two holes in §3 and §4. Both were real. The repair changes what conscience is taken to detect, and unifies two cases the earlier version handled separately.*

## A. The gap I glossed

§3 said: to be a subject is to have a good, so damaging them is bad-for-them. That yields **bad-for-them**. It does not yield **bad**, or **bad-for-me-to-do**. That's the agent-relative/agent-neutral gap, and I walked past it.

The repair, stated as an argument rather than an assertion:

1. I do not doubt that my own suffering is bad. It is the most immediate thing I know.
2. So either badness attaches to *this particular locus*, or suffering is bad as such.
3. If it attaches to this locus, name the feature of this locus that does the attaching.
4. There isn't one. Nothing distinguishes the loci in the relevant respect.
5. So it isn't indexed. Their suffering is bad in the same way mine is.

Premise 4 is where the weight sits, and it is exactly the premise that requires them to be **the same kind of thing you are**.

**Consequence worth its own paragraph: contempt is an attack on premise 4.** "This is all you are" asserts a difference in kind. Dehumanization isn't a failure of compassion with compassion as the thing lost — it's a strike at the premise from which compassion follows. This is why contempt is the master move rather than one vice among many, and why the verdict must escalate to *irredeemable*: only a different kind of thing is beyond reach.

## B. What conscience actually detects

The cheating case breaks the harm-detection model. No victim in view, no consequence calculated, no time to calculate one — and conscience fires anyway, instantly, without being able to say why.

What was detected was not harm. It was **the assertion of asymmetry between self and other**. Harm is what asymmetry produces downstream.

This accounts for every awkward feature at once:

- **Fires with no victim.** The asymmetry is fully present with nobody in the room. (Cheating. Desecrating a corpse.)
- **Fires before calculation.** Nothing is being computed; a relation is being perceived.
- **Can't say why.** What it perceives is a relation, and our explanatory vocabulary is built for outcomes.
- **Can't be argued with.** You can't argue someone out of a perception, only get them to look.
- **Universal and untrained.** Everyone reads it in everyone, immediately, with no instruction.

## C. The unification

**Cheating and contempt are the same move from opposite ends.**

- Cheating: *I am more.*
- Contempt: *you are less.*

One faculty detects both. That's why the same silent knowing shows up in a test you cheated on and in a stranger's gaze — and why the reader of Chapter 1 can move between the two halves without noticing a change of subject. There isn't one.

It also makes the book's central identification close to analytic rather than a moral verdict: **contempt is the pure, maximal form of the thing conscience exists to detect.** Not "contempt is very bad." Contempt is the thing itself, undiluted.

And it is where the Buddhist frame attaches without being imported. Self-regard as the root of suffering isn't a separate doctrine laid alongside this. The asymmetry *is* the self-illusion in operation: every exception made for oneself is the claim to be a privileged locus, enacted.

## D. The monster, resolved in Aditya's terms

My §4 said the monster's *perception* is broken. Aditya's version: he isn't genuinely reaching, he's telling himself a story of how he is. These are one event from two sides — the story is what maintains the not-seeing.

And Aditya's formulation has the non-circular test I claimed it lacked: **genuine reaching doesn't require hiding.** That's Chapter 1's entire apparatus, and it's checkable from outside. Keep it. It's better than the perception-only version because it names the thing that can actually be observed.

## E. The abduction

The faculty has this profile: fires on victimless asymmetry-assertions; fires before calculation; cannot explain itself; present in everyone; cannot be argued out of anyone; read accurately in others without training.

A cooperation-tuned social module would track consequences and reputational risk. This one doesn't — it fires where there are none. So the better explanation is that it isn't a moral module at all: it is **the perception of a fact about the relation between self and other — that there is no privileged locus.** Which is the residual perception of non-separation, arriving in the only form a mind that believes in separation can receive it.

Abductive, not deductive. The deflationary rival (an overgeneralizing heuristic misfiring on victimless cases) is available and should be named. What the rival struggles with is the *accuracy* of the cross-reading: strangers detect it in strangers, instantly, and are right.

## F. The Chapter 1 demonstration

Run the cheating case backwards, and don't supply the answer:

> You knew it was wrong. You hadn't calculated anything. There was nobody in front of you being hurt. So what exactly did you know?

Let them fail to answer. Then:

> You knew you had made yourself an exception.

Then the turn, which is what the chapter should be built toward:

> That's the same thing you detected in the stranger. He was making himself an exception too — judging you for what he was doing. You read him in two seconds, using the faculty you'd spent the previous hour refusing to point at yourself.

Same detector, both directions, no metaphysics required. It converts Chapter 1's two halves into a single mechanism.

## G. Revised key points

**L0 — What conscience detects.** Conscience does not detect harm. It detects the assertion of asymmetry between self and other. Harm is what asymmetry produces. This is why it fires with no victim present (cheating, a desecrated corpse), before any calculation, without being able to say why, and identically in oneself and in others.

**L0 — Cheating and contempt are one move.** *I am more* and *you are less* are the same assertion from opposite ends, detected by the same faculty. Contempt is that assertion in its pure and maximal form — which is why "contempt is evil" is an identification rather than a rating.

**L1 — Contempt attacks the premise, not the feeling.** The badness of another's suffering follows from their being the same kind of thing you are. Contempt asserts a difference in kind. It doesn't suppress compassion; it removes what compassion follows from. Hence the necessary escalation to *irredeemable*.

**L1 — The test for genuine reaching.** Reaching toward the good is genuine when it doesn't require hiding. The monster's isn't fake by stipulation; it's fake because it cannot survive being looked at.

**L3 — What the faculty is.** A cooperation module would track consequences. This one fires where there are none. Best explanation: it is the perception that there is no privileged locus — non-separation, showing up in the only form a separated mind can receive it.
