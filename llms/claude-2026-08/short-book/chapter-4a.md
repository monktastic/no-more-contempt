# The Picture I Use

*A note before this one. Almost everything up to here came with a check you could run. This doesn't. It's the picture I use when I step back from one mind to all of them, and I hold it as a picture. None of the checks in this book depend on it, and if it doesn't fit, leave it; the rest stands without it.*

Take the nudge from Chapter 1 and multiply it. Two halves of a country drive each other to extremes over a generation, millions of tiny nudges each way, many of them mine, knowingly, until the behavior on the far side is so grotesque that the only explanation left is Chapter 1's alien quality called "evil."

Under the nudges runs the low-grade infection from Chapter 3, in billions of people at once and in every feed, each turn too small to notice: a background hum of darkness. Everyone caught in it (all of us, to varying degrees) carries it to work: to the office, the county building, the boardroom. An institution does what all those small turns add up to, and nobody in it feels responsible.

Corrupt leaders meet a hidden need in all of us. Their visible wrongdoing lets us establish our own relative goodness by contrast, while providing cover for our smaller betrayals: _At least I'm not like them. I'm just sticking it to the man!_ If integrity were the norm, in our institutions and our leaders, our small self-deceptions would stand out, and we'd feel the pressure to look at them. When the people at the top are corrupt, my own misdeeds feel minor by comparison, and easier to get away with. Every time I claim they're a different kind of thing, part of me is helping keep them there.

So we help produce the "bad people" whose actions justify our contempt; all of us build that same division into institutions; and those institutions provide new objects against which our contempt can feel righteous. Everyone experiences themselves as fighting the darkness while the darkness reproduces itself through everyone.

It goes further than institutions. The darkness can't win a head-on fight with the light. It wins by usurping it, taking its place, and that's the same thing as our forgetting there was ever a real light to steer by. We never stop wanting to orient to it; we just progressively forget what "it" is. You can only choose the counterfeit once you've forgotten what the real one feels like, the way you can only choose junk food once you've forgotten real food. Our own thoughts just come to feel like the ones that decide what's good.

Chapter 1 showed that at the scale of one mind, when my justifications felt more righteous than my conscience. Now picture the same thing gaining in every mind at once. What culture would it build? One that raised the part that proves as high as it could go and quietly demoted the part that knows: across all of us, what the locker room built across ten men. That is what I see in the culture most of us were schooled in. The part that proves is honored from the first day of school, and it has earned the honor. The part that knows isn't attacked; it just stops counting as knowledge. Religion is no shelter: a creed can stand in for the part that knows as easily as a theory can. Nobody designed it. Everyone gets something from it, and that's enough.

This is, I think, where the "evil global conspiracy" archetype comes from. People are sensing something genuine: coordination with nobody coordinating, the same hidden state operating through everyone. Real conspiracies also exist; people meet in rooms and plan harm and hide it, and those rooms have to be fought as what they are. What's being sensed underneath them is the background hum, which supplies those rooms with people willing to sit in them, and supplies the rest of us with the certainty that we'd never be among them. And the archetype's own history is the warning. It is right about the shape and wrong about where it lives. The moment it puts the coordination in a people, a cabal, a face, it has become the thing it was sensing, and that misplacement has filled graves.

The license from Chapter 3 comes at the scale of a team too. When the other party looks to me like a conspiracy, I'm half right: the same hidden state really is operating through all of them. What I feel then is the presence of evil in a whole group of people, and my side takes the license. What I don't see is that the license goes to the same thing in us. When we use it, we become the coordination I was sensing in them.

Chapter 3 said that when you send the verdict to the "bad person," the darkness in him gains and so does yours, each better hidden from its host. So the darkness in every one of us benefits from the same thing, a darker world in which each has more cover, and faces the same way: from the same light, toward the same end. And there's nothing else to tell one from another. The darkness has no substance of its own; a turning away has no qualities. Two rooms differ in everything the light shows; the dark in one is the same as the dark in the other. That's why looking into the eyes of two "evil" people can give the uncanny sense of looking at the same thing, and why more than one tradition that looked hard ended up naming an adversary. Mara. The Devil. I'll borrow that name too when it helps, since it's the one everybody knows. They were seeing something real. It has a bottomless appetite. And in this picture it is the coordinator: not a plan, not anyone, but the background hum seen as one thing, the same interest in every mind, building the same cover, until the one orientation every one of us steers by is the one thing none of us believes is there. Where those pictures go wrong, when they do, is in giving the evil a substance of its own. The careful ones didn't. So there's a paradox to hold with both hands: it has no being of its own, and it must be fought. Treat it as an essence and you get contempt. Treat it as harmless and you get complicity.

And the old name comes with an old observation about how it gets in, which is the one from Chapter 1: never as the bad kind, always as the good kind.

> *And no wonder, for Satan himself masquerades as an angel of light. It is not surprising, then, if his servants also masquerade as servants of righteousness.* —2 Corinthians 11:14–15 (NIV)

You can hear the same move in the language. *Righteous* means being in the right, and it has also come to mean the contempt that's sure it is. One word now names both the light's side and its counterfeit, which is what usurping looks like from outside.

It doesn't care who you hate, as long as you hate. King's line from Chapter 2, that darkness cannot drive out darkness, has a reason behind it: it was never trying to. Provoking the darkness on the other side is how it stays hidden.

Step back once more, and look at it from the widening Chapter 2 did. What looked out of your enemy's face was alive. So was what looked out of yours, and one refused the other. And the same refusal is in everything that is eating the world. A man held as a thing, a forest as nothing but lumber. None of it needed a monster. Each needed only a living thing held as stuff, by something alive that had turned from what it is. How can almost none of us be monsters, and the world still be tearing itself apart and eating the biosphere it stands on? It doesn't take monsters. It takes the background hum.

The Cree have a word for this: wetiko, the cannibal spirit, a hunger that eats life and is never full. The word is theirs, and it carries more than I'm using it for. Jack Forbes, a Native American scholar, said wetiko was the disease the conquerors brought with them; he watched what it did to his people. Paul Levy, a writer who has spent his life on it, reads it as a mind-virus: it passes from person to person, hides inside its host, and gets its host to defend it. I know the word only through the two of them, and I borrow it because it names what this book's parasite does. I think it's what the verse above was pointing at, and that you may have looked at it too, the last time you held your enemy in contempt: small in your eyes, lasting a second, and the same thing all the way up.

It is anti-life. Not a rival life with a plan of its own; it has no life of its own. It is life turned against itself, eating itself, and calling the meal good. Hunting for it in an enemy is one of the ways it hides. In those moments, look for the turn in your own eyes.
