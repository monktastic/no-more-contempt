## Meera (46, labor-and-delivery nurse, Goenka Vipassana; cold, "the ice")

Dear Aditya,

I read this on the couch after two twelves in a row. That's probably the worst condition to read anything in, and also the one most of your readers will be in. I did the exercises as I went, with a pen. Here's what happened, in order.

**The opening.** I bought it tentatively, which is what you asked. I liked being handed "Hold them tentatively, until you see the picture they assemble." The central claim I didn't understand yet, and that was fine.

**The memory.** The one that came wasn't dramatic. About six years ago I had a patient in triage, first baby, early labor, a laminated birth plan, declining the vitamin K and the hep B before anyone had offered them. She put her call light on four times in an hour. The fifth time I sat at the station and finished charting that didn't need finishing, and let it ring. When my charge nurse said "Is 12 okay?" I said "She's fine, she just wants an audience." She was fine, as it turned out. That isn't the point.

Did owning up feel like new information? No. It came up two years later on day six of a ten-day course, sitting in the hall, the way old things do there. What came with it wasn't news. It was recognition, like finding my own handwriting on something. So your first claim I bought, for this memory. As a meditator I'm less sure it's true of everything; some things come up on the cushion that I don't think were ever available to me in the day. But you didn't claim everything, and the call light was available. I knew while I let it ring.

"If you did, did that happen passively?" No. "She just wants an audience" was chosen. It was quick, and it had a little edge of humor in it so the charge nurse would laugh and not walk down the hall. That's where I put the pen down for a minute.

"If seeing that makes you want to call yourself names, hold off. That's not owning up; it's another way to hide." Good. That's what we're taught: don't react, just see it. You said it in one line and moved on, which is right.

"Did that knowledge arrive before or after you'd reasoned it through?" Before. And here's something you don't say that I'd want to tell you: I knew *where* it was. It was in my stomach, a pull low and to the left, while my hands were typing. The reasons were all up in my head and moving fast. "Why did one side have to argue, and the other not?" I liked that question very much. It's the only kind of question I trust: one I can answer by looking. My answer was that the one in my stomach didn't have to argue because it wasn't trying to get anything.

**The cashier.** I used the woman at the Kroger on Kenny Road who wears her reading glasses on a beaded chain. The smile went right away. The sneer didn't. It stuck, like a drawer. Then, and this is what interested me, my mind handed me a reason without my asking: she's slow with the coupons. She isn't, particularly. So the cruelty wanted a reason before it would go, and when it didn't have one, it made one. Bought.

**Remorse.** I tested it, because I've seen men in the family lounge ashamed of having cried, as if tenderness were a failure. But that's shame at being seen soft, not "that sickening sense of having gone against myself." I couldn't find the reverse. Bought, tentatively.

Then "under the right circumstances, I'm capable of the worst acts humans have committed." I've done the intake on a woman seven months pregnant whose boyfriend waited in the hall. I don't love hearing that sentence from someone before I know what he's seen. But I didn't refuse it. I've had the thought myself, at three in the morning.

**The encounter.** The hot version isn't mine. I don't call people out. But it was believable as what a mind does, and the best of it was the rationalizations that sound reasonable: "I'm only holding him to account, and if he reacts poorly, well, it only proves my point." That's real. And "it's really more like an *invitation*" got me, because I've told myself the patient explanation I give anti-vaccine parents is an invitation. There's nothing inviting about it either.

Where I skimmed: "*Ha!* Classic." and "It's still a net benefit to the world!" Those felt like you doing a voice. The mind I watch doesn't exclaim. It's faster and quieter and more reasonable than that; it sounds like a charge nurse. "Surely that's not what I'm doing. I'm just being fierce with evil, right? Right." That one I kept, because it's short and it has the little relief in it.

Then the cold version: "a quiet filing-away, a politeness with nothing behind it, and a small satisfaction at being done with them." My husband Vikram calls it the ice. It's what I do with his mother. I'm flawless with Saroja. I bring the right sweets, I call on her birthday, I ask after her knees. She has never once been able to complain about me, she knows it, and I know she knows it. So when you told me to read the rest with the cold one in mind, I did, and almost everything fit better that way.

**"I'll just go ahead and name what's happening."** Insight, mostly. One paragraph I had to read twice: "Neither half alone would be enough to get me what I need: both his reaction and my innocence. If it felt accidental, he might forgive or pity me instead. If it looked self-aware, then his accusation would have somewhere to land, making *me* the bad guy." The first time it was a logic puzzle. The second time I read it with Saroja in it and it was simply true. If my politeness were sloppy she could pity me. If I were openly cold she could tell Vikram. It's the perfection that does it, because it says I know exactly what I'm doing and gives her nothing to hold. "The signal is the pretense that there is no signal." I underlined that twice. That's the ice in ten words.

"If you've been on his end, you know how it closes around you." I have, and I know who taught me the ice. It was her, twenty years ago, when I went back to nights after my son was born and she said nothing, beautifully. I had never put those two things side by side before.

"My stories kept changing; my conclusion never did." Yes.

**The corruption passing between minds.** Bought, because I could feel the hypocrite quote from the inside. "*What a hypocrite.*" and "why the hell shouldn't I?" I've said nearly that to myself about attendings.

**The two checks.** Is there something satisfying about seeing evil punished? Yes. That boyfriend: security walked him off the unit in cuffs, and I felt it in my chest, warm, and I looked at the other nurse and we didn't say anything and didn't have to.

The buttons, with him. My first move was the pill, and I was pleased with myself for about four seconds. Then I noticed I wanted the pill to work completely, so he'd see what he'd done, all of it, clearly, for the rest of his life. I wanted his heart changed so it could be broken. That's the first button wearing a cardigan. I wouldn't have caught it if you'd told me what to find. You didn't; you said "I'll be honest: there is in me." and left it with me. That was the freest I felt in the whole chapter.

"And that lie is exactly what lets me act on it, in ways I almost never recognize." Tentatively. I didn't yet see how.

**Whose intention.** This is where I first refused. "If it weren't, I'd have been embarrassed or confused when he called me on it. Instead I doubled down" — I watch people get called on things in the worst hour of their lives, and when they double down it's often not intention. It's a cornered body. Heart rate up, vision narrows, you hold your position because moving feels like falling. Embarrassment and confusion aren't the only things a mind does when it isn't intending. Fear does it too. So that argument didn't work on me. But I could still follow, because I didn't need it. My call light was intentional without any doubling down to prove it, and "Conscious yet unaware: that's the limbo state." matched what I already had.

"It acts as if it has goals of its own." I didn't hear a devil. I heard what Goenka calls sankharas: old reactions laid down over and over, which keep their own momentum and seem to want things because they keep producing more of themselves. Then you said right away "Don't mistake the grammar for the claim" and "It's my mind, and I answer for what it does." That came in time. A paragraph later and I'd have started to slide, because when people start talking about things with goals living inside us I usually stop listening.

The villain-or-machine paragraph is the best thing in this section for me. "When I'm sure someone was fully aware of what they were doing, I've made them a villain. When I'm sure they had no idea, I've made them a machine." I do both, by department. The obstetricians are villains: they know exactly what they're doing when they talk over a woman at eight centimeters. The anti-vaccine parents are machines: programmed by Facebook, poor things, and I explain slowly, the way you'd reinstall software. I hadn't seen that the slowness was contempt. I thought it was the opposite.

**The bad essence.** Honestly, the first two paragraphs read as you heading off an objection. "I think badness is the result of turning away, not its cause." is a good sentence and would have been enough. But this one caught me:

> I don't do it because I'm a bad person. I do it because I believe there *are* "bad people"—people who are fundamentally defective. And I only do that because, at a level I cannot let myself see, I fear I might be one of them.

Last spring a nurse I was precepting, Kayla, asked to switch to someone else because I made her "afraid to ask anything." Since then there's a fear I don't look at, which is that I'm a hard person and not just a hard nurse. Reading that, I could see the fear makes me more correct with people, not less.

**"What does "yourself" mean here?"** I tried. I closed my eyes with the question. What I found isn't quite what you point at. I didn't find a thing. I found the breath at the upper lip, the heaviness in my legs from the shift, and something aware of both. Not a self; I've spent too many courses watching "I" come apart into sensations to find one there. But the knowing was there, and it was the same knowing that sat in the hall on day six. So I found something, but I'd call it awareness, and I'm not sure it's what you mean.

"Why would you have to hide something from *yourself* in order to keep it from your better nature?" This one worked. Because they're the same, or close enough that you can't hide from one without hiding from the other. "did it feel like *losing* something of yourself, or like getting something back?" Getting something back. After day six I slept properly for the first time on that course.

**What we are.** "our better nature isn't one part among others. It's a description of what we *are* in the absence of all self-deception." I granted it as your claim. It didn't sound grandiose, exactly; "not as some grandiose idea, but as a direct experience" protects it. But my tradition would argue with the word "are." I could get as far as "what's left when nothing is being pushed away," which is close. Something later moved me further toward you; I'll come to it.

**The gaze.** The one who came was Dr. Hollis, years ago, the night I questioned his Pitocin order. He looked right at me and there was no one behind it. Yes, I could feel the sense in which he was absent. "Why would he have to turn away at all, if what he is, is essentially bad?" I tried to answer, and what came was: because he couldn't have done that to me while looking. Which means something in him would have stopped him. I didn't want that answer. I like Dr. Hollis as a villain. But it came. The question pushed me, I'll admit; it's built to land in one place. I got there by looking, though, so I'll let it stand.

"Is it possible that you look a tiny bit like this when treating him with contempt?" Yes. The eye-roll to the other nurses over a doctor's head is the same flatness, turned sideways so it's deniable.

"In moments where you're feeling fully whole" — yes, it's easier. After a morning sit the shift goes differently, and I know it. On mornings I skip, I'm the ice by noon.

**Integrity.** The person who corrected me with integrity was Dolores, my charge nurse my first year, when I missed a run of late decels because I was charting. She pulled me into the med room and was fiercer with me than anyone has been before or since. I didn't dig in. I went back in the room. It felt exactly like "*you're better than this*," except she said it out loud. Bought, completely.

"Can you remember a time when you corrected someone in a nasty way, but then later, with more years of wisdom, took an entirely different approach to the same problem? One that worked better?" I tried. I have the first half twice: Kayla, and my son Arjun, who tells me I "sound like a nurse" when I'm angry at him. I don't have the second half yet. The question assumes I've already grown. It didn't corner me, but it skipped over me, and I felt a little behind.

"It's said that integrity is what you do when no one is watching." and "But someone always is: me." That landed. For me what watches is the body. The stomach knew about the call light.

**Common knowledge.** I followed it, and this is the section that surprised me most. "how could the part of me that was provoking him be so sure of his reaction?" I had never asked that about the ice. Of course I'm sure. I'm sure Saroja will read it because she's the one who taught it to me, and she's sharp, and I'm counting on that. The middle, "he knows *I* know he can read it (so that it reads as a signal and not a slip), and so on" — I followed it, but it went abstract, and "and so on" is where I drifted. The hypocrisy brought me back: "He charged me with hypocrisy, and I knew his charge was valid. That only makes sense between two people who answer to the same thing." That's clean.

"In the opening, were you surprised that I knew you needed limbo to cause harm? If not, why not?" I read this twice and it still feels like a trick. It felt like you'd turned around and pointed at me.

Where someone's contempt reached in me: Saroja, the nights. It didn't hit my pride. It went for the place that knows I'm a good mother, just under the sternum, the same place I'd have to leave to hurt someone. You're right about where it was aiming.

Then: "**Love and contempt are addressed to the same place.** One says *I know you're in there.* The other says *there's nobody in there*, and they both say it to the somebody who is." I stopped reading for a long time here. When a woman in transition says she can't, that she's done, that she's dying, I put my face close to hers and say, "You're still here. I see you. You're right here." I say it twenty times a month. I had never once thought that it goes to the same place the ice goes. This is what moved me toward your "what we are." I know that place in other people. I aim at it professionally, and I'm good at it. I didn't have to find it in myself as an idea. The last time I sent the look: Monday, to Tasha, over Dr. Hollis's shoulder, while he talked over a patient who was trying to tell him she felt pressure. Did I know Tasha would read it? Yes. An out? **Length.** If I had to cut a fifth: the exclamations in the encounter; two of the three bad-essence paragraphs; the "hall of mirrors" paragraph and the "If not, why not?" question; "My justifications are believable only to me and my allies," which I read twice and couldn't hold onto; and shorter versions of "Most contempt is quiet" and "When I can't see someone's better nature." Everything else you give me through eyes, tone, the little satisfaction, and I can feel those. "Darkness" I can't feel; it's a word from a sermon. Also "combo," which sounded like a menu. What became newly consequential is that the ice and the eye-roll are signals I send on purpose, to people I'm counting on to read them, and that "I'm only being accurate" is a verdict. That changes my week. Tomorrow I'm on with Hollis. If he talks over someone, I'm going to say it to him, not to Tasha: "She's telling you she feels pressure." Fierce, and to his face.
