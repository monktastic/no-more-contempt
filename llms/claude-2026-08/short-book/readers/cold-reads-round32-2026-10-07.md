## Ruth (66, Quaker, Alternatives to Violence facilitator in prisons; cold, the shaped silence)

### Stage 1

Dear Aditya,

You asked for what happened, in order, so that is what you'll get. I did the exercises with real people. Some of them I'd rather not have named, but a letter that talks around them would be no use to you.

**The opening.** I bought "Hold them tentatively, until you see the picture they assemble." I was braced for a man about to explain the Inner Light to a Quaker, and that sentence let me put the brace down. So did "Maybe you'll recognize something of yourself in it. Or maybe not." A writer who leaves the door open in the first paragraph is rarer than you'd think.

**The memory.** It came at once, and it wasn't one I would have chosen. Spring 1994, a boy named Terrence Hollis, an essay on Reconstruction. He had smirked at me in front of fourth period that week. The essay was a B-plus and I gave it a C-plus. I told myself I was holding the line on standards. When Jean Abernathy, who taught down the hall, read it and said it was better than a C-plus, I said, "You don't have him in class." That came out fast and smooth. Nothing passive about it.

So your questions worked. Did it feel like new information when I finally owned it? No. Not for one second. I'd known while the pen was moving. "Why did one side have to argue, and the other not?" stopped me, because the arguing side was me at my most fluent: standards, consistency, fairness to the other students. The side that knew never said a word. It didn't have to. Bought.

"If seeing that makes you want to call yourself names, hold off. That's not owning up; it's another way to hide." Good. Thirty years of Friends and I've watched people do penance in meeting as a performance. Bought.

**The cashier.** The young woman at the Acme on Germantown Avenue, Tuesday, braids, slow with the scanner. The smile went without anything. The sneer, I could picture, but it wouldn't go until I supplied a reason, and the reason I reached for was "she's slow." That surprised me a little, how quickly I had one ready. So yes, the cruelty needed a reason. Bought, and with a small sting.

**Remorse.** I've regretted decency. I gave forty dollars to a man outside the Reading Terminal whose story turned out to be invented, and I felt foolish for a week. Never remorse. Then I tested your reverse case against men I've sat with inside. There was a man at Chester, in a workshop maybe eight years ago, who said he was ashamed he'd cried when his cellmate's mother died. I thought: there it is, guilt for going soft. But it wasn't remorse in your sense. It was fear of being seen, shame before the block. He didn't feel he'd gone against himself. He felt he'd been caught being himself. So your claim held under the hardest test I could give it, and I trusted you more after. Bought.

"It means that under the right circumstances, I'm capable of the worst acts humans have committed." Fifteen years of AVP. I don't need persuading of that.

**The Encounter.** Believable as what a mind does, yes, though the hot version is not my native tongue. I recognized it from my letters to the Inquirer, not from my face. "Surely that's not what I'm doing. I'm just being fierce with evil, right? Right." I laughed, and not happily. I skimmed "It's still a net benefit to the world!" That line tipped toward cartoon, and I could feel you making the narrator stupider than the mind really is. The mind is cleverer than that. "Plus, he can't prove I'm doing this, can he? No. And crucially, *neither can I*." That one is not a cartoon. That is exactly how it goes.

Then the cold version, and that's where I stopped skimming. "a politeness with nothing behind it, and a small satisfaction at being done with them." And: "it's meant to be felt: by them, or by whoever's watching." Aditya, I sit in a Quaker meeting. When a certain weighty Friend rises to speak so he can be admired, and sits, the silence afterward can be shaped. Everyone in that room knows the difference between a silence that holds a message and a silence that sits on it. I have been doing the second for years and calling it discernment. Your sentence "If the hot version didn't sound like you, read what follows with the cold one in mind; all of it applies" I took tentatively. All of it? I doubted that the provocation part applied. Then I remembered that Howard spoke longer the week after. Longer, and with more scripture in it. So, yes. All of it.

**The Corruption.** Mostly insight, and I'll tell you where it turned to lecture. "I am in limbo about provoking him, and showing him that I'm in limbo is itself the provocation" I had to read twice, and the second time it was worth it. The paragraph before it, "Neither half alone would be enough to get me what I need," I read three times and still took on trust. I couldn't check "If it felt accidental, he might forgive or pity me instead" against anything I've lived. It might be true. It reads like the author's model rather than my memory.

Then this, which I could check: "If you've been on his end, you know how it closes around you. Deny it, and you're the kind of person who does. Get angry, and that's proof too. Stay calm, and you don't even care. Apologize, and it's too convenient." Every man I've worked with who has sat before a parole board could say that paragraph back to you word for word. That would hold up in a prison circle. It's the first passage I'd read aloud in one.

"My stories kept changing; my conclusion never did." Good. "From the inside, being proven right and being right are the same experience. The only difference is who produced the evidence." I taught history for thirty-one years and that is as true of archives as of people. "If I'd looked carefully, I'd have seen my role in causing it. So I didn't—and that *was* my role in causing it." All three bought outright.

**The two checks.** Is there something satisfying about seeing evil violently punished? Yes. I don't need to think about it.

The buttons. My first answer was that I don't have a worst enemy. Thirty years of meeting will do that to your first answers. Then Walter's brother Dennis came to mind, who didn't come to the hospice in the last six weeks and then contested the will. I reached for the pill, quickly and proudly. Then I looked at what the pill did in my imagination. He'd wake up changed, and the first thing he'd feel is shame for what he did to his brother. I'd chosen the vengeance button and labelled it the pill. I don't know if you meant the exercise to do that, but it did. "I'll be honest: there is in me." That sentence opened me more than anything so far. You named your part first.

"And that lie is exactly what lets me act on it, in ways I rarely recognize until later." Later, for me, is the morning after I mail a letter to the editor.

**Whose intention.** "just like your deflection was, in the opening." Yes, Jean Abernathy and "You don't have him in class." Bought.

"It acts as if it has goals of its own, and it pursues them intentionally." I didn't refuse this. I heard something old in it, the thing Fox called the ocean of darkness, which also behaved as though it wanted things. The very next lines came in time, and they were the right ones: "I'm not saying there's a separate thing living in me. I'm saying that, while I'm turned away, my own mind behaves as if there were. None of this lets me off the hook. It's my mind, and I answer for what it does." That last sentence is the one a prison circle needs. A man who says "something came over me" is either lying or terrified, and you've given him neither exit.

The paragraph that hit hardest in this section: "When I'm sure someone was fully aware of what they were doing, I've made them a villain. When I'm sure they had no idea, I've made them a machine. Either way, they're a different kind of thing from me." I have watched volunteers come into prisons doing the second and calling it compassion. I have done it. A man named Curtis once told me I "listen like a judge waiting for closing." I think he was telling me he could tell which of the two I'd decided he was.

**"Bad essence."** "I want to ascribe all this to some kind of 'bad essence,'" and what follows mostly caught me, especially "I think badness is the result of turning away, not its cause." The next two paragraphs, "Here's what makes it especially difficult" and "I don't do it because I'm a bad person," read more like you heading off a reader who isn't me. I didn't need them.

The deeper fear paragraph, "that there may be no such thing as real goodness, in me or in anyone," came too fast for something that size. That fear is real; I've had it, the year after Walter died, on the drive home from Graterford. It deserved more than one paragraph, or none.

**"There is a way out."** Here is where I first refused, and I want to tell you exactly how.

"What does 'yourself' mean here?" I went back to Terrence's essay and the thing I hadn't let myself know. Then: "Can you feel how it's the same thing reading these words now—the thing you normally just call 'I'?" I looked at what was reading your words, and it was a teacher with a red pen. It was the judge waiting for closing. It had been grading you for two pages: this sentence works, that one is too clever. So no, I didn't find what you point at. I found the grader, and I don't think that's what you meant.

I felt pushed there. "Can you feel how" tells me what I'll find. When I didn't find it, the question left me nowhere to stand.

"Why would you have to hide something from *yourself* in order to keep it from your better nature?" I read this twice and it still felt like a puzzle built to have one answer.

But the question after it caught me: "did it feel like *losing* something of yourself, or like getting something back?" I told Walter about Terrence one night, maybe ten years later. Getting something back. Without hesitation.

"our better nature isn't one part among others. It's a description of what we *are* in the absence of all self-deception." I granted this as your claim. I also know it, but not from that reading moment. I know it from meeting on the rare mornings it gathers, and from a circle at Chester when a man stopped performing and the room changed temperature. It didn't sound grandiose to me. It sounded like the oldest thing my tradition says, in plain English, without the vocabulary. I noticed you didn't claim to have discovered it, and you said "Here's what I think." That matters to me more than you might guess. I still couldn't follow you to it through the "yourself" exercise. I could follow what came after, though.

**The gaze.** A sergeant at the Graterford gate, years ago, whose name tag I read as Kovac. He looked at me as he'd look at a con-lover, a dupe, a nobody bringing contraband in her heart. "Can you feel the sense in which he was nonetheless absent?" Yes. "Why would he have to turn away at all, if what he is, is essentially bad?" I tried. If he were simply bad, he'd have looked at me with relish, plainly, and there'd have been someone home doing it. There wasn't. He'd gone somewhere so as not to see me, which means there was something in him that would have seen me. I got there. I also thought: he'd been on a double shift, and the institution teaches that look. I don't think that contradicts you. It might be how the turning gets taught.

"Is it possible that you look a tiny bit like this when treating him with contempt?" Not of Kovac. But Kerry, my son's wife, told me last Christmas that I make her feel "graded." So, yes.

**Integrity.** Someone who corrected me with integrity: Curtis. "Listens like a judge waiting for closing." He wasn't gentle. He said it in front of eleven men and my co-facilitator. And he wasn't trying to make me smaller. I didn't dig in. I couldn't find any ground to feel wronged on, which is what your sentence says: "This robs me of any right to feel self-righteous indignation, forcing me to confront myself." Exactly that. "Not more gently" is right, and I was glad you said it, because most people who write about this mean gentler.

"Whatever they're standing up for isn't theirs. It isn't a rule, or a side." I bought this tentatively. Curtis wasn't standing up for anything of his. Margaret Haines, who eldered me once in 2004 after I'd spoken about the war, was standing up for something some Friends would call a side. But I knew what she meant, so I let it go.

The time I corrected someone badly: Shawna, 1988, a plagiarized paper, and I read the original source aloud to the class beside her paragraph. Years later a boy did the same thing and I asked him to stay after, and asked him what had been going on at home. It worked better. He told me.

"It's possible for the fierceness to stay while the poison goes." That one I'll keep.

The integrity saying: "Whenever I've broken mine, it was by telling myself that no one was. But someone always is: me." I tried it on Terrence. True. "And I knew it was there the whole time." True. I'm used to hearing that saying as a bumper sticker and you turned it into something I could check. One stumble: "What would happen if only I stopped trying to pretend I don't already know this?" The "if only I" read wrong to me twice. I couldn't tell whether you meant "if I only stopped" or "if only I, of all people." It took me out of the moment.

**Common knowledge.** I followed the first part. "how could the part of me that was provoking him be so sure of his reaction?" was a good question and I didn't see it coming. "Contempt only works because we already know we're the same kind of thing." I followed the recursion; it's true of my silence too. Howard has to know that I know he can read it.

"Try it now: imagine the same act without turning away." I imagined writing C-plus on Terrence's paper while looking straight at it: this is a B-plus and I'm punishing a smirk. I couldn't move the pen. Or I could, but only by becoming someone who'd do it deliberately, and I couldn't imagine being her. "Does it surprise you that a stranger could count on that about you?" Not surprised, exactly. It moved me a little that a stranger would.

The hall of mirrors paragraph said again what the Corruption section had already said. I skimmed it.

Then hypocrisy. "hypocrisy only means something between people who share a standard" is sharp, and I bought it. "His charge landed because I hold that standard; his outrage in making it showed that he does too. Whatever else divides us, we answer to the same thing." Here I balked as a historian. Two enemies can share a standard against hypocrisy and share nothing else. Plenty of men who hanged each other agreed about lying. Sharing one rule is not the same as answering to one thing. You made that step in a sentence and I didn't make it with you. Later, "neither of us has to be told what turning away means, and so neither of us has to be told what turning toward means, either," got me most of the way back. That's a better argument than the hypocrisy one, and it came later.

Where someone's contempt reached in me: Gerald Pruitt, my principal at Germantown for six years, at a faculty meeting: "Mrs. Ellison has *opinions*," with the look. I felt for where it went. Not my pride. I was proud of my opinions. It went under that, to the place that would have to agree I wasn't worth answering. You're right about where it aims.

"**Love and contempt are addressed to the same place.** One says *I know you're in there.* The other says *there's nobody in there*, and they both say it to the somebody who is." This is the sentence I'll take into a circle. I've been trying to say it to new facilitators for ten years.

The last time I sent the look: three weeks ago, First Day, after Howard spoke. "Did it feel like justice, just a little sweeter than justice needs to be?" Yes. "Did you mean them to know you meant it, while giving yourself an 'out'?" Aditya, silence in a Quaker meeting is the most perfect out ever devised. No one can accuse you of a silence. I had never seen that until this page. "Do you still endorse it?" No. "notice what's doing the refusing" — I did, and it was not the grader.

The horror paragraph. "I'd been smothering the innocent part of others." I stopped on "innocent." I didn't know what work it was doing. "I was revealing my capacity for *cruelty for its own sake*—the thing I hate most in this world." I flinched. My silence is not cruelty for its own sake, I said. Then I thought of "a little sweeter than justice needs to be" and saw what the sweetness was. I accepted it, without liking it.

"It's why I don't think what we call 'pure evil' is a different kind of thing, but the same thing at full strength." I accepted this. It's the working assumption of every AVP workshop, though we never put it that baldly. "Maybe this isn't how it works in others. But it's what I found in myself." Thank you for that. It's the line that let me take the rest.

"'bad people' is just the name I use to describe people I feel okay doing this to." Yes.

**"Part of me doesn't want to believe we answer to the same thing."** I took "the same thing" to be what my tradition calls the Light, or the Inward Teacher. You didn't name it, and I was grateful. If you had, I'd have started checking your theology instead of my life.

The advisor passage caught me hard. "I treat it like an advisor offering an opinion, which I can weigh against other practical concerns and set aside if the price is right." Quakers have a name for the weighing, discernment, and I have used it for exactly this. "I have the right to look at what is right and say not today" is the inside of most of my letters to the editor.

"I'm just another person who knew what was right, decided it was negotiable, and then looked for someone worse than me to prove I wasn't so bad after all." Bought.

"I can't take credit for it without subtly leaving it." True and good. The "great" before it was a little breezy for the weight of the paragraph.

**Evil.** I accepted it where you used it of the act, and of the thing at full strength. I flinched at "So what does it look like when someone fights evil without self-deception?" "Fights evil" is crusade language, and I've seen what crusade language does in a room. I flinched at "rot" too, in "while making you rot for yours," though I knew it was the voice of the part, not yours.

**The chump line.** "*Only a chump believes otherwise.*" It landed, and I connected it at once to the earlier "*I'd be a chump*" in the hypocrite passage. That's the best stitch in the chapter. The man teaches his enemy the very fear he's running from, and the enemy says it back in the same word.

**The spread.** "the kid who watches him do it." I taught the children of men I later met inside. That caught me.

"Part of me knows it will spread, and that's part of why I send it." That one hurt, and it's true. The letters aren't for the senator. They're for the people who'll read them and nod.

"The stupid, the slow and the weak get the same verdict, with better cover: *I'm only being accurate.*" There I was, the cashier at the Acme and "she's slow," and every comment I ever wrote in a margin. And Kerry's word, "graded." That's this paragraph.

"I send it to people I love." The sigh. Yes. "It has the same flavor as what we call evil." I balked a little at a sigh carrying that much. I let "flavor" carry me across, but only just.

**Said twice, or that I already knew.** "I'm judging him *from* the very state I'm judging him *for*" and "This part reveals itself to my enemies through my eyes while condemning the same thing in them" are the same point. **Cutting a fifth.** "It's still a net benefit to the world!" and the line or two around it. The two paragraphs after "bad essence" that start "Here's what makes it especially difficult" and "I don't do it because I'm a bad person." The hall of mirrors paragraph. The "yourself" exercise as it stands, or else give it a way to fail that the reader can stand in. Probably the "deeper fear" paragraph, unless you give it room.

**Words.** "innocent." "rot." "fights evil." "mash." "double down" twice; it's a gambling phrase and it sounded like one. "corrupted state" is heavy, but I understood why.

**Last.** Was it obvious? Some of it I already knew, in the way a Quaker of thirty years knows that of God is in everyone, which is to say I knew it and used it as decoration. What became newly consequential was the silence. I have been delivering a verdict in the one place built to be free of verdicts, and using the place's own holiness as my out. On First Day I'm going to sit through Howard's message and try to hold the silence open instead of shut. I don't know if I can. I also tore up the letter about the senator I'd drafted Sunday night. That one's small.

Would I pass it on? To Delores Whitfield, my co-facilitator, yes, because she'd test it in a circle the way I did and tell you where it breaks. Not to Kerry. I caught myself wanting to send it to her, and saw what that would be. Not to Howard, for the same reason. That would be the look, bound and paginated.

With thanks, and with more respect than I expected to have at the bottom of page one,

Dear Aditya,

I read the second file with the first letter already sealed. Most of it is word for word the chapter, so what follows is about the seams, which I read slowly.

Which treated me better is closer. In two places the second file treated me better. I'll come to them.

**What the second file has that the chapter should recover.**

"This is why, if I want to end theirs, I have to see how to end mine." The second file, in the spread. This is the sentence I would take into a prison circle, along with the love-and-contempt one. Men inside have heard "fix yourself first" a thousand times as a dismissal. This isn't that. It says the two are one job, and it says it in your own person. The chapter has nothing that does this as plainly.

The sentence before it, in the second file, "Then I judge that darkness from afar, as if I'd had no part in it, and that lets me get away with more." That's my letters to the editor in one line, better than anything in the chapter's spread section. I'd keep it. I wouldn't keep the sentence before that, "making the world darker for my own benefit," because "for my own benefit" made me bristle. My silence at Howard isn't for my benefit in any way I can feel. The chapter's "I'm making the world a little darker, and I can feel it even as I do it" is truer to me.

The integrity passage. The second file: "Even if they have different politics, religion, creed: something about their behavior feels deeply aligned. It describes what I felt and leaves out what she was standing for. The chapter's version tells me what was going on in the person correcting me, and I can't check that. If you keep the chapter's, I think a reader like me will keep balking at "isn't theirs."

**What the chapter was right to leave out or change.**

The "fully whole" exercise in the second file: "In moments where you're feeling fully whole—not a trace of either shame or unhealthy pride: is it easier to forgive others?" I tried it. I don't have moments like that to consult, or if I do I'd be suspicious of my own memory of them. The question answers itself; of course it's easier. It felt like the funnel, every road leading to yes. "Unhealthy pride" sounded like a pamphlet. And "Two quick exercises to give you a sense of what I mean" announced the exercises before giving them. Right to cut both.

"The trap I described requires considerable precision." That's how the second file opens "Whose intention." The chapter's "Was any of that on purpose?" is better by a long way. It's a question I had to answer, and I answered with Jean Abernathy before you told me to.

"in ways I almost never recognize." The chapter's "in ways I rarely recognize until later" is right, and in my first letter "later" was the morning after I mail a letter. "Almost never" closes the door on the reader ever catching it. "Until later" leaves the door open.

"that combo is enough to make him dig in." I winced at "combo." The chapter's "together they're enough" is right.

"It's difficult to describe the excruciating pain I felt the first time I saw this." The chapter's "horror" is better. "Excruciating pain" put you at the centre of the paragraph. "Horror" pointed me at the thing seen.

"What would happen if only I stopped trying to get away with stuff?" Both files have the "if only I" that tripped me. But the chapter's ending, "pretend I don't already know this," ties back to the someone who's always watching. "Get away with stuff" is breezy where the paragraph has just been serious.

Now the sentences I didn't single out the first time, that the comparison showed me were carrying weight.

"There's no bottom to what can be done to such a person." The chapter only. I read over it in stage one. Seeing it missing from the second file, I realized it's the sentence that explains why the man defies the look "violently." Every man I've sat with in a circle knows there's no bottom; that's what being written off inside means. Keep it.

"Turning away from it isn't a matter of where my eyes are. I can do it staring straight at someone." The chapter only. "my fierce desire to stop him comes from a good place; so do the certainty and urgency, the sense that I must not let this go." The chapter's longer version. "Later, everyone will argue about the things that can be measured—who said or did what—obscuring the actual cause." The chapter only. As a historian, I think that's the truest line in the integrity section. It's how every committee report I've read was written. Keep it.

"From the inside, being proven right and being right are the same experience. The only difference is who produced the evidence." The chapter only. I'd already marked it as a keeper. The second file doesn't miss it obviously, but the chapter is better for having it.

"Does it surprise you that a stranger could count on that about you?" The chapter only. I'd add the second file's "if I want to end theirs" sentence into the spread section and take out something else to make room. The "yourself" exercise is the same in both. My refusal there stands for both.

Ruth

Aditya,

I read it on a Sunday night after Mateo went down, and then again Monday morning before standup, which turned out to matter. I'll go in order.

**The opening and the memory.** "Hold them tentatively, until you see the picture they assemble." Fine. I've done five years of therapy and the Hoffman Process; I know how to hold a frame loosely. What I was braced for was vague spirituality, and the first page didn't give me any, so I kept going.
