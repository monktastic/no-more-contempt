## 2. READY?

**Preface and Introduction: READY.**

No major objections. The warning ("Nothing here is a verdict on you.") is the right promise to make, and the rest of the book mostly keeps it. Naming the plant medicines up front is honest, and this group will respect it.

**Chapter 1: READY.**

No major objections. This is the best thing in the book and it's ready. **Chapter 2: CLOSE.**

One major objection.

- In "What I'm claiming," under "Nobody chooses evil under that name": "Judging them is the same evil, since it takes turning away from them to make it." Say "Passing that verdict on them is the same evil," or cut the sentence. (I'd cut the whole entry anyway; see below.)

**Chapter 3: CLOSE.**

Four major objections.

- **The book contradicts itself about the darkness's goal.** In the parasite section: "What it wants is what Chapter 1 found: to stay hidden from the one it lives in." Six pages later: "The darkness has one goal, and Chapter 1 showed it at the scale of one mind: to make us forget there was ever a light to steer by." Two different "one goals," both credited to Chapter 1. A careful reader will catch this, and it costs you on the very claim you most need trusted. Either make the second the means of the first in one sentence, or cut the culture paragraph, which I'd do anyway.

Then: "That's why "they need the most love" isn't sentiment, and it doesn't mean anything soft." But a hurt reader can skip the note's exit ("let this part be about someone else"), and the hostage image is what stays. Put one sentence in that paragraph: seeing the hostage is how you hold him in your mind, never a reason to go near him or get him out.

- **The footnote exercise.** "Try to imagine, from the inside, being *maximally* turned away from a person." **Chapter 4: CLOSE.**

Not quite as it stands. My circle is Sufi, mostly women, several of them out of marriages like mine. After three changes I'd send it gladly.

1. **Take the excuse out of the parasite, and the gag out of the claims list.** Change "So this wasn't me doing something wrong." and "Judging them is the same evil" as above. Those two sentences are the ones a controlling husband would underline, and the ones his wife would take as an order.
2. **Remove the specimens and the false "never".** Replace the factory-farm worker with the reader's own case, and either cut the culture paragraph or limit "never named" to secular schooling. A religious reader and a working-class reader should not each find the one place the book does to them what it says not to do.
3. **Give the darkness one account, and fix the coordinator.** Cut the institutions-and-culture paragraphs in Chapter 3 and the three paragraphs in Chapter 4. That leaves one statement of its goal and one statement of how it wins. Rewrite "it is the coordinator Chapter 3 said there wasn't" so that no one can lift it out as a conspiracy line.

One addition, not an objection. You name Mara and the Devil and leave out the clearest scriptural case for your thesis. In the Qur'an, Iblis's refusal is contempt in its first recorded form: *I am better than he; You made me from fire and him from clay.* He looked at Adam and saw only the clay. Your first sentence in Chapter 1 is his sentence. The Sufis built a whole teaching on that one-eyed seeing, and what they called the *nafs* that commands to evil is very close to your parasite: it borrows the self's voice and is fought by being seen. Your Muslim readers will notice it's missing. One line in the Picture would do.

This is a true book, and in places a beautiful one. It found my mother-in-law in me, which nothing else has managed in fifteen years.

With respect,
Ayesha
