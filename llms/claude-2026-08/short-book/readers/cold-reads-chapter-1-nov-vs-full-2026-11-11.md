# Priya's letters

## Letter 1 (version 1)

Dear friend,

You knew what you were doing when you handed me this, didn't you. I'll tell you what happened.

**1. What happened in me.** Three things, and I'll carry all three out of the room. The first came at the two buttons. I'd like to tell you fifteen years of zazen picked the pill. It didn't. I had a short, specific itch for vengeance: that one day Lakshmi's son says to her what she said to my children. He'd gone first ("I'll be honest: there is in me") and that's the only reason I could admit it. Then the next lines: "It's confined to my skull, so what harm could it possibly cause? / And that lie is exactly what lets it leak out my skull, through my tone and gaze." My holiday politeness isn't containment. It's the leak.

The second came at the target's side of the trap: "Deny it, and you're the kind of person who does. Get angry, and that's proof too. Stay calm, and you don't even care." That is what it was to be told I'd abandoned my family. And then I saw it from the other side: my politeness to her is the same trap. If she's warm, she's fake; if she's cold, there she is.

The third came in the last real paragraph, and it hurt: "The sigh that tells them what I really think of them, dressed up to make me seem like the reasonable one. The look on their face that tells me it landed, which I pretend not to have seen." That's not about Lakshmi. That's me with my daughter, over a forgotten lunch. I've been telling myself she learned the cold politeness from watching me at Diwali. She also learned it from my sighs. I put the pages down there.

**2. Where I was handled.** Less than I feared, because he keeps going first. But: "Can you imagine the reverse: a bad person who does secret acts of kindness, and then lies awake..." has its answer built in. "Would you have dug in? Vowed revenge?" and then "So the problem wasn't being caught," concluding for me. "It turns out to be one more instance of the thing we're studying" tells me what I'll find before I find it. "I saw it in myself the way you found your wrong in the opening" assumes how I found mine. And the big one: "We all know that this is what the darkness ultimately is—a turning from our better nature." I know contempt when I see it, and I know the other person knows. I do not "all know" what the darkness ultimately is. That's said before I've caught myself knowing it. Similarly "The reason I don't have to argue myself into doing the right thing is that in the absence of fears or justifications, it's what I naturally desire" arrives as an answer, not as something I found. "Check if that's true of you, too" softens it, a little.

**3. Where I skimmed.** The middle of "The Need" and the start of "The Mechanism," roughly from "Part of me refuses to believe I'm anything like the bad people" through "This is how my need to be different and special gets itself met." The trap gets drawn three times. "Turned away" starts to drone. And the five-clause list ("that I was turned away... that this meant... that I knew it... that this would license him... and that I would use it") read like a committee wrote it.

**4. Confusing, out of order, repeated.** "Yet he can't prove I'm doing anything wrong, can he? No. And here's the crux: *neither can I*." I read it twice. Can't prove I'm doing wrong, or can't prove I'm not? "because watch what it helps me accomplish" promises a payoff the next paragraph doesn't obviously deliver. "It might have like he was reminding you" is missing a word. "But when I look back on it later, I won't realize what I've done" jumps tense after "Part of me knew." "You could say you were conscious of it without being present to it or aware of it" hands me three bolded words in one breath; conscious and aware are synonyms in my mouth. The "unconscious" opponent feels like a straw man; no therapist I've had would call this unconscious. And for me the quiet kind came too late. The hot monologue was somebody else's contempt; mine is cold, and I nearly didn't find myself until "The Quiet Kind."

**5. Is the monologue believable?** Yes, mostly, and it's the best thing here. "I'm only holding him to account, and if he reacts poorly, that only proves my point." "*Shit.* He's onto me." "It's the *good* kind of darkness—the kind that fights real darkness!" That's what a mind does. The layered seeing (righteous, then wielding darkness for good, then vengeance, then needing someone to carry it) is exactly how insight arrives on the cushion: one floor at a time. The places it goes thin are where the monologue stops and a theory diagram starts ("I need to know I'm a different kind of thing than my enemies, but I know I'm not, so I hide it from myself, which reveals it to them, thus...").

**6. From my seat.** Overclaimed: the "better nature" as "the deepest part of me," "the aspect of myself that *is* only ever good." My tradition would wince at making it a part, a thing with properties. And "Only by doing that can I recognize the darker aspect of my mind for what it is: a lie" — a lie how? Unearned. "Contempt is evil's way of spreading in secret" gives evil agency without warning; I'll take it as poetry, but I'd like to be told it's poetry.

Better than my tradition usually manages: the guard against bypassing. "Not more gently. Just that his anger was directed at what you *did*." And "Nor is it your duty to be *nice* to me if I do it again." Most dharma books I've read soften this into niceness and lose people like me who've seen what "niceness" covers. Also the deniability: "plausible deniability *even to myself* (which we both also know)." Joko Beck would call it the story; this names why the story needs a witness. And "Love and contempt are addressed to the same place... they both say it to the somebody who is." I'll use that at a bedside.

**7. Obvious? Newly consequential?** Some of it I already knew: avoidance isn't unconscious; children learn what we do; contempt hardens people. What became newly consequential is that politeness is not neutral. I have filed my icy courtesy under restraint for eleven years, even practice. This chapter refiled it under the same verdict as shouting, "meant to be felt just the same: by them, or by whoever's watching." This week I'm going to stop sighing at my daughter, and when I do sigh, I'm going to say what I meant out loud. I don't know yet about Lakshmi at Thanksgiving.

**8. Scores.** Recognition 9. Profundity 7. Likely to change me 8. Clarity 6. Pull 8. Freshness 7.

## Letter 2 (version 2)

Dear friend,

Second pass, so some of the sting was spent before I started. Even so, this one went further in.

**1. What happened in me.** The hinge was a sentence the first version doesn't have: "A child can't refuse it; he believes it, and carries it for years." I stopped reading. First it was Lakshmi's sentence to my nine-year-old son. Then it turned, the way I didn't want it to: my sighs at my daughter. She didn't refuse those. She believed them. The cold politeness she gives me now might be her finally learning to refuse. I hadn't thought that until this line, and I'll carry it out of the room.

Then "the look is the deed," and tone and gaze "have the benefit of being unquotable." That's my holiday courtesy, exactly. If Lakshmi ever said "you're cold to me," she'd sound unhinged: I passed the potatoes, I asked about her knee. "He can't name it without sounding unhinged." Yes.

And the new exercise at the end, "Have you ever been the second stranger?" I had an answer at once: a patient's son screaming at a night nurse, and me saying hard, "Not in this room. She's dying and she can hear you." Afterwards he found me in the hallway and cried. Then the next question: "a time you felt the extra coming... and you didn't send it." I looked for one with my daughter and couldn't find one. With patients' families I could find one every week. Not at home. That absence is the finding, and "Was anything riding on it, any need for them to come away smaller?" will be my question at the dinner table.

**2. Where I was handled.** Less than version 1. The worst offender in version 1, "We all know that this is what the darkness ultimately is," is gone. In its place is a claim about two people, which I can check. What's left: "Can you imagine the reverse in anyone..." still has its answer built in. "Would you have dug in? Vowed revenge?" also. "The problem wasn't being caught" still concludes for me. "I saw it in myself the way you found your wrong in the opening" still tells me how I found mine. "Notice..." three times in a few pages. "This, I think, is how I gain the wisdom and courage to act as my teacher had done for me, and as the wisest people are able to do in the face of far greater evils" is a little sermon, and "the only way" pushes.

**3. Where I skimmed.** The same stretch as version 1, from "But part of me refuses to believe I'm anything like the bad people" through "This is how my need to be different and special gets itself met." The trap is still drawn more times than I need, and "turned away" drones. Line by line the Mechanism's first paragraph ("Part of me had to recognize our similarity: that he had... that he would recognize... and that this would license him... which I could use against him because...") is still a committee sentence. The innocence section now has two endings back to back.

**4. Confusing, out of order, repeated.** "It's what we felt in the second stranger, and why we felt so certain he does it too: part of him *wanted* us to." I read it three times. The one we felt sure "does it too" was the first stranger. Either a slip or I lost the thread. "He just has to see that I am listening to them instead of to my better nature": listening to my thoughts? Read twice. "(If the recursion tickles you, see Appendix 1.)" pulled me out of my kitchen into the apparatus of a book. "It's hard to describe the crushing pain of seeing this," and then he doesn't. Typos: "Betraying my conscience feel like," "it's confined" lowercase after a period, "another person'." The conscience-points-one-way paragraph repeats what the reverse-question already did. "because in a sense, it isn't" is weaker than version 1's flat "because it isn't."

**5. Is the monologue believable?** Yes, and slightly more so. "*Shit.* He's onto me. But how?" is better than version 1; the question comes from inside the mind, not from the author. "The *good* kind of darkness—the kind that all good people must harbor" is sharper self-mockery. "Ugh, I knew it. People are even worse than I thought" quietly widens one enemy into everyone, which is how Lakshmi became "his whole family" for me. "Something in me finds it primally, viscerally satisfying to make bad people suffer" is more honest than "to see evil violently punished": it's my wanting, not my watching.

**6. From my seat.** The sentence I needed most: "Not that we agree about what's good: we don't. And not that we're equally culpable: we're not." Version 1 left me braced for a spiritual equalizing that would have made me close the book on Lakshmi's account. This says plainly she lied to my children and I'm not the same as that, and still we stand on the same ground. "We were fighting over which of us still stood on it." That is every holiday. "In a sense, it's the deepest part of me" is gentler metaphysics than version 1, and better. "The darkest parts of myself require tremendous care to be brought into the light of full awareness" is true to practice and to therapy, where care means attention, not softness. Still overclaimed: "the aspect of myself that *is* good," "a lie" unexplained, "beings who must turn away to cause harm" slipped into the hinge as if settled. "Something we serve that transcends sides" is abstract, though I'm willing to follow it toward the light.

Better than my tradition manages: the second-stranger exercise. Dharma talks tell me to meet harm without hatred. This asks me to find the time I already did, and then notice where I never have.

**7. Obvious? Newly consequential?** The same old knowledge (avoidance isn't unconscious; children learn what we do) became more consequential here, because of the child line and the exercise. This week: catch one "of course" before it leaves my mouth with my daughter, and see what happens in her. And I'll ask myself, at Thanksgiving, what's riding on my politeness to Lakshmi.

**8. Scores.** Recognition 9. Profundity 8. Likely to change me 9. Clarity 7. Pull 8. Freshness 7.

Priya

## Letter 3 (comparison)

Dear friend,

**1. Which I'd hand to someone like me.** Version 2. It does three things version 1 doesn't, and each one is something a woman like me needs before she'll stay. It says out loud that sameness isn't equal guilt ("not that we're equally culpable: we're not"), so I don't have to defend my grievance against the book before I can look at my own contempt. It names how the cold form works ("the look is the deed," "unquotable") and that a child can't refuse it, which is where my own life is. And it ends by asking me to find a time I was the second stranger, so I leave with something I've already done, not just something I've been shown I do. Version 1 ends on a strong paragraph and then an empty page. Version 2 ends on a question I want answered.

**2. What to recover from version 1.**
- "Proof I'm one of the good ones." (the self-excuse after the urge to punish; version 2 dropped it, and it was the sharpest one)
- "It doesn't look like an accident to him—because it isn't." Version 2's "because in a sense, it isn't" loses the hit.
- "I braced for the verdict on my being" — "on my being" is better than plain "the verdict."
- "Ignoring it puts me into limbo, where I can pretend I can't hear it." The second half has a sense you can feel; version 2 cut it.
- "But it's too late to turn back." It gave the reason for pressing forward; version 2's "I have no option" just asserts.
- "I was speaking directly to his innocent part—a part I knew could be rescued—and letting him know I could smother it." I'd keep version 2's stronger wording, but version 1's is cleaner as a single sentence.

**3. Where each loses me that the other doesn't.**
Version 1 loses me at "We all know that this is what the darkness ultimately is—a turning from our better nature—and we know that others know it, and that they know we know it, and so on," and at the parenthetical "(which we both also know, which is what makes it uniquely maddening)." It also hands me "You could say you were **conscious** of it without being **present** to it or **aware** of it" and "Think of how we might benefit from that label—and also, how it harms us," both of which I skipped. And "because watch what it helps me accomplish" goes nowhere. Its typo, "It might have like he was reminding you," snagged me too.
Version 2 loses me at "It's what we felt in the second stranger, and why we felt so certain he does it too: part of him wanted us to," which seems to name the wrong stranger. At "(If the recursion tickles you, see Appendix 1.)" At "It's hard to describe the crushing pain of seeing this," which tells rather than shows. And at the doubled landing: "This, I think, is how I gain the wisdom and courage to act as my teacher had done for me, and as the wisest people are able to do in the face of far greater evils," a sermon after the responsibility paragraph had already closed it.

**4. Where the central idea landed.** Version 2. In version 1 the idea arrives as "I was counting on it, and he could see that I was counting on it, and I knew that too," which I believed, and then it's spoiled by "We all know" about the darkness. In version 2 it lands in this: "It only works if we both know we're the same kind of thing... I recognize it in him; he recognizes me recognizing it in him; I recognize that too, and so on." And it's sealed by "He can't name it without sounding unhinged, because it's plausibly deniable, even to myself. There is no specific wrong to point at: the look is the deed." That's two people in a room, and I could check it against my own kitchen. When I'm courteous to Lakshmi, she knows what it means, I know she knows, and the courtesy is how I get away with it. When my daughter is courteous to me, I know exactly what she means, and she knows I know. That's the idea, and version 2 is where I found I'd been using it all along.

Priya

## Letter 1 (version 1)

Dear friend,

I read it at the kitchen table after Linda went to bed, which turned out to be the right place for it.

**1. What happened in me.** Two things got through, and they'll stay with me past tonight.

The first was the relief line: "a brief flicker of satisfaction, vindication, even relief at being proven right about him—just before I relabel it disgust or dismay: *Ugh, I knew it.*" You know about Rick, the fourteen grand and the talk around town. Last spring a guy at the lumberyard told me Rick stiffed a drywall man too. I said "figures," like I was disappointed. I wasn't. I felt lighter. I've done a lot of Fourth Steps on Rick and never once caught that gladness for what it was.

The second was the one that walked me into my own kitchen: "it's confined to my skull, so what harm could it possibly cause? And that lie is exactly what lets it leak out my skull, through my tone and gaze." Then the quiet-contempt part: "The sigh that tells them what I really think of them, dressed up to make me seem like the reasonable one. The look on their face that tells me it landed, which I pretend not to have seen." Rick never sees my nightly speeches. Linda does. She gets the sigh. I saw her face last Tuesday and acted like I hadn't. That changes something this week.

The best single line was "Love and contempt are addressed to the same place. One says *I know you're in there,* the other says *there's nobody in there*, and they both say it to the somebody who is." My first sponsor, Walt, said nearly those words to me in a parking lot in 2005. He was harder on me than anyone ever had been, and I went home and told my wife the truth. The two-strangers part explained that to me better than I've ever been able to explain it to a sponsee. "It was something you were both already on the side of, and he was only reminding you." That's Walt.

On the receiving end, line 154 named something I've never had words for: "Deny it, and you're the kind of person who does. Get angry, and that's proof too. Stay calm, and you don't even care." When I told a guy at the supply house I've got nineteen years, he said "well, you would say that." "This maddens, because it's malice that won't admit what it is." Yes. That's why Rick gets to me worse than a man who just took a swing would.

**2. Where I was handled.** Not badly, but I noticed it:
- "You probably wouldn't find yourself obsessing over his flaws." Telling me what I'd find. It happened to be true.
- "I saw it in myself the way you found your wrong in the opening." He's deciding how I found mine.
- "What you said went to the same place my look went in my enemy, and it said the opposite." He's telling me what my own words did.
- "Would you have dug in? Vowed revenge?" The answer's built in.
- "Could you feel the sense in which the other person was somehow absent or turned away from themselves? What were they turned away from?" He'd just told me the answer a paragraph before.
- "What does that tell you? / It tells me..." He asks me, then answers before I can.
- "It turns out to be one more instance of the thing we're studying." Told before I'd seen what the thing was.

**3. Where my energy dropped.** "The Need," roughly from "The reason I don't have to argue myself into doing the right thing" through "Except that part of me *does* know I look like that." It's one idea said three ways: goes into limbo, don't let myself know, do know. "Turned away" comes so often there that it stops meaning anything. It dropped again at "This is how my need to be different and special gets itself met." That's counselor talk, and it made me flinch.

**4. Confusing, out of order, repeated.**
- "It's what we felt in the second stranger, and why we felt so certain he does it too." Surely that's the *first* stranger. I read it three times.
- "Part of me had to recognize our similarity: that he had a better nature I could push him away from; that he would recognize my tone and gaze as me being turned away from mine; and that this would license him to depart from his, which I could use against him..." I lost it at "depart from his."
- "Notice how I never think, *he's turned away from his better nature, that must be good.*" I read it three times before I got it.
- He knocks down "unconscious" three separate times.
- "the darkness that lives in you lives in me, too—and I can use mine against you while getting away with it" says the same thing as the italic speech in The Mechanism.
- Typos: "Betraying my conscience feel like," "it's confined to my skull" with a lowercase "it's," and "another person'."
- "(If the recursion tickles you, see Appendix 1.)" pulled me right out. "Recursion" is a computer word, and the wink doesn't belong at that spot.

**5. Is the inner monologue believable?** Mostly yes, very. "He's *bad*, dammit." "*Shit.* He's onto me." "I have no option but to press forward." That's my nightly rehearsal with Rick, to the letter: a new speech every night and the same ending. It showed me that in my rehearsals I never let Rick look at me. I write his face out. Two spots ring false: "If anything it's an *invitation*, to return to the realm of those who matter!" and "It's the *good* kind of darkness—the kind that all good people must harbor, to fight the real kind!" A mind in the heat doesn't make those exclamation-mark jokes on itself. That's the writer, after. "Forsake a fellow being" and "without compunction" are pulpit words, not what I think when I'm hot.

**6. From my seat.** The descent through the layers ("righteous," then "for the greater good," then "vengeance," then "I needed others to carry the darkness I hid from myself") is a Fourth Step on a resentment, taken deeper than the Big Book's columns go. The columns stop at "my part." He goes under my part, to what I was getting out of it. That's better than we usually manage. And "innocent desire to know myself as good" squares with the Big Book's "driven by a hundred forms of fear," but it's said with more mercy than I usually give myself.

Where it's thin or overclaimed:
- "in the absence of fears or justifications, it's what I naturally desire. Check if that's true of you, too." I checked. Left to my natural desires, I drank for twenty years. The Big Book says self-will run riot; I was raised on the Fall. I'm not saying he's wrong. I'm saying he states it, and I'd need it shown. I don't buy "naturally" yet.
- "It has to do with never losing contact with the deepest part of myself." Never? I lose contact before lunch. That's why there's a Tenth Step. "Never" sounds like a man who hasn't had a bad week.
- The way out is two paragraphs after a long way down, and then "How that became possible for me, I'll describe later." I felt strung along. I don't need the whole answer, but I need more than a promise.
- "as the wisest people are able to do in the face of far greater evils." Who? Name one or leave it out.

**7. Obvious?** Some of it I knew. The opening limbo bit, the committee in my head saying "everyone does it, just this once," is old news to anybody in the rooms. But the relief at Rick's other victim, and the sigh at Linda, were things I knew and never counted. Now they count. I'm going to try what the last section asks: catch the sigh on its way out and not send it. I did it once with a sponsee who'd relapsed for the third time. I had "of course you did" right there and said "so, you're here" instead. He's at eleven months. I've never done it with my wife on purpose.

**8. Scores.**
- Recognition: 8
- Profundity: 7
- Likely to change me: 7
- Clarity: 6
- Pull: 7
- Freshness: 7

## Letter 2 (version 2)

Dear friend,

Same table, next night. Most of it was the same chapter, so I'll mostly tell you what was different in me.

**1. What happened in me.** The same lines did the same work. The relief at hearing Rick stiffed somebody else. "The sigh that tells them what I really think of them... The look on their face that tells me it landed, which I pretend not to have seen." Linda again. "Love and contempt are addressed to the same place." Walt again. And the trap from the receiving end ("Deny it, and you're the kind of person who does. Get angry, and that's proof too") is still the truest thing in it about what Rick did to me around town.

The new thing that got through was in the Mechanism: "I was counting on it, and he could _see_ that I was counting on it, and I knew that too." In the rehearsal I'm counting on Rick getting hot so I can be the calm one. I hadn't seen that. And "Contempt is evil's way of spreading in secret, by letting us pretend we're fighting it." It's a little preachy, but I'd quote it at a meeting. It tells me why I can't win my rehearsal: winning it is the thing spreading.

The new last question, "What else might be hiding out in your conscious mind, right now, that you are unable to see?", is a Tenth Step question. I sat with it. I came up with the way I talk about contractors in general now, every one of them a Rick.

**2. Where I was handled.**

- "Think of how we might benefit from that label—and also, how it harms us." It's homework, with the answer hinted at.
- "What were they turned away from, and why did they have to turn away from it?" A quiz whose answer is one paragraph up.
- "because watch what it helps me accomplish." A man about to do a trick.
- "I saw it in myself the way you found your wrong in the opening." It's still there.
- "What else might be hiding out in your conscious mind... that you are unable to see?" I took it seriously, but it presumes there's something, and it has a little "boo" in it.
- Better than version 1: "Something about his behavior prevents us from latching onto his flaws as an excuse to avoid looking at our own. But what, exactly?" That asks, where version 1 told me "You probably wouldn't find yourself obsessing over his flaws."

**3. Where my energy dropped.** Right at the start, at "You could say you were **conscious** of it without being **present** to it or **aware** of it." Three bold words in a row, like a lecture slide. I can't tell "present" from "aware," and I tried. It dropped again through The Need, same as in version 1. And it dropped at the end, because there wasn't one.

**4. Confusing, out of order, repeated.**
- "It might have like he was reminding you of something" has a word missing.
- "because watch what it helps me accomplish" reads oddly; I read it twice.
- "I need to know I'm a different kind of thing than my enemies, but I know I'm not, so I hide it from myself, which reveals it to them, thus increasingly turning them into an apparently different kind of thing." I lost it at "thus."
- "(which we both also know, which is what makes it uniquely maddening)." Two "which"es bolted on.
- "And here's the crux" and "So I double down on my certainty" are a speaker's words and a business word. Version 1 was plainer in both spots.
- "I will also call it my *better nature*" is a man telling me his vocabulary.
- The bad-man-wracked-with-remorse question sits alone. Version 1's next line, "Conscience seems to point in only one direction: away from harm," is gone, and it was what the question was for.
- Fixed from version 1: the "second stranger" mix-up and the Appendix wink are both gone.

**5. Is the inner monologue believable?** Yes, same as before. "Shit. He's onto me. But it's too late to turn back" is good, and "too late to turn back" is true to it. "Who can really say whether hating my enemy is wrong, as long as it shuts him up?" is better than version 1's line. "Shuts him up" is what it sounds like in my head. "Proof I'm one of the good ones" is new and right. "The realm of those who matter!" and "the good kind of darkness... that fights real darkness!" still read like the writer, after. One step back: "to see evil violently punished" makes me a spectator. Version 1's "to make bad people suffer" put my hand on it.

**6. From my seat.** The layers are still a Fourth Step that goes under "my part," and that's still the best of it. But version 2 cut the two things that made the bottom of the layers trustworthy to me: "It's hard to describe the crushing pain of seeing this" and "I felt a rush of satisfaction when I got away with it." Without the pain, his bottom reads like a finding. With it, it reads like a man who hit one. In the rooms we trust the second kind.

Overclaims: "the aspect of myself that *is* only ever good." Only ever? I don't have anything in me that's only ever good on my own power, as far as I know. **1. What happened in me.** The opening question about a wrong I knew at the time took me straight to a dinner at Priya's two years ago, doing my impression of He Who Shall Not Be Named for a younger writer who had said she liked his work. He's clearly the kind of person who does this, too*" is the hundred replies I've composed.

First: "I sense his hardening, and feel a brief flicker of satisfaction, vindication, even relief at being proven right about him—just before I relabel it disgust or dismay." That flicker is the cruel little smile. I've never had a sentence for what happens in my face before I say something cutting about him, and now I do, and I don't like it. Second, near the end: "The sigh that tells them what I really think of them... The look on their face that tells me it landed, which I pretend not to have seen." That one isn't the critic. One says *I know you're in there,* the other says *there's nobody in there*, and they both say it to the somebody who is." When the page asked me to feel where the critic's contempt was trying to reach, it was the part of me that sits down at the desk believing the work matters. That's the same place my impression at Priya's was aimed at, in him and in her. I put the laptop down.

The buttons exercise worked on me too, honestly: when a younger critic dunked on him online last spring I read it three times, years after any harm to me had stopped. So the pleasure was in the punishment. I didn't want to know that.

- "if in anything that follows, you catch yourself thinking *I'm a terrible person*, hold off on that judgement. It turns out to be one more instance of the thing we're studying." Managing my feelings before I've had them, and telling me what they'll turn out to be.
- "You've probably been in this kind of situation before. Really try and visualize it." "Really try" is the yoga teacher in me hearing another yoga teacher.
- "Would you have dug in? Vowed revenge?" followed straight away by "So the problem wasn't being caught." The "So" assumes my answer.
- "Think of how we might benefit from that label—and also, how it harms us." Homework.
- "The reason I don't have to argue myself into doing the right thing is that... it's what I naturally desire. Check if that's true of you, too." Claim first, then permission to check. - "We all know that this is what the darkness ultimately is." I hadn't caught myself knowing yet when I was told I knew.
- "There's another half that's harder to describe, because it can sound like an excuse." Answering an objection I hadn't made.

**3. Where my energy dropped.** The Mechanism section, from "Now look at what this trap depended on" through "thus increasingly turning them into an apparently different kind of thing." It turns into a chain of "that... that... that" clauses and a "thus." And the long paragraphs on innocence ("In a sense, my state had been utterly innocent" through "a lie") went abstract after the most frightening sentence in the chapter. Also the final two sentences: "getting back in touch with that deeper part of myself" landed a hard chapter on a soft cushion.

# Letter 2 (version 2)

Dear one,

Second pass, same Celeste, same critic, same dinner at Priya's. Here's what happened this time.
