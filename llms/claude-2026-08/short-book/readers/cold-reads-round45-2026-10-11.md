Dear Aditya,

I read it steadily, and twice I sat back with the pages in my lap. My attention went slack in the middle of "The Corruption" and through the second half of "The Encounter," where you stop telling a story and start explaining the trap. I skimmed from "Normally, having my enemy read my self-deception" down to "In brief," and I'll tell you why below.

**What happened, in order**

The opening questions got me right away. You asked for a wrong I knew was wrong at the time, so here it is. About 1996, in a parent conference, I told Mrs. Ochoa that her son Danny "is going to be a problem for every teacher he ever has." She had missed two conferences. Danny had bitten a girl that week. I was tired, and I said it in my teacher voice, the calm one. She didn't argue. She looked at her hands. I knew while I was saying it that it was cruel, and that it was for me, not for Danny. I didn't let myself know it until maybe a year later, at a sesshin, when it came up out of nowhere during the third day and I cried on the cushion.

So when you ask, in the chapter, "did it feel like new information? Or like something you had always known, but somehow hadn't *let* yourself know?" I can answer that without thinking. It was not new. It was like finding a letter in my own handwriting in a drawer. And the reasons I'd been giving myself were exactly your list: she never shows up, someone has to tell her, it's not a big deal. I bought the whole "Limbo" section. "The part that knew may not have won the argument, but it never budged, either" is true to my body. That part was quiet the whole year. It didn't nag. It just stayed.

"Why did one side have to argue, and the other not?" I liked that you stopped there and didn't answer it.

The two strangers I bought, mostly. For the second stranger I have a real one. My first teacher in the sangha, who I'll call Margaret, told me in dokusan once that I was "performing serenity for the room." It was the harshest thing anyone in that zendo ever said to me, and I did not dig in. I went home and it was true. She was talking to the person who could hear it. So "he could have been *harsher* than the first person... and it still wouldn't have triggered the digging-in response" is exactly my experience. And "It's like he was reminding you of something: that *you're better than this*" is right, except Margaret would never have said "better." She'd have said "you know this."

My first refusal was small, and it came early. In the chapter you write, of the first stranger, "I'd take that bet at any odds." I wouldn't. I've had a stranger chastise me in a Safeway parking lot for leaving my granddaughter in the car for ninety seconds, and I think that woman was frightened, not a hypocrite. Contemptuous, yes. But "the kind of person who does this too" was not what I felt. What I felt was "you don't know me." I could follow after that, because the rest of the chapter doesn't depend on that bet. But I noticed you assumed my reaction and it wasn't mine.

"The Encounter" I read with interest because it's you, and you don't spare yourself. "Sure, it may be a bit dark, but it's the *good* kind of darkness—the kind that fights the real kind." I laughed out loud at that, because that is my brother-in-law Rudy and me at every Christmas since 2004. Rudy says I sit around doing nothing. I say nothing back, very peacefully, and I let my peacefulness be the thing that says *you are a lesser kind of man*. That's the good kind of darkness. I use my practice as a weapon on him. I didn't know that sentence until I read yours.

"I wasn't *exposing* his darkness. I was creating more of it." That one landed hard and I want you to keep it.

Then the buttons.

**The checks**

The two buttons: my worst enemy right now is Darlene, two houses down, who reported my grandchildren's chalk drawings on the driveway to the HOA. A rainbow, a dog, and the word "ABUELA" in purple. We got a letter. I was asked: vengeance or the pill. I wanted the first button, and I want to be honest that I wanted it physically, in my hands. I wanted her to get a letter. I wanted her to stand on her driveway reading it with her face hot. Then I sat with it for a while and the pill started to look better, but only because I was proud of choosing it. I didn't trust that.

"Was the pleasure only in the harm stopping, or in the punishment?" The punishment. When Darlene's own HOA complaint about the Hendersons' palm tree got denied, I was glad, and it wasn't because of the palm tree.

"Could you have done it without a story?" No. I couldn't have said that sentence to Mrs. Ochoa without "someone has to tell her." I tried to imagine saying it with no reason at all and I couldn't do it. My mouth wouldn't.

"Have you ever felt it over something you did out of decency?" No. I have felt foolish over decency, embarrassed, taken advantage of. Never the sick feeling. I believe you on that one, from my own case.

The check that did the most was one you didn't quite ask. You write, in the chapter, "I'm afraid of what I already know: that I'm doing what they do." I went looking for whether that was true with Darlene, and I found it. In 2011 I called the city on the Nuñez family's rooster. Twice. I never spoke to them first. I told myself it was the noise. The memory-and-grammar check at the end ("you hid it from *yourself*. Not from a part of you; the part was the one doing the hiding.") I followed, and it is the closest thing in the chapter to a koan. I liked it. Which part would I keep? The one that knew without arguing. Not a hard question.
