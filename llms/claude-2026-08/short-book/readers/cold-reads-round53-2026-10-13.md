## 5. Would I send it to my own group?

Not as it stands, but it's a week from yes, not a year. I'd send Chapters 1 and 2 to my parish reading group tomorrow, and the hurt note to two women I know who need it. The book is true where it matters most. It is also generous, which is rarer, and it caught me with both of my contempts. What stands in its way is length made of restatement, plus three places where a reader like me will stop trusting you.

The three changes that must come first:

1. **Cut Chapter 3's middle and Chapter 4's first page down to what's new** (redundancy items 3, 4, 5, 10, 11, 17 and 20). This is where the book stops transmitting and starts insisting, and where I nearly put it down.

2. **Fix Chapter 4's opening and the second definition of the light.** Cut “So you can't think your way out” and the "Go back to the second stranger" paragraph. As written, a careful reader catches you contradicting your own guide and your own contract inside the practical chapter.

3. **One sentence on Augustine's Devil, and the wetiko paragraph out.** Say plainly that you're borrowing the name for the turning, not for the one who turned. Without that, the Picture enlists a Father for a claim he would refuse, and the readers who know him are the ones you most want to keep.

Do those and I'd send it to my group, and I'd be glad to have them read it.
