**3. Chapter 4's opening**

I believed the narrow version: "The mind I would have checked it with was the one it was running, and every argument sharpened the picture and made me surer I was seeing clearly."

I pushed back hardest here: "It can borrow the feeling of knowing, though." If it can, introspection can't tell knowing from its counterfeit. The marker the book offers, that the real thing "doesn't need to be sure," is also a feeling.

But the chapter does give a way that isn't introspection: counterfactual probes. The robbed check, and "Keep the analysis, the genuine care, the valid critique, and give up only the permission to feel contempt for the people on the other side. Would you still want it?" Both ask what you'd want in a changed world, not how sure you feel. That's how we test models whose self-reports we can't trust, and it worked on me. The inside isn't blind, just unreliable, and the chapter handed me instruments.

**4. Lost, put off, cornered**

- Chapter 2: "If awareness were neutral between care and harm, why would harming someone require turning away from it?" The premise does the work. The torturer paragraph saves it by admitting "That makes part of this a definition." Honest, but then the conclusion is partly built in.
- Chapter 2: "Check it against the cases you'd already use the word for; I haven't found a second kind." If every case is reclassified as the turn, no case could count against it.
- Epilogue: "My hatred for the people destroying the rainforest was creating a world in which the rainforest would be destroyed." A causal claim with no mechanism.
- Epilogue: "Then Darwin is just the way we explain Love in our particular reality." Lost me there.

Never cornered; the opt-outs read as meant.

**5. For a fragile reader**

- "It was self-evidently the Source of creation, and I was That." Together with "I have met the people in charge, and they are I." For someone prone to grandiosity, that pair is a template, and such readers skip the markers.
- "so knowledge of my wrongdoing always takes physical shape in my "external" world." The parenthesis after it protects others; a seriously ill, scrupulous reader will still apply it to themselves.
- "As best I can tell, all the harm I've caused through my self-deception still lives in the world somewhere, waiting for me to clean it up." Fuel for scrupulosity.

**6. Said twice**

- The Picture I Use: "This is, I think, where the "evil global conspiracy" archetype comes from." and "Real conspiracies also exist". Epilogue: "The most deeply buried is the sense of the evil global conspiracy." and "Which, again, is not to say that real conspiracies don't also exist, or that we shouldn't fight them with everything we have."

**7. The good undefinable; disagreement**

I read it as literal, and as a claim I already half hold. "No rule for the good holds every time, because the good isn't a rule." (Chapter 4) That's the uncodifiability thesis, and it's Goodhart: a written proxy, optimized without the thing it stood for, drifts away from it. The book does better than most by putting the failure in the follower, not the proxy: "It's that it can be followed without looking." That's how I think about specification gaming, now pointed at me.

On disagreement I was unmoved by the argument and moved by an observation. "But what differs is what people believe, and belief isn't where the knowing lives." (Chapter 4) As stated it can't lose: any variation gets assigned to belief, and the knowing is whatever survives. Relabeling doesn't answer Mackie. But the book has better evidence than it uses there. Contempt is legible across moral codes: two people who agree on nothing both read the look, and both feel the second stranger's correction as made on their side. A shared target predicts that invariance; plain relativism doesn't. That changed my question from whether humans share a value specification to whether they share a detector.

What cut deepest was Chapter 4's second road: "The darkness never had to deny that good exists. It only had to make it mine to define." Extrapolation proposals define the good as what we would want. I had never read that move as a risk in itself. I don't yet know if I agree, and I can't put it down.
