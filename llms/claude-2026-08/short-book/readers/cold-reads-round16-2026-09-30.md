**The memory.** I did it honestly. Twenty-odd years ago I kept a patient, I'll call him Mr. K, at twice a week for most of a year after he no longer needed it. It felt exactly as you put it, like something I "hadn't *let* myself know." I also remember changing the subject in my analyst's office the one time his schedule came up. So yes: some part of me knew I was deflecting while I deflected. Your first claim matched. Bought.

A clinician's footnote: my field has words for this. Disavowal, bad faith, the preconscious. I could have said it at the time if anyone had asked me the right question.

"Did that knowledge arrive before or after you'd reasoned it through?" Before. The reasons came after and were very good reasons: termination is its own clinical work, he's still consolidating gains. **The better nature.** "In fact, I *can't*, when I can clearly see myself in them." This was the first place I balked. It's the accusation, and it's also the proof. Right now I get the accusation in the scene and the proof pages later, after the recursion I was told not to trace.

**Which did more.** The second file. Three reasons. It puts me on the receiving end before it puts me in your shoes. You tell your own story about stealing, so I'm not just hearing about "a mind." And it goes where I already live: evil, darkness, the voice that says there's nothing to steer by. The chapter talked around that. The second file walks right up to it.

**Which treated me better.** Also the second, mostly. There's no Gnostic gospel at the door. It leaves me a way out early, "Mine was. If yours wasn't, hold on to that," and later, "If neither sounds like you, I have no argument to offer here." That's fair. The chapter told me "If that doesn't match anything in your memory, the rest of this won't make sense, and I'd rather you know that now," which is honest too, but it felt like a gate, not a door.

The second file has more questions with the answer inside them, though. "Can you picture them saying that underneath everything, what they'd found was that they didn't care about anyone, or wished they could harm others more?" There's one answer to that and you know it. And near the end it's check after check after check, "A lot rides on this, so here are three more checks," until I felt like I was being walked through a checklist at the shop.

**What the second file has that the chapter should get back.** A lot. In order:

1. **The first stranger.** "Now, imagine that while you were in that state—having done wrong, justifying it, half-knowing—a stranger had caught you and publicly chastised you for it." And then "*who the hell is he to judge?*" I've had that exact thought about everybody who ever called me a bigot for how I vote. The chapter starts me as the guy doing the confronting, so I'm defending myself from the first page. The second file lets me be on the receiving end first, feeling what I actually feel. After that, I couldn't pretend I didn't know what the look does when I send it.

2. **The teacher.** "Mine was a teacher who'd caught me stealing." This is the single biggest thing the chapter is missing. You own your mess first, and with a real story, not "my mind." Then "He was searching my face for something, some sign that I knew, and that made me look for it in myself." That's Ray Dunbar behind the cruiser. In the chapter I had to bring Ray myself. Here you showed me yours, and mine came up on its own. And "I don't want to make too much of that afternoon. It worked because there was something in me for it to reach, and I was young and easy to reach. It doesn't always work." That's the fairest sentence in either file. A cop knows it doesn't always work, and you said so before I could.

3. **The question that pulls me forward.** "What is it that even nominal enemies can be aligned on?" It comes back near the end, answered: "Underneath the argument, my enemy and I were never disagreeing on what the ground was. We were fighting over which of us still stood on it." I stopped reading there for a while. That's Kaylee and me. She thinks I've lost my compassion. I think she's lost her sense. We both think the other one walked off the ground we were raised on, and neither of us ever said out loud what the ground was. The chapter's Common knowledge section is trying to say this, I think, with the mirrors and the levels. The second file says it so a man can hear it.

4. **"What am I supposed to do—be *nice* to evil?"** That's my objection, and the second file puts it in your mouth inside the scene, as one of the stories. The chapter never lets me say it. Then at the end: "Going easy on him? He didn't, and I don't think we have to, either." And "But everything I've been able to accomplish with it, I've been able to accomplish better without it." Where I come from, the first thing anybody hears in a book like this is soft on crime. The second file answers that without me having to raise it.

5. **The list of stories.** "Every time he saw through one story I had another ready: just being "fierce," then an invitation, then the good kind of darkness, then projection. The stories kept changing; the conclusion never did." Anybody who's worked a case knows that. When a suspect's story keeps changing and his conclusion doesn't, you know he's lying. It's the same with me. The chapter has the stories but never lines them up like that, so I didn't catch myself doing it.

6. **The loop, said plain.** "I sent the verdict. The verdict provoked his defiance. Then I treated his defiance as if I'd discovered it: independent proof that the verdict was right." Three short sentences. The chapter spends a page on it.

7. **The receiving end, said plain.** "A slip I can shrug off, or even forgive. What I can't shrug off is the sense that he means it, and wants me to know that he does, and to top it all off, wants to believe he's the good guy for it." I understood that the first time. The chapter's version, "In short: I am in limbo about provoking him, and showing him that I *am* in limbo is itself the provocation," took me two reads.

10. **Looking them in the eye.** "Can you imagine looking the people it would affect in the eye and still doing it?" I could not have typed "became combative" with that kid's mother standing in the room. That one question did more to me than the whole Common knowledge section.

18. **Evil as absence.** "Evil is not the presence of a particular quality but the profound absence of one." My pastor preaches that, darkness as the absence of light. 19. **The ending.** "If darkness were an essence, I would be right to be afraid of it, and ashamed, and I'd have to keep proving it's in others and not in me.
