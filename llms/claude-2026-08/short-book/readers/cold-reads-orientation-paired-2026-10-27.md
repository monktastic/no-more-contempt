## Dana: version A

### Letter 1 — after the preface and Chapter 1

Jo,

Okay. You owe me a beer and possibly a therapist.

The preface almost lost me. Gospel of Philip at the top, plant medicines in a parenthesis, "a settled seat," and "May all beings be free of suffering" at the bottom. I thought, here we go, it's a retreat-center brochure. The one thing that kept me was him saying it's "not one that I can claim to be fully living myself." I'll give a guy a chapter for that.

Then Chapter 1 got me, and not where I expected.

He doesn't start with people you hate. He starts with something *you* did that you knew was wrong. I went straight to the Accord job — you don't know this story and you're not going to — where I wrote an old parking-lot crease into a supplement because the adjuster had lowballed me three times that year. "They screw everybody, it evens out." He asks whether owning up felt like new information or like something you always knew, and no, I knew it while I was writing it. And when the adjuster called about the photos I got LOUD. He has a line, "Why did one side have to argue, and the other not?" — and I sat there with it, because the excuses were all mine and busy, and the knowing just sat there.

Then the two strangers. One catches you and says you ARE that kind of person; the other is angry at what you did and not at what you are. I had the second one. Walt, my first boss, caught me at nineteen taking a quart of shop paint home. He said "Dana. What are you doing," and waited. The book says the teacher "was searching my face for some sign that I knew, and that made me look for it in myself." That's Walt's face exactly. I'd have walked through a wall for him after.

Where it really hit, though, was a couple of pages later. He writes out the other guy's side: what a hypocrite, why the fuck shouldn't I, I'm not going to be the lone chump. And then he asks: "Did some part of you feel you *deserved* something? That everyone does it, and only a fool plays it straight?" Jo. I padded the Accord because adjusters treat every shop like crooks. Adjusters treat every shop like crooks because shops pad. And the week before, I'd gone off on a guy from Progressive on speakerphone over Tanya Reyes's frame, said things I'd fire one of my guys for, and he knocked another two hundred off "per policy," and I thought: see, proves it. He has that too: "if he reacts poorly, that only proves my point." I've been telling the adjuster story for fifteen years as a story about them.

And then the part I'm not going to write much about. You know the Rick voice I do at Easter. The book has a two-buttons thing — vengeance or a pill that changes their heart — and I'd press the pill, I prayed for that pill for ten years, but yes, something in me itches for the other one. Then it asks about watching someone you despise get what's coming, and I thought of Rick's DUI and that I did the voice about it at Easter with Tyler at the table. Then this line: "**Love and contempt are addressed to the same place.** One says *I know you're in there.* The other says *there's nobody in there*, and they both say it to the somebody who is." And right under it: "A child can't refuse it; he believes it, and carries it for years." He meant a child who's the target. Tyler's not the target of the Rick voice. But he's half Rick. I sat in the truck in the lot for a while.

Then the "quiet kind" section finished the job. "The *what's wrong with you?* that needs them to know they're broken. The sigh that tells them what I really think of them... For one second the verdict flashes through my eyes, and I make sure they see it." That's me and Tyler when he gives me some excuse about a late assignment. "You sound just like your father." Or I don't say it, I just give him the look. The book didn't accuse me of that. It just described it and I was in it.

The peak for me was that stretch, from "only a fool plays it straight" through the quiet kind. The very end asks if you've ever been the second stranger, and if you've ever felt the sigh coming and not sent it. I had to dig for that one. I found one, three weeks ago, Tyler and his phone, and I didn't say it, and he told me the truth about the bleachers. One. I hadn't connected that to anything until I read the question.

Where it sagged: the middle, "The Need" and "The Mechanism." It goes from people to "it" and "the need" and "turned away" and "limbo." There's a sentence, "This is how my need to be different and special gets itself met: by revealing itself to my enemies while hiding itself from me, turning them into what it needs them to be," that sounded like a counselor's handout or a computer. The need is doing all the acting. Also the paragraph about "never losing contact with the deepest part of myself" and "the wisest people" — who? Name one. It went soft right after a hard page.

Where I got lost:
- "On the other hand, he can't prove I'm doing this, can he? No. And *neither can I*." Read it three times. Prove I'm doing it, or prove I'm not?
- "I had to be present enough to plant the evidence, and absent enough not to recognize it as planted." Plant what? Evidence that I'm dark, or evidence that he's bad? I guessed the second.
- "He just has to see that I *am* listening to my thoughts—because my better nature doesn't have to argue." How do you see somebody listening to their thoughts? I can guess. He didn't say.
- "The threat is over, and now I'm calling him out." What threat? Took me a second to get that it means nobody needs protecting anymore. That's a big deal and it's four words.
- "(If the recursion tickles you, see Appendix 1.)" I don't have Appendix 1, and "recursion" is Tyler's coding-class word.
- "darkness." It's in the preface like I already know what it is, then "the darkness in their eyes," "my own darkness," "their darkness." Is that a figure of speech or does he mean something real, like the devil? With the Gospel quote at the top I can't tell. Also "innocent part" shows up once — same as "better nature"? Right next to him calling himself "utterly innocent" in a different sense.
- There's a typo: "I must *turn away* from it to do act that way."

Where I felt handled: the remorse-in-reverse bit. He asks if you've ever felt remorse for doing something decent, then says "If you think you've felt it, look closer," and lists what it really was — regret, anger at being taken advantage of, feeling foolish. Mine was exactly on his list (a guy skipped out on a payment plan and I felt like a sucker), which almost made it worse. He told me what I'd find before I looked. And one claim cornered me: after "Evil doesn't have to originate from some kind of *bad essence*. It only requires a certain kind of confusion," he says if I see a bad essence in somebody, "I'm almost certainly lost in that confusion myself, and looking for an excuse to spread it." I just read a whole book on Dennis Rader. "Confusion" is a soft word for that man, and me thinking he's evil isn't me spreading anything. Maybe he gets to this. Right now it rubs.

Machine or committee sentences: the "need gets itself met" one above. "a uniquely insidious expression of evil" in the preface, which is a book-jacket sentence. "you're better than this" — the Walt story works, the slogan doesn't; my mother said that to me a hundred times and it never once worked. "I'm no saint, and won't pretend that I am." "levels of increasingly subtle limbo" sounds like a video game.

What I'll say for him: he goes first every time. Every ugly thing he asks me to look at, he's already shown me his. That's the only reason I didn't throw it at the wall at "if you think you've felt it, look closer." It reads like a person, mostly. The monologue where he's sneering at a guy and catching himself ("*Shit.* He senses what I'm up to. But how? He's not a mind reader!") sounds like the inside of my head on the Kevin call.

How it ended: quiet. "Underneath the argument, my enemy and I were never fighting over whether a shared ground exists. We were fighting over which of us still stood on it." Good line. Kevin and I were both claiming to be the one on the customer's side. Then it asks the same question it asked forty lines in: "What is it that two people who agree on nothing can both be serving?" Asking it twice made it heavier, but it didn't get me any closer to the answer.

Do I want Chapter 2? Yes. But honestly, the question I'm carrying isn't his. His is "what's the shared ground." Mine is: what do I do about Tyler and the voice. And I'm nervous about his question, because if the answer is conscience I'm with him, and if it's God or "the Light" or something out of the plant medicines, I'm going to feel like I got walked into a church basement under false pretenses. Which, I mean. I've been in a lot of church basements.

Love,
Dana

### Letter 2 — after Chapter 2

Jo,

Chapter 2 is called "What Evil Is," and it was half insight and half drag, and I can tell you exactly where the line runs.

The first line didn't follow from where Chapter 1 left me. Chapter 1 ended asking "What is it that two people who agree on nothing can both be serving?" Chapter 2 opens "If good is impossible to define, what about evil?" Chapter 1 never said good was impossible to define. It said we may never agree on a definition and don't need to. That's not the same thing. And the question I'd carried in got set down without a word. About ninety lines later he says "The care that came with the face is what I'll call the *light*," and I *think* that's the answer to the Chapter 1 question, but he never says so. I had to put it together myself, and I'm still not sure I'm right.

The drag first. In the middle he goes from one person's face to "the world as a whole." Contempt "subtly, imperceptibly, darkens *the world as a whole*." If it's imperceptible, how would I know? Then the politician who lies his country into a war, and then: "I think the *vast majority* of what we see in the world is the result of such hidden intentions." The vast majority of what? That's the guy at the end of the bar who watches too many videos. Then he says he decides who to support by feeling whether they're "turned-toward or turned-away *in general*," and that we can "sense better than we let ourselves know." Jo, I married Rick because I sensed he was a good guy. Bundy was charming. Voting by vibe is how con men win. My cousin Mike did two tours, and the second I read "lies his country into a war" I was wondering whose side the author's on, which is exactly what Chapter 1 told me not to care about. That page almost made me put it down for the night.

The good parts were very good. Early on he asks if you know that someone treated with contempt is more likely to cheat and cut corners. I thought of Dale. When Dale was my foreman he rode the guys, called them idiots in front of customers, and comebacks went up and flag hours got fudged. I thought I'd hired a bad crop. Dale left, same guys, comebacks went down. I never connected that till this book. That one I'll carry.

Then the face exercise: "Imagine looking into a stranger's face until it stops being something you're looking at and becomes a place somebody is looking out of." I did it with the guy who cut me at the Giant Eagle deli. Once there was a tired sixty-year-old in there, maybe a wife in the car, I couldn't wish him harm without going "entitled jerk" first. Then he says put your enemy's face there. I put Rick there, and the first thing that came wasn't his face. It was the voice, *"Dee, it's a total setup."* My impression of him got there before he did. And the book says, "For me the story is there before the face is. That's the turning away, live." Fifteen Easters of practice, and it runs faster than his face. When I pushed past it I got Rick in the hospital holding Tyler, crying, sober that week. Then the voice came right back like a commercial.

And then: "A grudge has to be fed: the reasons rehearsed, the case kept open, their face kept out of view... when a grudge finally drops, it doesn't feel like starting a new effort. It feels like setting something down." Two years of Al-Anon in four lines. And "Hunger doesn't have to look away from the bread." And "Accounts of atrocity return again and again to the same preparation: the hood, the number instead of the name, the word for vermin." I read history; I believe that one all the way down.

He also half-answered my Dennis Rader gripe from Chapter 1. "A torturer has his victim in plain sight, and the cruelest watch the face closely." He's not saying Rader was confused like a guy who lost his keys; he's saying even Rader had to get them out of view first. When it asked me to picture the worst person I know of doing their worst while seeing their victim the way Walt saw me, I couldn't hold the picture. Okay. Point to him.

What felt like Chapter 1 again:
- "I'm just another person who knew what was right, decided it was negotiable, and then looked for someone worse than me to prove I wasn't so bad after all." Chapter 1 already had him needing others to carry his darkness, "to prove to myself and the world that I was good."
- "One way or another, something in me needs there to be an essential difference between us." Chapter 1 said almost word for word that he needs bad people's behavior to come from something he doesn't have. That's the third time.
- "Badness is the result of turning away, not its cause... some 'bad essence.'" Chapter 1: "Evil doesn't have to originate from some kind of *bad essence*. It only requires a certain kind of confusion." Same claim. To be fair, this time he backs it up instead of just saying it.
- "When I treat him with contempt, I'm revealing to him that I *know* it's the same move, and that I'm using that fact against him." That's the "I know exactly how you cause harm, because I'm making you watch me do it to you" bit from Chapter 1's mechanism.
- "Check if you know this yourself—whether someone treated with contempt is more or less likely to cheat, steal..." Chapter 1 had the target thinking "why the fuck shouldn't I? I'm not going to be the lone chump." The Dale memory was new for me. The point wasn't.
- "Does he know he's doing this? I think the answer is yes and no, just as it is with myself." Limbo, from the opening of Chapter 1.
- "In the last chapter, I said the deepest layer of limbo was..." He's repeating himself on purpose, but it's still repeating.
- "And recall the dehumanizing gaze check from Chapter 1." That's the one check that didn't work for me in Chapter 1 (the appraiser didn't seem "absent from himself," he seemed very present and a jerk), and now it's holding up a paragraph.
- The point that harm needs a story and decency doesn't was Chapter 1's "Why did one side have to argue, and the other not?" Here he says it plainer and proves it better with hunger and the grudge. So it's a repeat that improved.
- The face exercise is a fuller version of Chapter 1's "picture someone in front of you, fully in view." That one I don't mind. Chapter 1's "fully in view" only made sense to me after I read this.

The opening cases, before the first break: the drunk-driver pair, red wine vs. the cutting remark, the soldier and the corpse, the mouse, line-cutting. Honestly, I was finding things as I read them. Linda's "Dana always did like a project" when I married Rick, twenty years ago, and I still hear it. The deli guy. Mike would say the corpse thing is wrong without blinking. But where they landed, "they weren't really *looking*," and "When I turn away from my better nature, I turn away from the life in others," was a point I already had from Chapter 1. I hadn't predicted it, though. My log going in said: "Chapter 2 tells me what the shared ground is. I'm afraid it'll be God or 'the Light,' capital L. If it's 'conscience,' I'm with him." And after the first chunk: "Predicted Chapter 2 would tell me what the shared ground is. Wrong. It skipped to evil." So the cases weren't predictable to me, but when he named where they were going I thought, oh, this is turning away again. The cases were fresh. The landing wasn't.

What I could skip: the politics section, from "It means that my mind, with enough cleverness, can inject *massive* amounts of darkness into the world" through "it's something we can each sense better than we let ourselves know." I'd lose nothing and I'd stop wondering who he votes for. Most of the "world as a whole" stretch could go too, except Dale, if Dale were in it, which he isn't; that's mine. What I'd least want cut: the face exercise through the grudge. The enemy's face with the story arriving first is the best thing in the chapter for me.

Where I was lost:
- "And that darkness can sometimes be 'shaped.'" Shaped how, by who, into what? The quote marks tell me it means something and I don't know what.
- "That asymmetry is what it means for care to be the baseline: what needs no turn and no story is where I am when I'm not doing anything, and turning away is a departure from it, not a second baseline of its own." Read it twice, right after the great hunger-and-bread line.
- The ending. First "I think it's fully explained by the turn," and evil is "something I *do*, then I can stop." Then "It tracks, it aims, it fights to stay hidden. It's an absence that behaves like a presence." So is evil me, or something else using me? Is it me fighting myself? It reads like he's saying "you can stop" and then describing the devil with a hole in him. Sister Agnes would recognize the second one.
- "whether I feel it *at* my enemy who's present, or just *for* them in the presence of others." Feel it *for* them? "For" usually means on their behalf. I think he means about them, in front of other people. Which, by the way, is the Rick voice at Easter, exactly.

Where I was handled:
- "But when I hate the animal abuser, I have to see that my hatred for him is the same move, or else I'm lying to myself." Chapter 1 showed me contempt spreads and provokes. It didn't show me that hating a guy who tortures a cat is the same as torturing the cat. And "or else I'm lying to myself" makes my disagreeing into lying. First time I felt cornered.
- "That is why I say contempt is evil. It's not a value judgment." Calling something evil is about the biggest value judgment there is. He said it in the preface too. Saying it doesn't make it so.

- "Notice whether it requires any stories to maintain, or if there's something natural about it." I know which answer he wants.
- "What isn't a definition is what you checked: that your wrong needed a story, and your decency didn't." He's telling me my results. He's right about mine, but it's my report to make.
- "The point of chapter one was to show you that this isn't some abstract idea." Don't tell me what the last chapter was for.

"They diffuse into the environment in subtle ways I can't track." "his corrupted state." "So darkness is not an equal and opposite force." "It's an absence that behaves like a presence" sounds like a sentence somebody was proud of. And "actually" three times in one sentence near the top.

How it ended: on evil "borrowed" from me, "It's what I saw in my enemy, and what I showed him in return." Do I want the next chapter? Yes, but for a worried reason. I'm carrying two questions. His "the light... For now, that's all the word will mean." *For now.* So it's going to mean more, and I can hear a church-basement door opening. And what's the "it" that "tracks, aims, fights to stay hidden," if evil is just something I do? Under both of those is still mine: I know now the voice gets there before Rick's face does. What do I do about that with Tyler in the room? He hasn't touched it, and I don't think it's his job to. But that's what I'm reading for.

Dana

### Letter 3 — after the passages taken out of Chapter 2

Jo,

He sent along the bits he cut from Chapter 2. Ten little pieces. A few of them hit me harder than most of the chapter did, which I don't think he'll like hearing.

**The ones I miss, most first:**

**5.** "The padded expense, the lie to the insurer, the joke that makes someone smaller, the slur, the order to clear a village: each needs someone held out of view so that I, or mine, can be the exception."

This one stopped me cold. The lie to the insurer is the Accord. The joke that makes someone smaller is the Rick voice. Both of mine, in one line, right next to the village, and it doesn't accuse me of anything. It just lists, and I'm on the list. Where it goes: right after "It is that move that I call *evil.*" That's where he names the move, and this shows the move small to huge. It does it better than the chapter does. The chapter's version is "If it was cheating on a test... For me, I have to feel that *the rules don't apply to me.* To treat myself as worth more," and I had to bend my "I'm owed" into his "worth more" to make it fit. "So that I, or mine, can be the exception" fit me with no bending. And "or mine" covers the executives and the company without another sentence.

**4.** "That's why the smallest evil carries a whiff of the whole: there's nothing in it but the turn, and the turn is the same turn at any size."

Goes right before 5, after "It is that move that I call *evil.*" It pays off the line-cutting from the top of the chapter ("the heat is out of all proportion, because what you're reacting to isn't the cost"). The chapter never says *why* the deli guy makes me want to run him over. This says why. "A whiff of the whole" is a good, plain phrase.

**1.** "When I'm the first stranger, notice what I do: I make sure the facts are on my side, and sneak in the thing that I know will harm him. Why would I do that if I really believed facts were enough to settle it? I know better, and I'm counting on being able to claim otherwise."

After "The objective facts supported both of them." That's the Kevin call. I had the estimates and the labor times laid out like a lawyer, and then I sneaked in the tone. The chapter says both strangers had the facts and then jumps to the world getting darker. This says what the first stranger actually *does* with the facts. Chapter 1 called it "this self-righteous quality," which I flagged as soft. This is the hard version of the same thing. It says something the chapter doesn't.

**10.** "If contempt is how evil spreads, the next question is how far it goes. Does my contempt make any of those things more likely: the slur, the village, the forest? Or does it stay in my skull?"

This should be the ending. More on that below.

**9.** "And contempt is the turn made into a message. **It's evil's way of spreading in secret, through people who sincerely believe they're fighting it.**"

Right before 10, at the end. It says plainly what the preface said in jacket-copy ("how the darkness spreads between us while masquerading as righteousness"), and says it better. It's also a better version of the chapter's "That is why I say contempt is evil. It's not a value judgment." That line asks me to take his word for it. This one describes what contempt *does*, and I can check that against the Kevin call and Easter.

**7.** "To wield contempt, I had to already know that its target was redeemable: the verdict was aimed at pushing him further from the light, and nobody can be pushed from a place they aren't in... Mine told him the door was closed."

After "One way or another, something in me needs there to be an essential difference between us." It shows that contempt itself admits the guy isn't all bad, which makes the "essential difference" fall over on its own. "Nobody can be pushed from a place they aren't in" is a good one. "Mine told him the door was closed" is a better one. But to be honest, half of it is Chapter 1 again. Chapter 1 already had "I was knowingly smothering his innocent part—a part I *knew* could be rescued." So it says it better, but it isn't new. It also has to come after "the light" is named or it won't make sense. And see the problem with it below.

**3.** "It's an absence. Every faculty is still at work, and the other person's life is no longer allowed to count the way I still know it does."

I'd want this in place of, or right before, "It's an absence that behaves like a presence: it has no substance of its own; everything it uses—my attention, my reasons, my eyes—is borrowed from me." The chapter's version never tells me what's absent. This one does: the other person counting. I got it the first time. I had to read the chapter's version twice.

**2.** "There is one kind of evil, and it's the turn... Check it against the cases you'd already use the word for; I haven't found a second kind."

Only half. I'd want "Check it against the cases you'd already use the word for; I haven't found a second kind" in place of "But I think it's fully explained by the turn." It's honest, it's his finding and says so, and it hands me a check instead of a verdict. The rest of 2 ("By *evil* I don't mean damage. Damage can be an accident.") the chapter already does better with the two drivers. And it ends on "It was never a thing some people have. It's the turn itself," which is that tidy it-isn't-this-it's-that rhythm.

**Where they fight with the chapter:**

- **2 and 3 against the ending.** 2 says "There is one kind of evil, and it's the turn," and 3 says "It's an absence. Every faculty is still at work." The chapter ends: "It tracks, it aims, it fights to stay hidden." I already flagged in my last letter that I couldn't tell whether evil is something I do or something using me. Putting 2 back makes that sharper. If it's "the turn itself," who's doing the aiming? 3 helps, because it says the faculties are mine and still working, so the tracking and aiming could just be me, turned. But then "it fights to stay hidden" has to mean *I* fight to keep it hidden. If that's what he means, he should say that. Right now the chapter says both.
- **10 against the middle of the chapter.** 10 asks, as an open question, whether my contempt makes the slur and the village more likely, or stays in my skull. But the middle of the chapter already answered it: "In that sense, my contempt subtly, imperceptibly, darkens *the world as a whole*." So you can't have both. Either the chapter claims it in the middle, or it asks it at the end. I'd rather it ask. That middle stretch was the part I could have skipped anyway.
- **10 needs 6.** "The slur, the village, the forest." The slur and the village come from 5. The forest only comes from 6, and I don't want 6 back. So if 10 comes back without 6, the forest dangles. Drop the forest.
- **7 and Chapter 1.** 7 says "So it wasn't *the good kind of darkness* I told myself it was in Chapter 1." I can't find that phrase in Chapter 1. What Chapter 1 has is "I was wielding my own darkness to do so—but it was still 'for the greater good.'" Close, but if he puts it back he has to quote himself right, or I'll go looking and not find it, which is what happened.
- **5 against "worth more."** No real fight. The chapter says making yourself an exception isn't enough to be evil ("A human *is* worth more than a mouse... I can take the last seat on the bus"), and then says it's about not looking. 5 says you need both: someone held out of view *so that* you can be the exception. That ties the two halves of the chapter together instead of fighting them.

**The ones I don't want back:**

**6.** "Now widen it... A man held as a thing, a forest as nothing but lumber... everything that is eating the world... by something alive that has turned from what it is." The forest is where he lost me. "Everything that is eating the world" is a rally sign. "Something alive that has turned from what it is" I read twice and still don't get. After the politics page, this would've been the second time in one chapter I was wondering what side he's on, and the first page of the book told me that's not the point.

**8.** "Contempt is evil. That's not a value judgment." It's already in the chapter, and I didn't buy it there either. Bare like this, it's a pronouncement. Chapter 1 earned "contempt spreads." It didn't earn "contempt is evil, and saying so isn't a judgment." 9 says the same thing in a way I can check. Use 9.

**The ending.** Right now the chapter ends on "It's what I saw in my enemy, and what I showed him in return." That ending looks back. It's Chapter 1 again, and it left me holding "wait, is evil me or the devil?" instead of a question I wanted to chase. I'd end on 9 and then 10, minus the forest:

> And contempt is the turn made into a message. It's evil's way of spreading in secret, through people who sincerely believe they're fighting it.
