# Round 54 (October 14): the whole short book and Appendix 1, six readers

*Six cold readers, three spiritual and three not (all sympathetic), each at low, medium and high analytical level, read the clean short manuscript built from d1151d7, through Appendix 1 (Appendices 2 and 3 optional). Brief: a verdict per part with major objections only; contradictions; answers to questions they didn't have; questions they had that went unanswered; redundancy; would they give it to someone they care about. ## Rosa (retired preschool teacher; Catholic, centering prayer; low analytic)

Dear Aditya,

**1.** Steady, and in places more than steady. Chapter 1 and the first half of Chapter 2 I read in one sitting and then sat with them. My attention went slack in the back half of Chapter 3, at "Everyone who remains caught in the same ignorance (all of us, to varying degrees) imports the plausible deniability from Chapter 1 into the structures they participate in." That is where I would have put it down for the night, and I might not have come back. Chapter 4 brought me back. Appendix 1 I read the way I read my late husband's tax papers: dutifully, and I stopped taking things in at "Take one away." I am not the reader it's for, and it says so, which I appreciated.

I should tell you what I brought to it, because the exercises are how I'm judging it. I have a son, Daniel, who hasn't spoken to me in four years. For the wrong I knew at the time, I didn't have to look far. At a Christmas dinner five years ago I said to his wife, "I'm sure your mother did her best." I knew exactly what I was doing while I said it. When I finally owned it, it wasn't new information. You're right about that, and it was not pleasant to be right with you.

For the two buttons, the face that came was hers, not his. Some part of me did itch toward the first button, and I'd like to say it didn't. At the face exercise in Chapter 2, a stranger's face came easily; I've done centering prayer for fifteen years and that kind of resting is home to me. Then I put her face there, and you're right again: "For me the story is there before the face is." Mine was there before I'd finished picturing her. The story is: *she took him.* The thousand-year tree: yes, it landed where the violation of a person lands. I thought of a saguaro some boys shot full of holes out past Gates Pass.

Then the check in Chapter 4: "if the harm simply stopped tomorrow, with nobody punished and nobody exposed, would you feel relief, or robbed?" If Daniel called tomorrow and nobody ever said who had been wrong, would I be glad? I'd be glad. And I found the other thing too, under the gladness: I want him to say it. I want to be shown to have been the one wronged. I have prayed about this for four years and never once seen that. That one question was worth the book to me.

I tried the Far End exercise for about a breath and stopped, as your note says to. What stopped me felt like love for my son. I didn't push.

**2. READY?**

**The Short Version and the Introduction: CLOSE.**

- The Introduction begins: "The mind is intrinsically compassionate. Evil is nothing but a mind turned away from itself." That is stated as fact on the first page. Later Appendix 1 says plainly that the argument "does not get you that care is what awareness *is*," and moves that belief behind the door marked "From here on I'm telling you what I believe." So the first thing a reader meets is the thing you later admit you can't show. I noticed I was being pushed before I had done anything. I'd write it as yours, the way you do so well elsewhere: something like "I've come to believe the mind is…", and then let the next sentence ("The rest of this book is me checking that…") do its work.
- Nothing else here is major. "This version is for friends who sit" and "bodhicitta aspirations" told me I was overhearing a letter to someone else. That's fine for your sangha. For readers of other traditions or none, you'll want a different opening.

**Chapter 1: CLOSE.** This is the best thing in the book, and I mean that. The second stranger, "Not more gently," and "Love and contempt are addressed to the same place": I'll carry those. One major objection:

- In The Spread: "It's the same verdict, delivered by withdrawal, and it's meant to be felt: by them, or by whoever's watching." I am the parent who was cut off. I read that line and my first thought was *there, that's what Daniel is doing to me.* I used it. It took me until Chapter 3's note for the hurt, more than a hundred paragraphs later, to read "It is possible to leave, cut contact, and never see them again with no verdict in it at all," and to wonder if that is what he did. A great many readers are estranged from someone, on one side or the other. The withdrawal sentence needs its counterweight right beside it, one clause, so that nobody holding a son's silence can take the first half and leave the second.

**Chapter 2: CLOSE.**

- "That care is what I'll call the light, and it's all the word will mean." Then the word goes on to mean other things (see section 3 below). If you promise that, keep it, or don't promise it.
- After the face exercise, the chapter turns into a row of bold verdicts: "There is one kind of evil, and this is it." "Goodness is one thing too." "No amount of turning away ever produces a turning back." Each might be true. Four bolded pronouncements in a few paragraphs felt like being lectured right after being invited, and the change of voice lost me some trust. I'd unbold most of them and let one stand.

**Chapter 3: CLOSE up to the locker room; the last third, as it stands, NOT READY.**

- The chapter does too many jobs: the choice, the parasite, its goal, its drawbacks, the Mahayana, the war, *marigpa*, home, the slow and the stupid, the note for the hurt, the far end, the hostage, the roundup, the locker room, consuming contempt, the water, institutions, culture, the conspiracy. The note for the hurt and the hostage passage are some of the best pages you have, and they get buried. I'd move the institutions, culture and conspiracy material into "The Picture I Use," where you've already said there's no check. Then Chapter 3 ends on "So the question isn't whether you're in this."
- "So it doesn't only need us to believe in corrupt elites. It needs them in power." That's a large claim about the world, said flatly. Even marked as "less to check," it's where a careful reader would start to suspect the rest. Say it as yours or cut it.
- On the note for the hurt: it's written for the person who needs to get away. It doesn't see the person who was left. See section 5.

**Chapter 4: READY.** The relief-or-robbed check, the three sentences with "the extra" ("*You lied to me.* With the extra: *Of course you lied to me.*"), and "pity, which passes for compassion and is contempt with a soft face" all caught me where I live. I have pitied my son. Nothing major here.

**The Picture I Use: CLOSE.**

- Wetiko. "I know the word only through the two of them, and I borrow it because it names what this book's parasite does." You're honest about the borrowing, and I respect that. But one of the two is a white writer who made the word his life's subject, and some readers will notice you got a Cree word secondhand through him. Lean on Forbes, or let the paragraph go. The Augustine and 2 Corinthians already carry the point for me, and more surely.
- "And it is the coordinator Chapter 3 said there wasn't." I understand you're marking the step up, and it's honest to flag it. But the reader who skipped the italic note will think you've contradicted yourself, because Chapter 3 said "coordination with no coordinator." I'd say in one plain clause that this is the picture going further than the check did.
- The last paragraph, the promise you can make only about yourself, is lovely. Leave it alone.

**An Exercise for the Far End: CLOSE.**

- The warning is given twice in one short page: once in the italic note, and again inside the exercise. And the two disagree. The note says "If you have a teacher, do it with them, not alone." The exercise ends "If it frightens you or stays with you afterward, stop, and don't do it alone again." *Again* assumes I did it alone the first time. Keep the italic note, take the repeated warning out of the paragraph, and change the ending to "stop, and don't go back to it alone."

**Appendix 1: CLOSE, for its reader.**

- In "What I think is really happening": "Not that there's one awareness. Only that there's nothing to tell them apart, which is all the mutual recognition needs." Then, two paragraphs on: "The appearance of separate people at all, me here and him there, is what the first turning looks like from inside." The second is what "one awareness" means, as far as I can understand it. Since it's marked as belief you're allowed it, but not both at once. Either say you believe it's one, or soften the second sentence.
- "desecrating an enemy's corpse does the dead man no damage, and everyone knows it's wrong (Appendix 3 comes back to this)." "Everyone knows" is the kind of phrase the rest of the book is careful never to use.

**3. CONTRADICTIONS**

- *Evil as the turn, or evil as what the turn produces.* Chapter 1: "Badness is the result of turning away, not its cause." Chapter 3: "It can't hold, because evil is the *result* of turning away, not its cause." But Chapter 2: "It was never a thing some people have. It's the turn itself, and contempt is the turn made into a message, which spreads it." And Appendix 1: "Evil, in this book, names the turning: your own caring held out of view so that someone else can be." Is evil the turn, or what comes of it? I think you mean "a bad *essence* is the result, not the cause." Say that in Chapter 3.
- *"The light."* Chapter 2: "That care is what I'll call the light, and it's all the word will mean." Chapter 4: "That's what I've been calling the light: the thing that, when someone is aimed at it, you know it, and when they're aimed away from it, you know that too, and you can't define either." That's what a person is aimed at, not the care that aims. Appendix 1: "Everything doing anything, the sending, the hiding, the reading, the answering, is the light: one faculty, in two bodies, turned against itself in one of them." Now it's the faculty itself. Care, the direction care points, and awareness are three things, and the one word is carrying all of them.
- *"In view."* Chapter 1, The Way Out: "That's the only sense of "in view" I'll use, and it's the sense contempt was using all along". Your sense is not seeing the person's claim, not their presence. Then The Spread: "And I send it *about* people, to my allies, with nobody's face in view." And Chapter 3: "I can't find a hot atrocity that didn't need it. The cold ones begin with nobody in view at all." Both of those mean ordinary presence. I noticed it because you'd made the promise.
- *What the reader can claim about the mind.* The Introduction's "The mind is intrinsically compassionate," and Chapter 2's "So darkness is not an equal and opposite force. It's what remains when awareness turns away from its own nature," both sit against Appendix 1's "It does not get you that care is what awareness *is*." Chapter 2 does hedge first ("The simplest explanation I can find is that care belongs to awareness itself"), and then drops the hedge two paragraphs later.
- *One or not one:* Chapter 2's "Whether that makes them one thing is a question the traditions fight over, and the check doesn't need an answer," against the Appendix pair quoted above.

**4. ANSWERS TO QUESTIONS I DIDN'T HAVE**

- Chapter 1: "Out of view doesn't mean out of sight. A torturer has his victim in plain sight, and the cruelest watch the face closely." I hadn't asked, but it chilled me in a useful way; it helped. The next sentence, "That makes part of this a definition, and I'll own it," made me wonder who you were talking to. A philosopher, not me.
- Chapter 2: "I don't mean guilt over stepping on the sidewalk; the sidewalk's place is to be walked on." Nobody had mentioned sidewalks. Who is this for?
- Chapter 2: "If that's right, the knowledge is exactly common; if not, it's close enough for the trap to work." I hadn't formed any doubt yet for this to settle.
- Chapter 3: "In the Mahayana's terms it isn't "ego" in the everyday sense, a big personality." It's not for me, though I expect your sangha will want it.
- Chapter 3: "This is not the claim that the two sides are the same, or equally right, or equally dangerous." I half had this one. It helped.
- Chapter 3: "Real conspiracies also exist; people meet in rooms and plan harm and hide it, and those rooms have to be fought as what they are." That helped too. Without it I'd have worried.
- Chapter 4: "One exception: if you lived with someone who hurt you, you may have heard *he's better now* before, and distrusting it is sight, not this." I didn't need it for myself, but I thought of a friend, and it was right.
- Appendix 1's Gödel note, "since the comparison will occur to some readers." It didn't occur to me. In an appendix, that's fine.

**5. QUESTIONS I DID HAVE**

- *What about the one who is left?* This arose at "It's the same verdict, delivered by withdrawal," and again in the note for the hurt: "Staying turned toward someone never means staying near them." The whole note imagines the reader as the one who leaves. How do I stay turned toward a son who won't let me near? What does it look like from my end, when he's the one keeping the distance, and I can't tell if it's a verdict or a wound? The book never comes to it, and it's the question I came with.
- *What does repair look like?* Chapter 3: "It lives in how fast you catch the blindness, in what you repair when sight returns, and in what you've done to give the blindness less room next time." How do you apologize without slipping the extra in? (I can hear myself doing it: "I'm sorry *if* you were hurt.") Chapter 4 shows the extra in a rebuke. I wanted it shown in an apology.
- *Forgiveness.* I was waiting for it from the note for the hurt onward. It arrives only at the very end of the belief section of Appendix 1: "The only way back is forgiveness, his and mine, which turn out to be one act, since there was never anything to forgive but the turning." That's too late, and it's behind a door most readers won't open. Even one plain sentence about forgiveness in Chapter 4, as something you can do and not only believe in, would answer what a Christian reader will be asking all through.
- *Effort, or gift?* Chapter 4: "That can be trained, and it's the one part of this that effort improves." Elsewhere the care is "what's there when nothing is in the way," which sounds to me like grace. I'd have liked one sentence on how those fit. Partly answered; not quite.
- *What about a parent who did it to a child?* Chapter 1: "A child can't refuse it; he believes it, and carries it for years." I taught small children for thirty-one years, and I'm a mother. I read that and I asked myself whether I did it. The book never returns to the one who did it to someone small and now sees it.

**6. REDUNDANCY**

- The Far End warning, twice: "Skip it if someone has hurt you badly, if you're in a fragile place, or on a day when you're already being hard on yourself." And "here's an exercise, and not for a day when you're already being hard on yourself, or for anyone in a fragile place; if you have a teacher, do it with them." Keep the first. The exercise's opening sentence also retells the footnote ("I don't expect that to count as evidence for you, and it shouldn't"); one of them is enough.
- Common knowledge, said twice nearly word for word. Chapter 2: "If what's looking out of us has nothing to differ by, then to know this in yourself is already to know it in them, and to know that they know it in you, and so on." Appendix 1: "To know this in yourself is already to know it in them, and to know they know it in you, and so on." Keep Chapter 2's. Have the appendix point back to it.
- The airport. Chapter 2: "The hundred people around you in an airport are in the background; if one of them dropped a bag and started crying, they'd come forward at once, and you'd feel the pull." Appendix 1: "The hundred people at an airport aren't turned from; any of them would come forward the moment one dropped a bag and started crying." Keep Chapter 2's. The appendix can say it in four words.
- Contempt for the slow. Chapter 1: "Someone slow, ignorant, or clumsy can get the same verdict (*you're a lesser kind of thing*) and I write it off as "just being honest."" Chapter 3: "Little of my contempt is for the "bad people" at all. It goes to people I've decided are stupid, or crazy, or weak, or slow". Chapter 3's has the toddler and the grandparent, which is where it came alive for me. Keep Chapter 3's, or make Chapter 1's a half-line.
- Contempt for the unenlightened. Chapter 1: "The subtlest contempt I know is the one I feel toward people who haven't seen through this trap yet." Chapter 4: "There's a trap in this too, and it's the one I fall into most: contempt for the people who still have contempt." Keep Chapter 4's, which has the pity and the question about being glad. In Chapter 1, keep only "Sometimes it looks like me writing this book *at* people, instead of *for* them," which is good.
- Result, not cause. Chapter 1: "Badness is the result of turning away, not its cause." Chapter 3: "It can't hold, because evil is the *result* of turning away, not its cause." Keep Chapter 1's (and fix Chapter 3's word, as in section 3).
- Fierce and dark look alike. Chapter 1: "But fierce integrity and contempt can be made to look alike from the outside, so much so that when I look back later, even I won't be sure which one I did." Chapter 1 again: "Fighting evil is where my own is most likely to come out, because nothing is easier to mistake for fierce righteousness." Chapter 4: "From outside, fierce and dark look alike, and it counts on our not wanting to tell them apart." Chapter 4 adds the sending end, so it earns its place. The second in Chapter 1 could go.
- "Genuine goodness is a myth," three times in Chapter 3: "Proving to them that *genuine goodness is a myth.*"; the parasite's own voice, "Genuine goodness is a myth—a name that "good people" use to cover what they really want."; and "genuine goodness is a myth." at the end of the culture passage. The last is a deliberate callback and it works. The first one could go; the voiced version does it better.
- Not nice. Chapter 2: "This is not a call to be nice." Chapter 4: "Nice is worse than useless against evil. None of this is a case for doing nothing." Chapter 4: "The easiest way to misread all this is as nothing to do. Whatever else this book is, it isn't permission." By the third, I felt you were worried I'd go soft. I'd keep Chapter 2's and the "Nice is worse than useless" line, and cut the last.
- Home. Chapter 3: "We are all trying to find our way home, not realizing that this cannot be done at another's expense (by keeping them from it)." Appendix 1: "trying to get home by the one road that can't get there, keeping others from theirs." Since the second is in the belief section, it may be meant as the deeper telling. I'd keep both, but I noticed.
- "What's there when nothing is in the way" comes back in Chapter 2, Chapter 4 ("It's what's left when nothing is in the way") and Appendix 1. I took it as a refrain, and it worked for me as one. I wouldn't add a fourth.

**7.** Would I give it to someone I care about? Chapters 1, 2 and 4, yes, today. I already know who: a woman in my prayer group who hasn't spoken to her sister in eleven years. The whole book, not quite yet. Three things first:

1. Put the counterweight beside "delivered by withdrawal" in Chapter 1, and give the note for the hurt a few sentences for the one who was left. Without that, the book hands estranged parents like me a weapon, and I picked it up.
2. Move the institutions, culture and conspiracy pages out of Chapter 3 into "The Picture I Use," so the chapter ends where its checks end. Then the note for the hurt and the hostage aren't buried.
3. Make "the light" and "evil" each mean one thing, and let the Introduction say "I believe" where Appendix 1 admits the argument can't reach.

Thank you for the robbed question. I didn't want to find what I found, and I did.

With affection,
Rosa

## Greg (carpenter; twelve years in a Kagyu sangha; medium)

Steady.

Aditya, Chapter 1 I read in one sitting and Chapters 2 and 4 I read the way I read Thrangu Rinpoche: put it down, did the thing, picked it back up. My attention went slack in two places. I did the exercises, with real cases, so here's what I had in hand while I read.

The wrong: two summers ago I found a soft joist under a client's porch, sistered it instead of replacing it to save a day, and told them the frame was sound. I knew. The story was "nobody's going to see it, it'll outlast them." When I finally owned it to myself it wasn't news. I'd known the whole time. Could I have done it without the story? No. Remorse the other way round? I looked, and what I found was regret over what a decency had cost me, which is what yours turned out to be too. I'll come back to that.

The second stranger: Walt, my first boss. He tore out a stair stringer I'd cut wrong, in front of the whole crew, and said he was angry because I knew better. It stung worse than anything my father ever said and I never once dug in. Your second stranger is real. I've met him.
