# Before and after the restructure (October 20): ten blind reads

*The short book (start through Appendix 1) as it stood just before the October 20 restructure, and as it stood after it, each under a random code with TODO notes stripped. The same five readers as the October 17 benchmark, each read once per version by a fresh reader who saw one version only. The brief kept the five scores and asked six things: what the book is finally trying to get you to see; where it landed hardest and what you felt; where you were let off the hook; where you stopped being pulled forward; whether it changed how you see a particular person; a line per score. The feelings Aditya is after were not named to readers. Every book quote in the letters was checked verbatim against the reader's copy (all passed). The letters were then coded by a separate reader under neutral labels, with chapter numbers and titles redacted; the codes are in the session scratchpad. Letters verbatim, grouped by version.*

### retired schoolteacher, fifteen years of Mahamudra

SCORES {"recognition": 8.2, "moved": 6.7, "change": 6.3, "clarity": 4.4, "give": 5.2}

**1.**
Contempt is a turning away from something I already know: that the person in front of me is the same kind of being I am. The look only wounds because both of us know it, so it spreads the darkness it claims to fight, and the way back is catching the turn in my own eyes and staying turned toward people, fiercely when it takes that.

**2.**

> A person treated as stupid stops asking questions.

I saw a boy in my fourth-period class asking for the third time what a thesis statement was. I sighed and said, "Can someone help Marcus?" He didn't raise his hand again that year. I have known that for thirty years without once letting it be what it was. My face went hot. Then the excuse: thirty-one children, a Friday. Under the excuse, a plain sorrow with nowhere to go. And, I'll admit, a flicker of wanting to tell the author he has never taught seventh grade.

**3.**

> Is some part of you *itching* to press that first button?

No, I thought. I'd give the pill to the principal who eased me out, and felt good about that. The opening for friends who sit had already let me feel like one of those who know the view, so I read Chapter 1's angry man as somebody else. Chapter 5 took both back. When that principal retired with a cake and a plaque, I felt robbed, and this named it:

> if the harm simply stopped tomorrow, with nobody punished and nobody exposed, would you feel relief, or robbed?

I had pressed the pill in imagination and the other button in life.

**4.**
I kept reading through The Picture I Use, guard up. The Epilogue lost me here:

> Ideas that live in our collective shadow manifest "externally," taking shape in our physical reality.

Before that, "Source of creation" sat oddly with how I was taught, and my teacher would call a mushroom vision an experience to let pass, not a foundation. He warns us, but it costs the earlier chapters some quiet authority. The Appendix I read out of duty; it explains again a trap Chapter 1's encounter had already shown me.

**5.**
Glen, in our sangha, asks most weeks whether having thoughts means he's meditating wrong. For three years I have answered him from here:

> The beginner's question, heard with a patient smile.

The book's question, "would I be glad if they got there before I did?", got an honest answer: not entirely. I had been calling that seniority. It was the verdict in a softer coat. I can't go back to Marcus. I can answer Glen next Thursday as though it were a real question, which it is.

**6.**
- Recognition 8.2: the limbo recall worked (a grade I lowered for a girl whose mother had insulted me; the knowing was never new), and with the principal's face the story arrived before the face did.
- Moved 6.7: Marcus reached me; much of the rest reached my head first.
- Change 6.3: the relief-or-robbed question is one I'll use. Using it on Glen is harder.
- Clarity 4.4: Chapters 1 and 2 follow cleanly; Chapter 3's passage on the one choice took two readings, and the locker room, King's line, the factory farm and the license each come round twice. The Appendix is hard going.
- Give 5.2: Chapters 1, 4 and 5 I'd hand my sangha friends tomorrow; the whole thing, with the Devil, the conspiracy and the mushrooms, only with a note.
