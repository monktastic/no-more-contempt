# Round 12 audit: what the three older Chapter 1s have that `chapter-1-alt.md` doesn't

*Stateless. A sentence-by-sentence pass (September 29) of `long-book/chapter-1.md` (A), `general-book/chapter-1.md` (B) and the old `short-book/chapter-1.md` (C) against the alt as the round-12 readers saw it, plus `reader-lessons.md`. Every quote was checked against its file. Items the author deferred to later chapters are marked so. Ordered by importance.*

## Missing, and worth considering

1. **The reader on the receiving end.** The alt asks the reader for two memories (their own limbo, the good corrector); everything about contempt itself is the author's monologue. A 47: "Deny it, and you're the kind of person who does. Get angry, and that's proof too. Stay calm, and you don't even care. Apologize, and it's too convenient." A 55: "You'd take that bet at any odds. But on what evidence? You'd never met him." B 135: "When someone's contempt made you dig in, did it feel like an accident on his part?" C 152 (vouched): "What you're seeing in them is limbo, aimed at *you*."
2. **The turned-toward exercise.** The alt's "I *can't*, when I can see myself in them" is a report, not a test. C 3: "Imagine looking into a stranger's eyes long enough that their face stops being something you're looking at and becomes a place somebody is looking out of." B 165: "Now, from this place, try to wish them harm. For me it can't be done." B 169: "For me the story is there before the face is. That's the turning away, live." C 128: "Can you harm or use someone while staring your conscience straight in the face, with no justification at hand? Try it in imagination."
3. **The reader's own case as sender.** B 133: "did you feel surprised? Or did some part of you feel vindicated? Maybe even a little relieved?" A 353: "It felt like justice, a little sweeter than justice needs to be."
4. **The teacher (the second stranger as a real scene).** B 43: "He was searching my face for something, some sign that I knew, and that made me look for it in myself." B 47: "It doesn't always work." B 219: "The teacher gave me no hook, and nothing to fight. Contempt is built to give one."
5. **Contempt "works" short-term; fierceness works better without it.** The alt's monologue raises "It's still a net benefit to the world" and never answers it. B 253: "It's true that contemptuous behavior can stop people, sometimes. But everything I've been able to accomplish with it, I've been able to accomplish better without it."
6. **What makes looking possible, and full responsibility.** B 111: "What I need in order to look is what my enemy needed from me: to be seen as more than what I do. Not to let me off the hook, but to allow me to look." B 255: "I'm fully responsible for the harm I cause while in that state."
7. **The alibi as political readers hold it.** B 87: "What am I supposed to do—be *nice* to evil?" C 79: "the *good kind* of darkness: the kind all good people must harbor to keep *real* evil in check."
8. **What enemies are aligned on, and why contempt stays unspoken.** B 229: "We were fighting over which of us still stood on it… Said out loud, we'd both hear that we were standing on the same ground."
9. **Better nature as "myself," earned.** B 181: "Did it feel like losing something of yourself, or like getting something back?" B 197: the remorse check.
10. **Limbo in degrees, and invisible afterward.** A 33: "Would the big ones be easier to recall, or harder?" C 77: "It's true while I'm in it, and close to invisible when I'm not."
11. **Proven right feels like being right.** A 305: "The only difference is who produced the evidence, and that's the part he can't see." A 307: "It only had to be _evidence_."
12. **A definition of contempt.** A 185: "Malice is frightening. Contempt is malice that believes it's righteous, and that's what makes it **maddening**."
13. Lower: the loop has no stopping point (A 207–211); the trust check (B 235); "if it's something I *do*, then I can stop" (B 257); the smallest flicker (A 391); cheating as making yourself the exception (A 171, C 170); the atrocity footnote (B 167).
