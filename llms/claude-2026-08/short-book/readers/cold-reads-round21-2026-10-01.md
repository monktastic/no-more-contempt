21. The ending: "It's still there in you, quietly knowing what it knows, patiently waiting to be remembered." and "Just as it is in him." I said the chapter ended in the right place, and I still think so. But this ending is kinder to the enemy, and it closes the loop on the first claim. The chapter ends on me; this ends on both of us. I'm not sure which is better. I liked being left with him.

**What the chapter was right to leave out or change.**

Being told what I feel. The second file does it constantly. "Of course not." "Harder, almost certainly." "Can you feel that?" "Doesn't it feel like the person is somehow absent or "turned away" from themselves?" "It's hard to do, isn't it?" That last one is about shooting a stranger a dehumanizing gaze. No, Aditya, it isn't hard. I do it at depositions for a living. The chapter's cashier, kindness first and then cruelty, asked me a real question. This one answered for me and got the answer wrong.

Claims about everyone. "Because we **all** know it." "We all know this is how it works, on some level. Check if that's true for you." The check comes after the verdict, so it isn't a check. "We all know this is how it works. We have to, or else it wouldn't work." "**The only reason we ever turn away is to enable harm, and we cannot intend harm without turning away**." Bold, universal, and it says "check both directions" only after claiming the result for all of us. "Nobody consciously designed that trap. Everyone's caught in it." The chapter makes most of these claims about you, then offers them to me. That's why I could go with it.

"Every road funnels into the same conclusion." That's true of the first stranger. It's also true of a lot of the second file. By the time it reached "Of course not" and "Harder, almost certainly," every response I might have had was already filed. I felt what the stranger does to you, done by the author.

The theology. "It's the thing we call **evil**. Not as a judgement, but as the very essence of what "evil" *means*: to turn away from the life in front of us in order to pursue our own ends." Evil as absence, turning from the good, is a Christian doctrine. It's Augustine. I don't object to you holding it. I object to "the very essence of what "evil" *means*," which presents it as the meaning of the word. In my siddur God "forms light and creates darkness," and the Gemara has God say He created the yetzer hara and created Torah as its remedy. My tradition doesn't settle that evil is only an absence. The second file walked a Christian frame in as universal, and that's where it nearly lost me. The chapter's "Don't mistake the grammar for the claim" and its restraint about the agent were the right call. The second file has the same disclaimer, then writes "That is its one trick," "That's how it keeps itself alive," and "The dark part of us: it doesn't "want" us to know this." Sentences like that make the disclaimer look like a formality.

"And it's what this book exists to provide you." I bristled. It made the book about itself at the moment I was most open.

The taunt monologue, from "*We both know exactly what I'm doing to you*" through "Good luck." Too long and too operatic. The chapter's one line, "*Don't pretend you're doing something noble here. We both know what you're up to.*", does the job, and it's in the enemy's mouth rather than the narrator's.

"**fuck it**. Game on." I'm not delicate, but it was louder than it needed to be. The chapter's "why the hell shouldn't I?" was enough.

The Loop section. The run of "Which... Which... Which..." made the point, then kept making it. "Deep down, he knows this cannot work" is another claim about what someone knows that I can't check.

"**infection**," "**seducing**." Bolded, they read as a sermon. The chapter uses "infecting" once, quietly, and I flinched even then.

The chapter was also right to add things the second file doesn't have. The fair warning at the start, "If that doesn't match anything in your memory, the rest of this won't make sense". The cold version of contempt, which is how I actually treat Malka to her face. The worst enemy and the pill, which is Beruriah and was the most honest moment of my reading. "And hypocrisy only means something between two people who answer to the same thing." "Contempt and love are addressed to the same place." The villain-and-machine paragraph. "And I send it *about* people, to my allies," which is lashon hara, and which the second file never touches. And the author's own case. All of that is better in the chapter.

**Order and length.**

The second file is longer and says each thing more times. Even so, the comparison taught me something about order.

In the second file, the person who corrects you with dignity arrives early, before any theory, as the second stranger. When I thought of my father there, he was evidence. I found him before I knew what he was supposed to prove. In the chapter he arrives after the mechanism, as the alternative, almost a remedy. I still found him, and I still cried, but I'd already been told what he meant. The second file's order did what I'd want a book like this to do: I supplied the case, and the name came after. If anything in the chapter should move, it's the corrector. Bring him earlier, while I'm still remembering, not explaining.

The other order lesson goes the other way. The second file puts "When You Send It" last, after pages of what he does to you. By then I'd spent so long as the victim that turning the camera on me felt like a trap being sprung. The chapter has the author send it first, then me. That's why the turn didn't feel like an ambush. Keep the chapter's order for that.

On length, the second file confirmed my cuts in the chapter rather than undoing them. It has no Self-serving section, and I didn't miss it. It explains the mechanism (Tell, Why, How, Righteous Appearance, Loop, Agent, Taunt) at even greater length than the chapter, and that's where I skimmed most. What it has that the chapter needs is short: a dozen sentences, nearly all of them about what it's like on the receiving end, and about the small after-effects nobody records.

I'm still calling Rachel tomorrow. The second file only made me surer I shouldn't send her either one.
