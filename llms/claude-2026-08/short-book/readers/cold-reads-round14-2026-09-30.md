Aditya,

The second file is almost the same chapter, so I read it the way I'd read a revised paper: side by side, looking for what moved. Not much did, but nearly every change mattered to me, and most of them went the first version's way.

I'd have refused that on the spot. It's a claim about everyone, and my first thought was that it's false for me. I hide decency all the time. **The better-nature evidence.** Same problem, later on. The second file asks: "If the darkness in me were me too, why wouldn't I have to hide my good behavior from it?" As a rhetorical question that only works if the answer is "I don't," and I do. The chapter already knows this and says so: "I've hidden good things too, from the part of me that would call them weakness. But only one of those hidings has ever brought me remorse." That is a man admitting the counterexample before I raise it, then drawing a finer distinction. It's the more honest sentence and the stronger argument. In my first letter I complained that the chapter uses the remorse point twice. I still think that. But the second file's fix, trading the repeat for a rhetorical question I can answer the wrong way, is worse than the repeat.

**The "in short" line.** The second file adds a parenthesis: "showing him that I *am* in limbo (which is something I *do* from limbo) is itself the provocation." That was the one sentence in that section I could hold in my head, and the parenthesis adds another level of recursion to it. The chapter was right to leave it out.

**Leaking.** The chapter has: "Not only can I not fool myself, but I can't fool him, either. The self-deception leaks out my eyes, and the harder I hide it, the more clearly he sees it." The second file stretches that into a loop ending in "etc., ultimately empowering it in me." The chapter's version is tighter, and "etc." in a sentence about feedback always makes me suspect the author hasn't worked out where it ends. The chapter was right.

**Hypocrisy.** The second file has no hypocrisy paragraph in Common knowledge. That's the paragraph in the chapter that ends "And hypocrisy only means something between two people who answer to the same thing." It was the one place in the section where I found the claim in myself instead of granting it. Reading the second file, I felt the section go straight from the exercise to "We could not treat our enemies with contempt..." without anything under it. The chapter needs that paragraph. If anything, it should come earlier.

**"Each knows."** The second file says of the private equity firm and the politician: "Each would sincerely say they're doing good, and each knows." The chapter says "and I suspect each knows." Two words, and they are the difference between a claim I can grant as yours and one I'd refuse. My ex-colleagues in finance would stop reading at the second file's version. Keep the chapter's.

**The flatness paragraph.** The second file has one paragraph more in "Other kinds of contempt": "The subtlest form of all is aimed at everything: a flatness toward the world itself, where trees and animals and nature itself are just so much inert stuff. Calling it \"contempt\" sounds ridiculous, but it has the same essence, and originates from the same place." I'll be fair to it. I felt something flicker. I work all day with people as rows in a dataset, and I know that flatness. But "it has the same essence, and originates from the same place" is two large claims with nothing under them. It arrives right after the passage about the people I love, which was the most personal page I read. It pulled me back out. The chapter was right to leave it out. If it belongs anywhere, it belongs somewhere that can earn it.

**What the second file has that the chapter should recover.** It's the coda. The chapter ends on "This is why, if I want to end theirs, I have to see how to end mine," which is fine. The second file goes on:

"I often tell myself that I'm getting away with stuff: that I get to freely choose what's right; that I can simply override my conscience. But here's what I think now: the self-deception implicit in that choice always finds its way into the world, one way or another, darkening it in a way that I will disown. I then judge it from afar, allowing me to get away with more."

Two things in there matter. First, "I often tell myself that I'm getting away with stuff" is the plainest confession in either version. It's you admitting your own mess in ordinary words, and for me that's what opens a door. "But here's what I think now" marks what follows as yours. Second, "darkening it in a way that I will disown. I then judge it from afar" named something I hadn't put together from the chapter. My coldness toward Carla goes out into the family. It comes back as Daniel's distance and Carla's coldness toward me. Then I judge those from across the room at a wedding, as though I had nothing to do with them. The chapter's "Self-serving" section gets near this with nursing homes. The coda says it about me, in the first person, and I recognized it.

I'd leave out the last two sentences. "Part of me already knows that this is how it works" tells me what I know. "All that's left is to become fully aware of it, so that I can make a different choice" makes the hardest thing in the book sound like a last errand. Becoming fully aware isn't what's left. It's all of it. And "always finds its way into the world" should be "often" or should be marked, like the rest of that paragraph.

Still, the coda gets one thing right that the chapter lacks: it ends on a choice. My first letter said I'd be sitting across from Carla on the 18th without knowing what I'd do differently. The chapter left me there. The second file at least said there's a choice to be made, even if it made the choice sound easy.

**Order and length.** Putting them side by side showed me where the chapter really ends. In both, everything after "Whether that number ever goes to zero is something you can discover for yourself" is a tail. The first tail, the people we love, is the strongest page in either version. The rest (self-serving, flatness, and in the second file the coda) gets weaker the further it gets from a face. The second file is longer, and all of its extra length comes in the two places I already thought were running long. That tells me the pull is toward adding at the end, and it should go the other way. If I were building it, I'd take the chapter as it stands, cut "Self-serving" down to the villain-and-machine paragraph, and end with the coda's first three sentences.
