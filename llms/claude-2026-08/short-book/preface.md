# The Short Version

*The book, condensed, for people who practice.*

> *For so long as the root of wickedness is hidden, it is strong. But when it is recognized, it is dissolved. When it is revealed, it perishes. ... It is powerful because we have not recognized it.* —The Gospel of Philip

This work is my attempt to convey an insight about the nature of good and evil that arose from my practice (as well as plant medicines) many years ago. It is not one that I can claim to be fully living myself.

What I saw was how I, specifically, was contributing to the horrors of the world—even while being flawlessly confident that I was one of the "good guys," valiantly fighting evil. *Because* I was convinced of that. At the same time, I saw how an aspect of my mind had always known exactly how I was lying to myself. It was always there, just patiently waiting to be remembered.

---

This version is for friends who sit, and it assumes things a general reader might not grant: that the mind has a nature, that the confusion covering it is not the same thing as it, and that recognition is available on the spot rather than at the end of an argument. The full book earns each of those slowly, from the reader's own memory; here I lean on them, which is what lets me be brief. It's meant to be read alongside practice, from a settled seat; Chapter 1 especially, since it still asks before it tells. If it provokes the recognition, the rest is detail.

For those of us with bodhicitta aspirations, it matters to see the mechanism by which straying from recognition creates samsara. This is one way to see part of it. Dzogchen and Mahamudra already say what the darkness is: awareness failing to recognize its own nature, with no substance of its own. What I think I can add is how that not-recognizing passes between people: through gaze and tone, from one mind into another, into groups, with everyone in it convinced they were fighting it. Like any text, it isn't enough on its own.

This work is *far* from finished. Part of my hope for this version is to find practitioners with deeper insight and better communication skills than mine, who wish to collaborate. If that's you, let me know.

A warning. Parts of this are hard to read, because they ask you to look at things in yourself that nobody enjoys looking at. Go slowly, and stop when you need to. Nothing here is a verdict on you.

May all beings be free of suffering and its causes.
