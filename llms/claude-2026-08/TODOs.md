## Ch 1

On evoking the parasite:

**Location: one block, immediately after "That leaves... what? His tone; his gaze; his mannerisms. Exactly the things you used to detect it."** Then resume with "And think back on exactly what it was that made you dig in."

Six beats, in this order:

**1. The asymmetry.** Try to look evil — you can't, you know you're faking. Now bring someone to mind and give them the look. Instantly, and you didn't have to decide how. _Why here: proves the channel from the sending end, at the exact moment you've established that channel is all he had left._

**2. It's aimed.** You couldn't do it into empty air. You had to pick someone. _Free, one line, and it makes the look an address rather than a mood._

**3. It has content you can't write down.** Something got said. You know exactly what. You couldn't put it in a transcript. _Sets up "beneath the words" later in the chapter, and now that passage is a callback instead of a claim._

**4. The thrill.** The imagined confrontation you've rehearsed — where they escalate and you demolish them. Notice the script requires their escalation. It never opens with them apologizing. _Keep it in the fantasy register. The reader admits it because it costs nothing; move it to the real encounter and they close._

**5. Manufacture, not exposure.** If he'd come back worse — was that him revealing himself, or you making him? _Closes the "just drawing it out" retreat using an inference already granted in the Cause section. Without this, Chapter 3's alliance claim has no floor._

**6. The braced-for return.** Not shame, not an apology — the same look, returned. **Where did you learn this?** _Ends on an open question. Plants alliance without asserting it, and pulls the reader forward._

**One further Chapter 1 change:** after the taunt, "That's what our contempt _is_" now has a demonstration behind it rather than being an assertion. Consider trimming it — the glare did that work already.

## Chapter 3

**Deferred, in dependency order:**

**The alliance.** _Where did you learn this?_ answered: you were braced for the same look back because you know they speak it. Both darknesses want the same outcome. Requires beat 5 to have landed — manufacture, not exposure — or this reads as paranoia.

**"It's using me as a vehicle."** The horror of looking is what prevents the looking, which is what it needs. Requires the trip's exoneration first, or it's shame with a cosmology attached.

**The pill test.** Reform vs. eternal punishment. Only after the exoneration is in hand _and_ can deepen alongside it. This is the hardest single admission in the project.

**The evil scene.** Real evil, the visceral pull of punishing it. Chapter 1's counterfeit passage is the priming; this is the payoff.

**The demoralizing message.** _Even the good people are frauds; the Light is a myth._ The second half of the taunt, and the bridge to the civilizational argument.

**The unspoken social contract.** The bystanders were in limbo too. Extends common knowledge from two people to the room.

## The line

Chapter 1 asks the reader to notice what they can do and what they enjoy. It never asks them to conclude anything about what they _are_. Every deferred item does — and none of them is survivable before the trip supplies _the hiding is evidence that you don't want to_.

That's the test for anything else you're weighing: does it require the exoneration? If yes, it's Chapter 3.
