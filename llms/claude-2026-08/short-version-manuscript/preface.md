# The Short Version

*The book, in a few pages, for people who practice.*

> *For so long as the root of wickedness is hidden, it is strong. But when it is recognized, it is dissolved. When it is revealed, it perishes. ... It is powerful because we have not recognized it.* —The Gospel of Philip

This work is my attempt to convey an insight about the nature of good and evil that arose from my practice (as well as plant medicines) many years ago. Alas, it is not one that I can claim to be fully living myself, by any stretch.

What I saw was how I, specifically, was contributing to the horrors of the world—even while being flawlessly confident that I was one of the "good guys," valiantly fighting evil. *Because* I was convinced of that. At the same time, I saw how an aspect of my mind had always known exactly how I was lying to myself. It was always there, just patiently waiting to be remembered.

---

This version is for friends who sit, and it assumes things a general reader might not grant: that the mind has a nature, that the confusion covering it is not the same thing as it, and that recognition is available on the spot rather than at the end of an argument. The full book earns each of those slowly, from the reader's own memory, and never asks for them up front. Here I lean on them, which is what lets me be brief. It also assumes the reader has done enough "shadow work" to look at deeply uncomfortable truths about themselves without flinching.

So this leaves out a great deal. The finer distinctions, the objections and the cases where the account could fail, the evidence for the claims about people you'll never meet: all of that belongs in the full book and isn't here. What's here is only the broad shape, quickly, and one or two places to look. And it works by telling you what the full book makes you find for yourself, in your own memory, before it's named; so don't take this as how the full book is written. It's what the book is about, said to someone who can hear it said. If it provokes the recognition, the rest is detail. If it doesn't, the full book is where to go next (though only chapter one feels finished for now).

For those of us with bodhicitta aspirations, it matters to see the mechanism by which straying from recognition creates samsara. This is one way to see it. Like any text, it isn't enough on its own; it's meant to be used alongside practice.

This work is *far* from finished. Part of my hope for this version is to find practitioners with deeper insight and better communication skills than mine, who wish to collaborate. If it transmits what I hope it does, finding them should be easy.

May all beings be free of suffering and its causes. 🙏🏽
