# Introduction

The nature of the mind is compassion. Evil is nothing but a mind turned away from itself. A mind turned toward itself knows these truths innately. So when we're *not* seeing them clearly—such as when we perceive our enemies as *less* or *other* than ourselves—we are committing **evil**.

This is not an attack on you. I only know it because I'm still caught in it myself. And it isn't an abstract idea. It has profound, real-world consequences; you already know them; and *believing that you don't* is what enables "evil" to keep operating through your blind spots.

This is an attempt to put a new lens on something ancient, so that we have one more tool by which to see it clearly. With that in mind, feel free to read what I say as a kind of poetry.

The darkness in each of our minds doesn't only operate *through* us; it collaborates *across* us. The corrupt structures we see around us are merely its outward manifestations. Attempting to destroy them without fixing the error in ourselves can only recreate them, because it is rooted in the same fundamental mistake: that they are essentially *other*.

This is the origin of the archetype of the "evil global conspiracy," as well as the Devil. The collaboration of darkness across our minds is, by its very nature, hidden from the part of us that could see it—and yet we can never stop sensing it, which is why we retain the suspicion that there is something there, operating behind the scenes. There is. Awakening to one's true nature can therefore be seen as finally stepping outside the conspiracy—the only vantage point from which one can actually take it down, rather than blindly participate in it.

Our attempts to define *goodness* are similarly folly. No matter how carefully we try to define it, the *dark* aspect of our mind finds clever ways to subvert it: to obey the letter of the law while violating its spirit. We recognize this error in others easily. In ourselves we can't, because following the definition has been made to feel exactly like being turned toward our true nature. Thus, the folly of trying to pin down *goodness* is not an accident. Getting us to believe our thoughts and definitions—as though it could substitute for staying *turned toward*—is precisely what the darkness *is*.

As civilization moves toward ever-greater abstraction, we feel a corresponding pull to abstract away our own nature, until "authentic goodness" can be filed away as superstition. Thus the pull toward pure abstraction can itself be seen as an operation of the darkness.

The main engine of this book is a familiar thing: *moral contempt*, the way we treat our enemies as though they were *inherently defective.* Traced carefully, it turns out to be the purest expression of evil: how the darkness spreads between us while masquerading as *righteousness*.

Now for the strange part, which this book exists to show. Contempt for our enemies is only coherent if both sender and receiver already know the things this introduction opened with: that the nature of mind is compassion, that darkness is nothing but being *turned away*, and—strangest of all—that these are both already *common knowledge*[^ck]. Contempt says the opposite, but it can only operate by denying exactly what it presupposes.

[^ck]: To my analytical readers: yes, common knowledge in the game-theoretic sense—but with a wrinkle, as we'll see.

I realize these are all very lofty ideas. Let me now pull them into the realm of our everyday experience.
