We've heard these ideas before (and maybe even have some personal familiarity with them), but what relevance do they have to our actual lives?

A mind turned toward itself knows these truths innately. So when we're *not* seeing them clearly—such as when we perceive our enemies as *less* or *other* than ourselves—our mind is turned away from itself, which is all that **evil** ever was.

This is not an attack on you. I only know it because I'm still caught in it myself. And it isn't an abstract idea. It has profound, real-world consequences. I want to show you that you already know them, and how *believing that you don't* is what enables "evil" to keep operating through your blind spots.

What's new here is only the lens: an attempt to see something ancient more clearly, so that we have one more tool for it. With that in mind, feel free to read what I say as a kind of poetry.

One piece of this is stranger than the rest. The darkness in our minds doesn't only operate *through* us. It collaborates *across* us, and the corrupt structures we see around us are its outward form. This is the origin of the archetype of the "evil global conspiracy," or what might also be called the Devil. That claim comes last, in Chapter 3, after you'll have watched it happen between two people.

Our attempts to define *goodness* are folly. No matter how carefully we try to define it, the dark aspect of our mind finds clever ways to subvert it: to obey the letter of the law while violating its spirit. We recognize this error in others easily, but in ourselves we can't, because following definitions has been made to feel exactly like being turned toward our true nature. Thus, the folly of trying to pin down *goodness* is not an accident. Getting us to believe our thoughts and definitions—as though they could substitute for staying *turned toward*—is precisely what the darkness *is*.

The main engine of this book is one familiar thing: *moral contempt*, the way we treat our enemies as though they were *inherently defective.* Traced carefully, it turns out to be the purest expression of evil: how the darkness spreads between us while masquerading as *righteousness*.

Now for the strange part, which this book exists to show. Contempt for our enemies is only coherent if both sender and receiver already know the things this introduction opened with: that the nature of mind is compassion, that darkness is nothing but being *turned away*, and—strangest of all—that these are both *common knowledge*[^ck]. Contempt claims the opposite, but it can only operate by denying exactly what it presupposes.

[^ck]: For my analytical readers: yes, common knowledge in the game-theoretic sense—the implicit kind, which is the wrinkle. Appendix 1 has the details.
