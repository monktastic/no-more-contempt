## Part I: Turning Away

Have you ever done something you knew was wrong at the time, but couldn't fully admit to yourself until later? Maybe you made a cruel comment to boost your ego, took credit for someone else's work, or cheated on a test (or even partner).

Try and remember what that was like. You knew it was wrong, even as you did it. The knowledge wasn't unconscious—but it wasn't fully conscious either, was it? It lived in a strange kind of limbo, where you simultaneously knew and yet didn't.

The part that *knew*: it couldn't tell you exactly how it knew, could it? So you listened to the part that could—the part that "proved" everything was fine.

I mean, what could be wrong with trusting something you can *prove?*

Trick question. I don't do it *despite* knowing this—I do it because of it. Watch what happens.

## Part II: The Replacement

Turning away from myself; from my humanity; from *your* humanity; from the full consequences of my behavior—it's **all a single move**.

I call it "myself" because it is the **deepest part of me**. A part that doesn't want to cause harm. That wouldn't *let* me.

But in a cruel twist, it is also the thing that warns me that I *am* turned away. Which means that when I do turn away, you see it even though I don't. *Because* I don't.

And you perceive this as **evil**—something I *must* fully see, making you feel morally compelled to punish me for it.

The hiding: it **is** the revealing.

---

But it's not merely that I *don't know* I'm turned away. I feel more certain than ever that I'm **not**. Like I'm seeing more clearly than I ever have. Isn't that true?

The further I drift, the more certain I become—and the less I remember that a deeper part of me even exists. Which makes *you* increasingly certain that it doesn't.

People committing atrocities: they don't feel "evil." They feel *impossibly righteous.* Just as I do when violently punishing them for it.

The darkness: it doesn't commandeer my mind by coming out and announcing itself as such. It does so by masquerading *as* the light—by slowly replacing my true North.

---

The "righteous" pleasure of violent punishment and the "righteous" feeling of acting from our deepest integrity: when tasted directly, they're not the same at all, are they? All they share is the *label*.

So what better way to disorient us than to make us focus on labels; on what we can explicitly name?

The part that *knows* I'm creating a darker world cannot prove it, while the part that's darkening it can prove that I'm not. 

So which part benefits by getting me to focus only on what I can *prove?*

The more I do this, the more maddeningly obvious it becomes that it's the *right thing to do*. The only *sane* thing to do.

Who could argue with something you can literally *prove?* Harming bad people is *good!*

---

Watch how it achieves this disorientation.

When I treat you with contempt and call it "good," I'm doing more than just revealing my inner darkness. I'm *taunting* you. Because you can't prove I'm doing it, can you? In fact, you already see how I'm hiding it from myself.

And I know what this will do to you: rob you of faith in goodness. And you can see that some part of me knows that, too, can't you? But you can't prove that either.

And I *know* you can't—because neither can I. That's what makes the whole thing cohere.

You're trapped inside a hall of mirrors: deafeningly obvious from the inside, but invisible from the outside. Unmistakable and yet unprovable.

And how does that make you *feel?* When a so-called "good person" does this to you?

It scrambles your definition of *goodness itself.* Because if this is just how the world is, then *fuck it. **Game on**.*

"Good" begins to mean what we can get away with; what we can prove. The self-serving aspects of your actions and agendas become easier to justify; harder to recognize as such. They feel increasingly necessary. *Righteous*. 

And that persists long beyond this one interaction.

---

When I see this shift in you, what do I feel?

Damn. This world is even shittier than I thought. I need to protect myself—even if I have to bend the rules to do it.

Which means the same cynicism I spread to you takes deeper root in me. I, too, will increasingly overlook the selfish aspects of my behavior. They won't feel "selfish" at all. And I won't feel like I'm fooling myself. In fact, I can literally *see*; *prove* that I'm not.

We all like to believe we're uniquely immune to evil. That other people might slide down that slippery slope, but *we'd* see it coming from a mile away—because we're *made of better stuff*.

And how do I treat you when I believe that? Even if I try maintain a facade of dignity around you, what can you *feel* coming through me?

---

I turned you into what I needed you to be, but where's the evidence that I wanted any such thing? There isn't any. Which is precisely how I got it.

There's nothing you can point at and say "there it is. There's the darkness" And yet it transmitted all the same.

Almost like an *infection*, taking place beneath our conscious thresholds.

It doesn't work *despite* being unprovable. It works *because* of it.

The darkness doesn't have to defeat the light. It just has to rob it of all meaning.

All there is is what we can calculate; what we can *prove*.

**I** am the ultimate arbiter of right and wrong. Not that silly voice deep within.

## Interlude: The Reminder

Even if it stung your ego; made you lose face, did you feel compelled to take revenge?

Maybe you were even enemies on the surface. Yet something felt deeply *aligned*, didn't it? It was clean, like there was nothing to hide.

What *is* that?

It's like they were reminding you of something about yourself—something you'd temporarily forgotten. Not by saying it, but by *being* it themselves.

Whatever it was, you knew you couldn't win against it—and more importantly, you didn't want to.

What were they reminding you of?

What does *contempt* try to make you forget?

Which presents an opportunity, doesn't it?

If I wanted to create a world aligned with my own desires—where that was the only "reasonable" choice—but I couldn't make it obvious what I was doing, even to myself...

## Part III: The Machine
