## Part I: Turning Away

Have you ever done something you knew was wrong at the time, but couldn't fully admit to yourself until later? Maybe you made a cruel comment to boost your ego, took credit for someone else's work, or cheated on a test (or even partner).

Try and remember what that was like. You knew it was wrong, even as you did it. The knowledge wasn't unconscious—but it wasn't fully conscious either, was it? It lived in a strange kind of limbo, in which you simultaneously knew and yet didn't.

The part that knew it was wrong: it couldn't tell you exactly *how* it knew, could it? So you trusted the part that could—the part that "proved" everything was fine.

I mean, what could be wrong with that?

---

Deep down, I know that what lives in the "bad people" lives in me. But this is too horrible to admit, so what do I do? I view them as fundamentally _other_ and treat them with contempt.

At some level, I know this "othering" is what enables all harm. Could the executive ravage the rainforest if he felt the life he was destroying? Could normal people commit atrocities if they recognized themselves in their victims? No. To cause harm, we must shield ourselves from the life in others.

So by dehumanizing them, I'm doing to them exactly what I condemn them for. But there's a difference, see, because they actually deserve it. Plus, maybe if they're forced to feel what it's like to be on the receiving end, they'll stop.

But does this ever actually work, or does it only make them feel more justified? I already know the answer, and yet I can't help but do it anyway. Why?

Trick question. I don't do it _despite_ knowing this—I do it because of it. Watch what happens.

One part of me feels completely righteous. I mean, if _this_ makes them worse, what does that say about them? What kind of person digs in their heels in response to righteousness? I'm only revealing their true colors—a genuine service to the world!

But something uncomfortable gnaws at me.

How do _I_ react to contempt—even when I know I'm in the wrong? Don't I dig in, too? And not because I'm a bad person. I can tell the other person isn't serving what they claim to serve. Their words say one thing, but their tone and gaze convey something _much darker_.

It's like they're baiting me—wanting me to react poorly so they get to look righteous. But if I accuse them of this, I'll just look crazy or paranoid, falling exactly into their trap. Seriously, _fuck_ those people. They know exactly what they're doing.

But _shit_—might that be what I'm doing right now?

No, of course not. Because my enemy is *actually* bad. It's not "baiting" if you're just exposing them for what they already are. It's *helping.*

And yet they seem to be accusing me of it anyway! Which means they can only be **projecting**. See? I was right: they really are beyond help. I mean, I gave them every chance, and _this_ is what they chose, of their own free will.

There's only _one way_ to deal with people like that...

---

There is **nothing** more primally, viscerally satisfying than violently punishing evil. Pause and feel that.

...

And my enemy is now *one step closer* to being the kind of person I'll be "forced" to deal with in that way. Any compunction they may have felt about their own self-serving behavior is now out the window.

Do you think that's a coincidence? That I'm producing exactly the conditions a dark part of me craves?

Try this. The very worst people—the ones knowingly destroying the world—if I had a choice between delivering them the righteous vengeance they so richly deserve, or offering them a pill that would magically enlighten them: which would I choose? 

Isn't there some part of me that would **gladly** mash that first button? Do I really think that part of me is private?

If they somehow saw it... what would it make them feel? What would it **license** in them?

---

The truth is, something in me **needs** my enemies to be utterly beyond help. Because if they're not—if they had any hope of redemption—then *what the hell have I been doing?* 

I've been shoving **fundamentally redeemable** people deeper into the abyss.

And part of me already suspects it's true. I mean, why *else* would they have to shield themselves to cause harm—unless *a deep part of them wasn't okay with doing it?* 

Which would mean I've been smothering that part of them just to prove it doesn't exist. To get that delicious hit of self-righteous satisfaction—of knowing *I'm the good guy.*

And all the additional harm they went on to cause as a result, and its countless downstream effects: would they not also be partially on me?

Which would mean I'm not truly separate from "what's wrong with the world." I am **part of it**—and something in me has always known this but chose to do it anyway, to cover up the whole thing.

*And that just... can't... fucking... be.*

So no: some people really are just fundamentally different. And I'm going to *prove* it.

---

What do you do when you **need** something to be true, but can't be responsible for causing it? When the mere knowledge that you *are* would undermine the very thing you're trying to prove?

You **do it without knowing it**. By **turning away** from a deep part of yourself.

I tell myself this is a private act—that if even *I* don't know I'm doing it, then others won't either. After all, they're not mind-readers.

But have you ever been on the receiving end of a dehumanizing gaze? Doesn't it feel like the person is somehow absent or "turned away"?

**That's** what you're looking at. What "shielding" looks like from the outside. They know they're in this state—and also, they don't.

It looks *dark*, doesn't it? So why do we think it looks any different when *we* do it?

---

We don't.

Deep down, I *know* there's a way to fight evil that doesn't make my enemies dig in; that doesn't make them see me as *evil.* But a dark part of me knows I can "speed things along" by making them see me that way. So it **telegraphs** itself to them while hiding itself from me. *By* hiding itself from me.

"I" cannot know I'm doing this, because I'd recognize it as evil—the very thing I hate. And the self-consciousness would render the ruse too obvious, and they'd fail to take the bait. And even if they did, the recognition of my own complicity would defeat the whole purpose: to know that I'm the good guy.

But I also can't simply not-know, because then how could I even do it? It's a sophisticated ruse. I must adjust the details to counter their every reaction. If it were unconscious, I'd be confused or apologetic when they accused me of it—but I'm not, am I? In some sense, I'm **behind the wheel**.

I must know and not-know **at the same time**. That's the only way it *can* work.

And there's nothing additional I have to do. No separate *thing* I have to know and not-know, apart from the fact that I am in the state of knowing and not-knowing.

Simply by being in it, I know and don't know that I *am*. I know and don't know that you can *see* it. What it will do to you. How I can use it against you. Etc.

That whole stack is known and not-known **at the same time**.

The limbo state: it's not an accident. It is how *fundamentally decent* people commit evil.

---

But is it really "me" doing this? Or is some dark part of me *using* me for its own benefit?

Both answers are defensible.

On one hand, it is **absolutely me**. If I'm really, deeply honest—and this is really fucking hard—I see that I have indeed been **smothering innocence** to cover up my own guilt. That's what contempt *is*, full stop. And part of me has always known this.

On the other hand, if I had really, truly, seen what I was doing—been unable to shield myself—I simply couldn't have done it. That's not an excuse. It's the truth.

Either way, I am fully responsible for the consequences. But which part is the "real me"? One choice leaves the door open for grace and rehabilitation. The other condemns me to the abyss.

Something in me desperately wants to answer one way for myself, and another way for my enemies.

Which part of me do you think that is?

## Part II: The Replacement

Turning away from myself; from my humanity; from *your* humanity; from the full consequences of my behavior—it's **all a single move**.

I use the word "myself" because it is the **deepest part of me**. A part that doesn't want to cause harm; that wouldn't *let* me. In my heart of hearts, I only want to do **right** by the world.

But, in a cruel twist, it's also the thing that warns me when I *am* turned away. Which means that when I do turn away, you see it even though I don't. *Because* I don't.

You perceive this as **evil**—something I *must* fully see—making you feel morally compelled to punish me for it.

The hiding: it **is** the revealing.

---

But it's not merely that I *don't know* I'm turned away. I feel more certain than ever that I'm **not**. Like I'm seeing more clearly than I ever have. Isn't that true?

It's almost as if **something else** has taken control. And the more I go along with it, the more it feels like **me**—and the less I remember that a deeper part even exists. Which makes other people increasingly certain that it doesn't.

People committing atrocities: they don't feel "evil." They feel *impossibly righteous.* Just as I do when violently punishing them for it.

The darkness: it doesn't have to defeat the light. It just has to masquerade *as* it.

---

The "righteous" pleasure of violent punishment and the "righteous" feeling of acting from integrity: they don't feel the same at all, do they? All they share is the **label**.

So what better way to disorient us than to make us focus exclusively on labels—on what we can *name?*

The part that **knows** my behavior is darkening the world cannot prove it, while the part that's darkening it can "prove" that I'm not.

So why not get me to focus on what I can *prove* instead of what I **know**?

The more I do this, the more obvious it becomes that there's no difference between the two. How could you *know* something without rational justification? How could you distrust something when you *have* it?

If harming "bad people" makes them stop, then by definition it can't be wrong.

---

Watch how I transmit this confusion to you.

When I treat you with contempt, I *know* I'm disobeying my conscience while also not-knowing it—because a very convincing voice is narrating how the urge to righteously punish you *is* my conscience.

I know it's a lie and I don't. I know you can *see* this and I don't. I know what it will *do* to you, and how I can use it *against* you. And I don't.

And how does this make you feel?

Like **genuine goodness is a myth**. All that matters is what we can get away with; what we can *prove.* How else can "good" even be defined? 

And if that's just the way the world works, you'd be a fool to listen to that part of yourself screaming otherwise. It's just one more voice amongst the noise—and not a very useful one.

I have perverted your very sense of what is good; further separated you from that part of yourself that **knows.** This is **profoundly malicious**—and yet where, exactly, is this "malice"? What can you point at and say "there it is"? 

*And that is exactly the point.*

It lives nowhere but in the **structure of the trap itself**. In the combination of knowing and not-knowing. Which is why there's nothing anyone could blame me for—myself included. Which I already knew. Which is what you saw. Which is why it worked.

This worldview, that knowing requires proof: it spreads through what we know but cannot prove.

---

Following this encounter, the self-serving aspects of your actions and agendas will become easier to justify; harder to recognize as such. They feel increasingly necessary. *Righteous*, even.

And the same thing will happen to me.

Because as far as I know, I did my best—and look what happened. People are even shittier than I thought.

And we will both go on to spread this corrupted worldview to others.

---

It's like something is being transmitted between us—but are *we* the ones transmitting it?

Because I **do not want** a darker world. **Could** not.

And yet it is happening all the time. Everywhere. Just turn on the news, or open social media.

Every contemptuous interaction: a secret handshake beneath our conscious thresholds.

But between *what*, exactly?

---

Until I recognize what this is that is moving through me, I will continue to be its puppet—even as I'm flawlessly convinced I am *fighting* it.

## Interlude: The Reminder

Recall a time when someone corrected you with integrity, without sneaking in contempt. Wasn't there something *different* about it?

Even if it stung your ego; made you lose face, did you feel compelled to take revenge?

You could tell they were serving something different—something beyond themselves. And it's not because they were being nice, either. Perhaps they had to be fierce and direct.

Maybe you were even enemies on the surface. Yet something felt deeply *aligned*, didn't it? It was clean, like there was nothing to hide.

What *is* that?

It's like they were reminding you of something about yourself—something you'd temporarily forgotten. Not by saying it, but by *being* it.

Whatever it was, you knew you couldn't win against it—and more importantly, you didn't want to.

What were they reminding you of?

What does *contempt* make you forget?

---

Think how similar those two forms of correction can be made to look from the outside.

Both can shut you up. Both can suppress your misbehavior—at least, your *visible* misbehavior, in the short term.

But what will you be like behind closed doors? And how *drastically different* will the long-term effects be—both for you and for those you interact with?

All this is invisible, and yet it operates all the same. And something in me knows it—


but also knows that it will never be caught, given our culture's obsession with the *provable.*

And every time that worldview is strengthened, this part of me has more leeway to operate. Has a bigger space in which to hide. And so does yours. Doesn't matter if we're "allies" or "enemies."

Can't you sense it? The *falseness* behind all our institutions? Our governments? Corporations? *Everything?*

This part of me, and the same part in those I hate—are they really fighting each other? Or are they just *pretending* to?

---

Which presents an opportunity, doesn't it?

If I wanted to create a world aligned with my own desires—where that was the only "reasonable" choice—but I couldn't make it obvious what I was doing, even to myself...

Then what better way to accomplish it?
